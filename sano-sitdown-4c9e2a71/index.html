Lunch with Nisa

Thu 11/12 · in person · second meeting · today decides it

Leave lunch knowing these 5 things
  1. She can sell an owner face to face (you'll see it, not hear about it).
  2. Her history and numbers hold up when you ask for specifics.
  3. She works without someone checking on her: logs, follows up, keeps going on a bad week.
  4. A recent manager you can call.
  5. She still wants it on these terms, with a start date that works.
Before you sit down
  • Ask, then stop. Count to three. She talks more than you.
  • Skip anything she already answered well on the phone.
  • Don't know? "I don't know yet. I'll text you today." Write it down.
  • No signing today. The offer comes in writing.

1 · Open · 2 min

"Thanks for coming out. Today's the last step, for both of us. I want to see you sell, hear the parts we skipped on the phone, and answer anything about the offer. Then we both decide."

6 · Be straight with her · 5 min

8 · Decide, then close

It's a yes if: her role-play asks for a time and gets better with your note, her numbers and history are specific, she gives you a recent manager to call, she's still in on these terms, and you'd want her walking into a shop with SANO's name on it.

A · She's the one

"I want you on the team. I'll call your reference, and your offer letter and commission plan come to you in writing. Then we lock in Tuesday, December 1."

B · Not sure yet

"This was really good. I want to think it over, and I'll text you by noon tomorrow either way."

Lunch traps: don't ask (California law)

Small talk is where these slip out. Not her age, kids, marriage or family plans, health, religion, where she's from or where her accent is from, citizenship, what she was paid anywhere, or her driving or criminal record. If she brings one up, don't follow up and don't write it down.

Don't say

"Jasper" · any profit-share or team-bonus number · that health insurance comes at six months · that the whole Valley is hers · another client's name · any price that isn't on the pricing page · her employer's name, to anyone.

After lunch, text Claude your notes ("Claude Nisa notes: …"). You'll get her score and the next steps for the offer letter.
