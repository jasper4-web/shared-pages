01

The Line — fully built

What was missing: the past piled up unbounded, four of the six small wins were unreachable, and it had only ever been drawn mid-morning on a good day. All three are fixed. The past collapses to one pill once it passes three stops, always-counts becomes a "hop off here" cluster welded to the train so all six are reachable from wherever you are, and it is rendered below across every state that matters — including the two that decide whether it survives a real week.

Misses are drawn as stations the train went past — hollow ring, muted label, the word PASSED — never as red blocks. On the bad-day state the route stays the same shape and a green line appears under the train telling him what is still winnable. That is law #1 handled structurally rather than by softening a colour.

02

The Face — executed properly

What was sloppy, named:

① The dial was a closed 360° circle, so 21:50 sat hard against 06:00 and the eye could not find where the day started. ② Six win labels were rotated tangentially — 6px vertical type on the left and right of the circle, unreadable in one second, which is most of what "sloppy" meant. ③ The win dots were 15px targets, a third of the minimum. ④ Short blocks were invisible: market-close is twenty minutes, which is 6.3° — a four-pixel sliver. ⑤ There were no hour anchors, so an arc's position told you nothing absolute. ⑥ Past-missed and future-shallow were rendered nearly the same dark colour. ⑦ The now-marker was a dot the same size as a win dot, so the current moment was not unmistakable. ⑧ The centre was a card-language button dropped inside a dial — two visual languages in one object.

What replaced them: an open 300° gauge with the mouth at the bottom, so the day has a visible start and end; hour anchors at 06 · 09 · 12 · 15 · 18 · 21 in horizontal type outside the ring; a duration-true sweep with an 8° floor so no block can vanish (the overflow is taken back from the longest segment — the distortion is under 4° across the whole day); the now-marker is a full-width wedge with a glow and an outside pointer, plus the centre carries a progress ring for the block you are in; missed time is a thin dashed outline, distinct from the solid future; and the six wins became a row of 46px pads under the dial's open mouth with horizontal labels — the day pours into them.

03

Three attempts at the seam

The Line's clarity about now-and-next, with The Face's whole-day-in-one. Three genuinely different places to put the join — one favours each parent, and one belongs to neither.

My read

Ranked on the thing he actually praised: can I see what's done, where I am, and how the day will go — in one second, with today emphasised?

1st

X1 · The Dial-Head Line my pick

It keeps everything he said he liked and adds the only thing he said was cool about the other one. The route is unchanged — done behind you, the train where you are, the day ahead in order — and a 104px dial sits on top of it answering "how much day is there, and how much of it is left" in a single glance, without him reading a single row. The dial is a summary, which is what a whole-day-in-one object is actually good at; the route is the detail, which is what a line is good at. Neither is asked to do the other's job.

2nd

The Line, on its own the safe answer

Now genuinely finished, and it survives all eight states — including the bad day, which it handles better than anything else in three rounds: the shape of the route never changes, misses are just stations you went past, and the hop-off cluster means there is always something to tap. If you want the least risk and the fewest new ideas, ship this and nothing else.

3rd

The Face, properly built much better — and still not first

Built right, it is the most striking thing in this document and the whole day genuinely is legible in one object. But see the argument against, below: a dial answers "how much" beautifully and answers "what next" poorly, and "what next" is the question this region exists to answer. It also costs 330px before anything else appears.

4–5

X2 Route-in-the-Face · X3 The Horizon

X2 is the most elegant object here and the one I would build if the Face wins — it uses the dial's dead centre for the three rows that matter instead of one instruction. Its cost is that the centre of a circle is the narrowest place on the screen, so objectives truncate. X3 is the cheapest by far, at roughly half the height of any dial, and the sun riding the horizon is the most immediately readable now-marker in three rounds — but flattening the day to an arc loses the block structure, so it tells you when you are without telling you what is left.

Hand-off to the GM

What I produced

renditions/rend-timeblock-v3.html — The Line and The Face each rendered across the same eight states, three fusions, and a visible cull of 24 candidate approaches with 17 killed in writing. Round 2's file is untouched. Both designs are generated from one shared state table, so neither got favourable data.

My pick, and what it costs

X1 · The Dial-Head Line. The route he already likes, with a 104px whole-day dial as its header. It is the only option that adds the Face's one real strength — instant "how much day is left" — without asking a circle to do a list's job. It costs ~118px more than the bare Line, which is real but affordable given NEXT UP still clears the fold in every state.

The strongest argument against my pick

The dial head is decoration and I may be fooling myself. Every fact it carries is already on the screen: the route's coloured track shows what's done, the train shows where he is, and "3h 15m to the terminus" is written in words two rows below. If he never looks at it, I have spent 118px — a third of the fold budget — on a graphic that repeats the row underneath it. The test is brutal and cheap: build the bare Line first, live with it for a week, and only add the dial head if he finds himself wanting a summary he doesn't already have. If I am wrong about this, the bare Line (2nd place) is the right answer and the dial belongs in The Record, not on Today.

There is a second, sharper version of the same objection: a dial is a "how much is left" instrument, and he is a man who abandons systems when he falls behind. An object whose entire job is to show how much of the day has burned may be exactly the wrong thing to put at the top of the screen at 3pm on a bad day. The Line never shows that; it shows the next stop. That difference is not aesthetic, and it is the reason the bare Line might beat everything here.

Honest verdict on The Face at phone size

The brief asked me to say plainly if the concept doesn't hold up once built properly. It holds up — it is legible, the current moment is unmistakable, no label collides with anything, and short blocks survive. But it is the wrong instrument for this particular question. A dial is excellent at proportion ("two thirds of the day is gone") and poor at sequence ("what do I do after this"), and this region exists to answer sequence. My honest read is that the Face's idea deserves to live as a component — the dial head in X1, or the summary object on The Record — rather than as the region itself. That is not a rejection of what he liked; it is where the thing he liked works best.

What verification caught in my own work

Six defects, all found by rendering and measuring rather than by reading my own code. Two of them were lies in the mock, not layout bugs, which is worse:

  • The bad-day rescue line asserted "2 deep stops and 6 side stops" when the state it sits in actually has one deep stop and five wins left. It was hardcoded. It is now computed from the state, like everything else on the screen — a mock that lies about its own data is worthless for a decision.
  • The Face's inner progress ring swept the whole dial instead of the current block's own arc, so "45 of 75 minutes into Block 2" rendered as a gold arc covering 40% of the day. It read as a completely different — and false — statement. It now spans only the block it describes.
  • The off-script banner broke the fold law. Above the instruction it pushed NEXT UP to +332. Moved below the train: nothing but a penance is allowed to push the instruction down.
  • My own pick broke the fold law too. The dial head at 104px put NEXT UP at +352. Fixed properly rather than by shrinking type — the dial now replaces the route's text header instead of sitting on top of it, which is also the better idea. All nineteen mocks now clear 299.
  • At 06:05 the wins header read "0 TODAY · +0" — the single most accusatory number on the emptiest screen in the set. It now reads "ALL 6 STILL TO PLAY".
  • A find-and-replace put an apostrophe inside a JS string and broke the entire page. Caught only because the check watches for console errors; every mock was blank and the screenshots would have been of nothing.

Things the GM should know

  • The eight states are the deliverable, not the decoration. Six of the eight only exist because the brief demanded them, and two of those six changed the design: the evening state forced the collapsing-past pill, and the bad-day state forced the rescue line. A design reviewed only in its happy state is a design that has not been reviewed.
  • Saturday is a genuinely different shape and both designs handle it without a special case — six stops instead of fourteen, and the dial's arcs simply get longer. No separate layout needed.
  • Off-script deliberately keeps always-counts live while everything else desaturates. An off-script day still lets him put something on the board; that seemed truer to the mechanic than freezing the whole screen.
  • The 8° floor on the Face is a small lie about time — about 4° across the whole day, taken out of "Personal life". It is documented in the mock rather than hidden, but if the GM would rather be strictly time-true, the cost is that market-close and Bible become untappable slivers.
  • The tsparticles v3 loadSlim bug from round 2 is still open in renditions/rend-bank.html and header-directions.html — particles are created but never painted, silently. Nothing in this file depends on it.
