THE TODAY PAGE · THE MERGE · FIVE WAYS · NOTHING BUILT

The checklist and the
side strip, joined to
the back page.

You picked two things: the Notebook's checklist and the little strip beside it, and the Two-Sided Day's back page — where the depth lives. All five below have exactly those two things. What changes is what the back page is attached to and how you get to it — and, because you said the side strip isn't all the way there yet, the strip is drawn five different ways so you can point at one.

Every screen is a drawing. Nothing has been built into the app.

FROM E
The checklist front. Time, name, a box. The whole day is one object with the live block sitting in it — no separate NOW card, nothing wedged.
FROM E
The side strip. Your wins, small, off to the side, tapped in passing. "Not all the way out but it's cool" — so five treatments below, from a full margin to a single row.
FROM I
The back page. The depth: what you're doing, what you wrote, the last time you ran it, the goal it feeds, what it pays. Never on the front.
THE QUESTION
Is the back page per block (five different backs, one per line) or per day (one back for the whole day)? M1, M3 and M4 say per block. M2 says per day. M5 says don't flip at all.
MERGE 1

Every line has a back

The checklist is the whole front. Tap any line and it turns over into its own page of depth.

The simplest join, and the closest to both things you liked. Front: the day as a checklist with the margin down the right. Every row is a door — the live one, the ones behind you, the ones ahead. Tap 15:05 at nine in the morning and you're writing the objective for a block that hasn't started.

The back holds everything the front can't: the objective, your notes, the last two times you ran this same block, the goal it feeds and how far along that goal is, and what a clean run pays. The front stays a checklist forever because all the depth has somewhere else to live.

MERGE 2

One back for the whole day

The switch at the top: DOING is the checklist, WRITING is the whole day's depth as one page.

Instead of five separate backs, the day has one back — every block in order, each with its objective and what you wrote, on one page you can read top to bottom. You write into it in place.

The advantage over M1 is real: at 21:30 the day is already written up. You don't visit five backs to see what happened — you flip once and read. Record gets a real entry for free. The cost is that the depth for the block you're in is one flip away rather than on the line you just tapped.

MERGE 3

The back comes up as a drawer

You never leave the checklist. The back rises from the bottom over it, still attached to the line you tapped.

M1's per-block depth, but nothing ever replaces the day. Tap a line and the back slides up over the lower half; the checklist stays visible above it, dimmed, with your line still highlighted. Push it back down with a thumb. There is no "where am I" moment — you can always see you're on the Today page, which matters when you open this half-awake.

Because the drawer's edge lives at the bottom, the side strip moves out of the margin and becomes one row under the day. This is the tightest treatment of the wins: six little boxes, one line, no column stealing width from the block names.

MERGE 4

The strip is the door

The side strip becomes a proper edge: your wins live on it, and pulling it opens the back page.

This is the answer to "the side strip isn't all the way there yet." Instead of a passive margin, the right edge becomes a working spine. The wins sit on it as small boxes. At the bottom of it is a tab. Drag the spine left and the back page slides in over the day.

One edge does two jobs, so the front is a clean full-width checklist and there's no switch at the top eating a row. The risk is honest: it's a gesture, and gestures have to be discovered. So the tab is always visible and always says what it is.

MERGE 5

Both halves, always on

No flip, no drawer, no gesture. The day is the top of the screen and the depth of the live block is the bottom.

The version with nothing to open. The top ~40% is the checklist, compressed and scrollable — you can see where you are in the day. The bottom is permanently the back page of whatever block you're in: objective, notes, thread, what it feeds. Both things you liked, on screen at the same time, for the price of each being smaller.

The wins live on the seam between the two halves — one row, six boxes, the quietest place on the screen. Tap a different line up top and the bottom half becomes that block's depth. Nothing appears or disappears, ever.

My read. M3 · The drawer is the one I'd build. It gives you the per-block depth you actually want to write into, it keeps the day on screen the entire time — which is the thing that kills "where am I" on a phone you open half-awake — and its side-strip treatment is the only one that lets block names use the full width of the screen. M5 is the safest and needs no gestures at all, but at 375px the top half only ever shows three or four lines of your day, and that's a real loss.
On the strip specifically: you said it isn't all the way there. I think that's because a margin of six unlabelled ticks is decoration — it doesn't do anything. M3's one row and M4's spine are the two that make it a control. If you want the wins genuinely minor, M5's seam is the quietest place on the screen.
There's also an independent take coming from an outside designer who has never seen this project — briefed only on what you're building, what you didn't like, and the rules. It'll be a separate page.
Pick a merge and a strip — they're independent choices, e.g. "M1 with M3's strip." Nothing goes into the app until you do.
