One object, five compositions

Round 1 offered six visual languages and the owner picked four parts out of four different ones. This round is not six new ideas. It builds a single design system from those four parts — one type scale, one shape rule, one colour logic, one vertical rhythm — and then asks the only question left: how should they be composed?

Every screen below uses the same components. Nothing is restyled between versions. What changes is proportion, order, and which part dominates. Sample state is held identical throughout: smoke — 6 days clean, best 11, 09:42, gate held 12 of the last 14 · the dishwasher — 4 weeks at or under, best 4, 0 of 1 used, Tuesday.

The four kept parts

what survived round 1 · and what was explicitly left behind

He separated form from concept in his verdict, so this round does too. Three of the four parts arrive without the idea that carried them.

From 01 · The Chain
The big visual
The streak at real scale — “I really just like the big visual with the six days.” It is now the emotional centre of every version.
Dropped: the chain metaphor entirely. No links, no forging, no welding, no “breaking a link”. He said the idea itself wasn’t cool. It is gone, including the language.
From 02 · The Gate Clock
The ring
His strongest single reaction in the whole set. The 270° arc with the rule at the crown. Kept exactly — same geometry, same danger bands.
Held in check: it is not allowed to become the whole screen in any version except the one that argues for it on purpose (03).
From 02 + 04
The modules
Discrete offers as separate takeable units. The hold-until-14:00 mechanic was rated “OK”, so the mechanic is unchanged and only the presentation is rebuilt.
Dropped: the quest-board framing, the rank/rarity language, the trophy shelf. A module is now a plain instrument row, not a game card.
From 05 · The Testimony
The plain graph
“Rudimentary but a cool little style.” That is a compliment about restraint, so it has been made more consistent, not more polished.
Dropped: the testimony framing, the 60px scripture setting, the reverent tone. Scripture survives as one of several rotating reason lines, not as the screen’s thesis.

The system

four decisions, held across all five versions

Congruency is not a mood, it is a set of decisions you refuse to break. These are the four the brief asked for, decided once.

2 · One shape rule

Every terminal is round. The ring is a stroke with a round cap; so is every module’s progress rail; so is every dot on the graph; so is every button and every legend key. Nothing in the vice screens ends in a square. The modules therefore echo the big visual’s geometry rather than contrasting with it — the whole screen is made of the same one mark at four sizes.

the same cap at every size

Corner radius follows suit: 14px on every module, 99px on every control. Two values, no others.

3 · One colour logic

Ember — nowThe rule that is live this minute. The ring’s elapsed arc, the running module. Heat means currently being held.
Gold — himEverything earned or offered to him: XP, the gate marker, every action. Gold is the app talking to him.
Green — bankedPast tense only. Claimed, clean, done. Never used for anything pending.
Red — not usedThere is no red anywhere on a vice screen. The make-up strip is amber. A slip is ember. This is law #1 enforced at the palette, not at the layout.

4 · One rhythm

An 8px measure, four stops: 8 · 16 · 26 · 38. 16 separates things of the same kind, 26 separates movements, 38 is reserved for the one break that matters in a given composition. Nothing else exists.

And one reading order, which is what the five versions actually argue about:
where you are → what you could do → how it has gone. Four of the five hold that order. 05 deliberately inverts it, to test whether at 9:42 in the morning he needs the offer before the trophy.

One consequence worth stating plainly: the two vices now share a grammar instead of sharing a screen layout. One ring — a 270° arc with the rule at its crown; for smoking the crown is noon, for the dishwasher it is the weekly allowance. One graph — a dot placed against a line; for smoking the line is noon and the dot is the time of first use, for the dishwasher the line is the allowance and the dot is that week’s count. Same two objects, two different rules, no re-learning.
01

The Crown

the ring and the streak fused into one object

The two visuals he liked most were competing for the same real estate, so this version refuses to stack them: the streak lives inside the ring. One object, roughly 44% of the screen, that answers “how long have I held this” and “where am I in today” in a single glance. Modules and the graph then sit underneath in strict rhythm. This is the most direct reading of his two strongest reactions — and the only version where the big visual and the ring are not fighting.

Composition
One dominant object (44%), then two movements of equal weight. The eye lands once and then descends in even steps.
How the parts relate
The big visual and the ring are the same element, which is the only way to give both his favourite things the top of the screen without either shrinking.
Both rules, one grammar
1a and 1b are the identical layout. The ring reads a day or a week; the graph plots a time or a count; the streak counts days or weeks. Nothing else changes — and the make-up strip is shown on 1b in amber, which is what “mercy with a price” looks like in this palette.
Risk — measured
The hero costs real estate. On a 390×844 phone the first module lands at 422px and the graph at 686px, so the history is the last thing above the fold and the screen scrolls about 145px. That is the price of giving the ring 300px, and it is the one thing to weigh against 02. The ring’s interior clears 213px against a 206px streak block, so the longest state (“32h clean”) still fits — but there is no room left, and any third line inside the ring would have to go back out.
The safest composition, and the one that most literally answers what he said he liked.
02

The Masthead

type dominates · the ring becomes a jewel

The inverse trade: the streak gets the full width as a masthead, and the ring is demoted to a 108px live indicator beside it. The bet is that the number is what he wants to see and the ring is what he wants to check — two different jobs that deserve two different sizes. It buys a great deal of room below, so the modules can sit two-up and the graph gets full width without the screen scrolling.

Composition
A wide masthead, then three even movements. Nothing dominates by size; the reading order does the work instead.
How the parts relate
The ring stops being a hero and becomes an instrument — a live read you glance at, the way you glance at a fuel gauge. It keeps every mark of the full ring at 112px, so the object is still recognisably the one he liked.
What it buys — measured
The only version that does not scroll. Streak, both modules, the full graph and the reason line occupy 600px of an 844px screen; the modules start at 195px and the graph at 325px. Every other version here runs past 900px. That matters for a sixty-second daily interaction.
Risk
He called the ring “the thing at the top that looks very nice”. This version makes it small. If what he liked was its size rather than its information, this is the version that loses the thing he actually responded to.
Most efficient. Possibly too efficient — it optimises for a glance he may not want yet.
03

The Instrument

the ring at full size · everything else is instrumentation

The version that takes his ring reaction at maximum face value. The ring runs the full width of the screen, the streak is demoted into a three-cell readout plate directly beneath it, and the modules become a horizontal rail you swipe rather than a stack you scroll. The graph anchors the base. It reads like a cockpit — the one composition where the screen’s job is monitoring rather than motivating.

Composition
One enormous object, then a hard horizontal break (the plate), then a sideways axis (the rail), then the base. Four movements, each in a different direction — the most architectural of the five.
How the parts relate
The ring rules and everything else defers to it. The streak stops being the emotional centre and becomes a number on a plate.
The rail
Horizontal modules are the one real innovation here: three offers occupy the space of one, so the screen can carry the whole ladder without three cards demanding decisions. It also physically caps what is visible — one and a half modules at rest, which is the anti-nagging default from round 1 expressed as layout rather than as a rule.
Risk — the real one
It demotes the thing he called “really nice… the big visual with the six days”. A cockpit monitors; it does not move anyone. On a screen whose stated job is to make him want to hold the line, that is a genuine cost, not a stylistic one.
Best-looking of the five. Least likely to change what he does at 9:42 in the morning.
04

The Spine

one continuous document · no cards anywhere

Every other version puts things in boxes. This one asks whether the modules need to be boxes at all. The ring is cropped to its crown — a wide, shallow arc across the top, which is literally the part he liked and none of the part he didn’t — the streak sits flush left beneath it, and the modules become rows divided by hairlines. The result is quiet and dense, and it is the only version where the screen reads as a single object rather than a stack of components.

Composition
No boxes. A single left-aligned spine with hairline rules doing all the separation, and the cropped crown as a lid.
How the parts relate
The ring is the same SVG as every other version, simply clipped to 116px. That is the strictest possible reading of “keep the top visual” — the geometry is untouched and only the frame changed.
What it proves
That the modules do not need card chrome to read as discrete takeable units. The state colour on the label, the rail, and the gold payout are enough. That is a useful finding regardless of which version ships.
Risk
Cropping the ring removes the countdown and the danger bands, which were the ring’s actual information. What is left is decoration in the shape of an instrument — handsome, and less useful than it looks. Also the flattest of the five: without cards, the live module has to compete for attention using colour alone.
The most disciplined layout here, built on the weakest use of the part he liked most.
05

The Inversion

act first · the streak is the payoff you scroll to

The one version that breaks the reading order on purpose. It opens with what he could do right now, gives the ring the middle as context, and puts the big visual at the bottom, lit from below, as the thing the screen resolves into. The argument: at 09:42 the streak is not information, it is a reward — and a reward at the top of a screen is spent before it is earned. It is also the only version where scrolling down is going somewhere good, which matters for a screen he opens on his worst days.

Composition
Deliberately inverted: act → context → reward. The payoff block is lit from below and pinned to the bottom of the screen, so the composition has a floor rather than a ceiling.
How the parts relate
The modules lead, the ring supports, the graph explains, the streak pays. Every part keeps its exact styling from the system — only the order changed, which is the cleanest possible proof that the system holds.
The honest argument for it
On a good day every version works. On a bad day — the day that killed v1 — the top of the screen is the first thing he sees, and in the other four versions that is a streak he may have just broken. Here the first thing he sees is something he can still do.
Risk — measured
It buries the thing he said he liked. The first module lands at 85px — the best number in the set for “what do I do now” — but the streak does not appear until roughly 760px, which is below the fold on a 390×844 phone. He asked for the streak “more grand”, and this version makes him scroll to it. Real idea, possibly the wrong answer to this brief.
The most interesting argument in the set and the least likely to be what he asked for.

Hand-off — agent 5, round 2

What I produced and where

This file — renditions/rend-vices-v2.html. Round 1’s rend-vices.html is untouched for comparison. Five compositions built from one component set: a shared type scale, one shape rule, one colour logic and one 8px rhythm, all declared at the top of the page so the GM can hold the system rather than reverse-engineer it. index.html was not modified. XP values are unchanged from round 1 (+35 / +60 / +120 / +180 / +400 · +90 / +250) — nothing in his feedback gave a reason to move them.

The one I would ship

01 · The Crown. It is the only composition where the two things he reacted to most strongly — the big visual and the ring — are not competing for the same space, because they became the same object. Everything else follows in a plain rhythm underneath. It also carries both vices in an identical layout with only the rule changing, which means the GM builds one screen, not two.

If it needs a hedge: ship 01 and steal 03’s horizontal module rail. The rail is the best single idea in this round — it caps how many offers are visible at rest without needing a rule to enforce it, which is the round-1 nagging guard turned into layout.

Ranked

  1. 01 · The Crown — fuses his two favourite parts instead of stacking them. Safest, most direct, works identically for both vices. Ship this.
  2. 03 · The Instrument — the best-looking screen in the set and the source of the rail idea, but it demotes the big visual to a number on a plate. A cockpit monitors; it does not motivate. Take its rail, leave its hierarchy.
  3. 02 · The Masthead — measured at 600px against 844, it is the only version that does not scroll at all, which is worth real money against the sixty-second rule. Loses because it shrinks the ring to 112px, and “the thing at the top that looks very nice” may well have been a comment about its size. If he says the screens feel long, this is the answer — it is the fallback, not a rejection.
  4. 05 · The Inversion — the most interesting argument here and the one I would most like to be wrong about. It is the only version designed for his bad days rather than his good ones. It also directly contradicts “show my streak more grand”, so it goes to him as a question, not a candidate.
  5. 04 · The Spine — the weak one, and I would not ship it. Its layout discipline is the best in the set, but it gets there by cropping the ring down to an arc with no countdown, no danger bands and no live read. That is the part he liked most, reduced to decoration. It stays in the set because it proves modules do not need card chrome — a finding worth keeping even though the screen isn’t.

Which of the four parts was hardest to reconcile — and why

The ring. Not the graph, which slotted in everywhere, and not the modules, which are inherently flexible. The ring is hard because it is the only part that is simultaneously the most beautiful and the most information-dense object on the screen, and those two properties want opposite sizes.

At full size it is genuinely striking — and it takes 55% of the screen to say one number (“2h 18m”). Shrink it and the danger bands, the gate tick and the now-hand all become sub-pixel decoration; crop it and you keep the beauty and throw away every piece of information it carried. Each of the five versions is essentially a different answer to that one problem: 01 fills it with the streak so the space earns its keep, 02 shrinks it to an indicator, 03 surrenders the screen to it, 04 crops it, 05 sizes it honestly at 188px and puts it in the middle where a supporting element belongs.

The second-hardest was the big visual against the ring, for the same reason from the other side: two heroes, one top-of-screen. Only 01 dissolves that conflict rather than choosing a winner — which is most of why it is ranked first.

Easiest by a distance: the graph. Keeping it rudimentary made it universally compatible. It is the same twelve lines of drawing code in all five versions and it never once fought the layout. That is worth remembering — the part he described most casually turned out to be the most portable.

Measured, not asserted

Every screen was rendered headlessly at 390×844 and measured. Where each version places its first module and its graph, and how tall the whole screen runs:

  • 01 · Crown — module 422px · graph 686px · total 923px
  • 02 · Masthead — module 195px · graph 325px · total 600px, no scroll
  • 03 · Instrument — module 520px · graph 657px · total 914px
  • 04 · Spine — module 274px · graph 652px · total 904px
  • 05 · Inversion — module 85px · graph 567px · streak ~760px · total 912px

Read across that list and the trade is plain: the more grandly the streak is shown, the further down the actionable part of the screen goes. 01 buys the grandeur and pays 145px of scroll. 02 refuses to pay it. 05 inverts the bill entirely. That is genuinely the whole decision, and it is his to make.

Congruency decisions the GM should know I made

  • Red is not used on a vice screen at all. Not for slips, not for the make-up, not for a broken gate. Ember covers “live and at risk”, amber covers “you owe a make-up”, and the palette itself now enforces law #1 rather than relying on layout restraint. If the GM integrates these, the existing --blood usages in the vice sheet should go with them.
  • The two vices share a grammar, not a layout hack. One ring (270°, the rule at the crown), one graph (a dot against a line), one streak object. Smoking plots a time against noon; the dishwasher plots a count against the allowance. He learns the objects once.
  • The chain language is gone completely — no links, no welding, no forging anywhere in this file, including in the make-up copy, which now reads as a price rather than as a repair.
  • Modules are unstyled by rank. Round 1 gave them rarity/rank framing; that is removed. A module is now state-coloured only: ember = running, gold = offered, green = claimable, dashed = locked.

Carried forward from round 1 — still open, still not mine to fix

  • The gauge arc in index.html overstates its own number — dial() uses Math.PI*21 as the dasharray for an arc of radius 19 (true length Math.PI*19), so 50% renders as roughly 55%. The rings and dials in both rendition files use the corrected length.
  • viceClean('smoke') can be tapped before noon, so a clean day can be banked at 9 AM and the gate broken at 11.
  • The vendored tsParticles slim bundle needs await loadSlim(tsParticles) before load(), or it reports a full particle count and paints an entirely blank canvas. Round 2 uses no particles, but this will bite the next agent who reaches for lib/tsparticles.
  • The ring hard-codes his waking day as 05:00–23:00. PROFILE §8c lists actual sleep hours as unknown. Every version in this file inherits that assumption — it should be confirmed before build, because the ring is wrong at both ends if it is off.
