What changed, what I'd ship, what is still wrong

The diagnosis was right

Three rounds failed because I was theming with words. Last round I wrote that "The Envelopes" had thickness carrying the balance — and rendered a brown rectangle with a number in it. The Cellar, the Counting House and the Logbook were the same row-list with a different noun on top. This round the object had to exist in pixels before a single caption was written: the envelopes have wads of paper standing out of them, the jars have coin fills at five different levels, the stack is 580px of coin edges against a graduated rule, and the dial is drawn from real gauge geometry rather than eyeballed.

Ranked

1 · 01 The Vault — the safe pick, and the one I would ship. Built from the shipped app's own parts: the furnace plate (gold instead of ember, because gold is what "earned" already means in this app), the real three-dial gauge strip using the same 46×27 SVG arc as renderGauges(), the glance card with .ms stat bars, and reward rows in the .blk schedule-block language with domain colour bars. It sits beside the Today tab without a seam. It is also the only one of the ten that needs no new design system to build.

2 · 09 The Stack. The best object in the set. Distance stops being arithmetic and becomes a height you read in one glance, and the graduated rule means every reward is marked on the same scale. It is the only direction where the balance and the whole reward list are a single drawing. Coin edges are a repeating gradient, so it costs almost nothing to build.

3 · 07 The Dial. The most finished single object here and the one with real craft in it — ticks, labels, needle and arc are all computed from ang(v) = 135 + (v/4000)·270, then swept into place with GSAP. Its weakness is honest: the face cannot hold five conditions, so they live on an engraved legend beneath it. That is an instrument convention, but it is still a second surface.

Honest notes

  • 05 The Ledger is the one that most nearly repeated the old mistake. A ruled table in a book is still rows. It earns its place on the book itself — page-edge block, gutter shadow, ribbon, red margin rule, double-entry columns — but if you read it as "a list with paper behind it", cut it.
  • 10 The Key Cabinet is the weakest. The tags are handsome but a hanging tag is a card with a hole in it, and at 390px the hanging never fully convinces.
  • 03's jars slightly lie, as they did last round: five jars filling separately implies five pots when there is one balance. The fills are true ratios of balance-to-price, and the labels state the real figures, but the object still implies something the data does not do.
  • Two of the ten use a numbered legend (07 and, in spirit, 04). That is one more than I would like; if both survive, give one of them a different way to carry its conditions.

Verification

arsenal-lint: clean ✓. Eleven screens rendered headlessly and screenshotted at 390×844 in both states, and I looked at every one and ran the caption test on it. No console errors, no failed requests, no horizontal overflow — every phone 388/388, and the gallery tested at a real 390px viewport with overflow-x:hidden disabled so nothing could be masked. GSAP and tsParticles are both actually used: the dial needle sweeps, jar fills rise, tags settle on an elastic ease, the beam tips, and the claim throws real gold dust.

Defects found by looking, not by reading CSS: the coin stack collapsed to a sliver because its column had no height for the percentage fill to resolve against; the balance beam tipped the wrong way, showing 2,000 as lighter than 1,530; the envelope amounts sat in the overlapped zone and were unreadable; the jar fill read as wicker rather than coins until the banding became tiled discs; dial marker 5 collided with the readout; the ledger ribbon hung over the balance; and the span{display:block} scoping bug from last round reappeared in nine new selectors.

For the GM

  • 01 is buildable now. It reuses .furnace, .gauges/dial(), .glance/.ms, .blk, .win and .btn as they already exist. Nothing new but a third gauge label and the token cards.
  • The blockers are unchanged and none are visual: claim() deletes a reward instead of recording it, so no direction here can show history; rewards are one-shot for all 91 days; S.privileges is a bare integer with no way to spend one; and dailyXp() uses a lifetime average, so every "~N days" on this page drifts optimistic.
  • Day one still leads with the privilege, not the cheapest reward — five days rather than eleven. That finding survives whichever object wins.
