Burn the Boats · Agent 2 · Round 2 · Fusion

One system, three altitudes,
four ways to move between them.

Round 1's Spine, Six Doors and Friday table are not three pages any more. They are three altitudes of one object, and the only question left is the one he actually asked: how do you zoom? The One and The Board are gone.

All four prototypes below render the same data from the same source — Agent 1's v4 model, seven ranked goals, landmark milestones, block-sized commitments. Nothing differs between them except the navigation. That is deliberate: it is the only way to judge a navigation model.

It is Overlay Motion
01

The One Spine

Zoom = filter · nothing moves · the spine is never redrawn

There is only ever one screen. The spine, the station dates and their positions are fixed furniture; zooming does not navigate anywhere, it changes what is lit. Tap a thread and the other six collapse to hairline ticks in place while that goal's rows open where they already sat — the same element grows, nothing is replaced. Tap the top node and the week unfolds inline while the run compresses behind it. The back path is a pill in the bottom-left thumb corner.

OUT OF REACHONE-THUMB ZONE
01 · THE ONE SPINE — TAP A THREAD TO FOCUS · TAP THE TOP NODE FOR THE WEEK
The gesture
Tap anything to zoom into it. A thread row → that goal. The top node → this week. There is no second gesture to learn and no drag anywhere.
The back path
A zoom pill pinned bottom-left, above the tab bar, reading RUN › SANO. Tap a crumb to pop to it. It is the only persistent chrome and it sits inside the thumb arc.
What persists
Everything. The horizon bar, the spine, every station date and the burned portion of the line are identical pixels at all three altitudes. You cannot lose your place because your place never moved — the dimmed threads stay on screen as ticks, so the run is still visible while you are inside one goal. No other option here can say that.
Thumb cost
Targets are spread over the full column height, so zooming in often needs a reach into the top third. Zooming out is always in the corner. Turn on the reach overlay: the pill is inside the arc, the upper stations are not.
Where he lands
Tuesday → the week node already unfolded. Friday → the same node, in ritual mode. He is never dropped at the run level — that tier is monthly by Agent 1's cadence rule, and this is the only option where landing deep still shows you the whole shape.

02

The Stack

Zoom = layers · child rises over parent · crumbs dock in the thumb zone

The familiar iOS model, with one correction: the breadcrumb is at the bottom, not the top. Every stacked sheet drops a crumb into a dock sitting directly above the tab bar, so going back up is always a thumb tap and never a reach to a corner chevron. The tapped card morphs into the new sheet's header — a measured FLIP, so the object you touched is the object that arrives.

OUT OF REACHONE-THUMB ZONE
02 · THE STACK — TAP A GOAL, THEN ITS WEEK NODE · CRUMBS GO BACK
The gesture
Tap to descend, swipe down or tap a crumb to ascend. Both work; the swipe is the one he already has in muscle memory from every other app on the phone.
The back path
The dock. THE RUN › SANO › WEEK as three tappable crumbs at y≈740, plus a ↑ UP button on the right edge. Deepest level is one tap from the shallowest — you never unwind one layer at a time.
What persists
The horizon bar and the dock. Not the content. When SANO's ladder is open, the run is gone — you know where you are from a text crumb, not from seeing it. That is the trade this model makes, and it is the reason it is not ranked first.
Thumb cost
The lowest of the four for going back: every ascent target is in the bottom 100px. Descending costs a reach up the list, same as F1.
Where he lands
Tuesday → the week sheet already open, two crumbs behind it. Friday → the same sheet in ritual mode. He can dismiss straight down to the run without reading anything.

03

The Dial

Zoom = a control · altitude is a thing you hold, and the subject travels with you

Altitude becomes a physical three-position dial in the thumb zone. What makes it not a tab bar: it is ordered, and it carries a subject. Pick BODY at the run level and the middle notch relabels itself GOAL · BODY; slide right again and the week is filtered to BODY's commitments, with the rest of the week one tap away. Zooming out is a leftward drag with the thumb already resting there. The body cross-fades directionally, so the movement has a direction.

DRAG THE DIAL · OR SWIPE THE PAGE LEFT AND RIGHT
OUT OF REACHONE-THUMB ZONE
03 · THE DIAL — DRAG OR TAP THE DIAL · PICK A DIFFERENT GOAL AT THE RUN LEVEL
The gesture
Tap or drag the dial; or swipe the body left/right. Three targets, always in the same place, always reachable. It is the only option a half-asleep thumb can operate without looking.
The back path
Slide left. Always one gesture, always the same gesture, from any depth to any depth.
What persists
The horizon bar, the dial, and — the real idea — the subject. The dial's middle notch always names the goal you are holding, so "which goal am I inside" is answered by the control itself rather than by a crumb. Switch subject at the run level and the other two levels re-aim.
Thumb cost
The best of the four. Every navigation target sits inside the arc, permanently, at a fixed position. Nothing above the fold is ever required to move between altitudes.
Where he lands
Tuesday → notch 3, subject = the WIG. Friday → notch 3 in ritual mode, and the ritual unfilters itself because a weekly plan filtered to one goal is a lie about the week.

04

The Descent

Zoom = expansion · one document · no navigation chrome at all

There are no screens. There is one scroll, and depth is what happens when you tap something: the goal opens its ladder inside its own row, a milestone opens the week's commitments inside itself. Nothing is ever replaced and nothing is ever hidden — the parent is literally still above your thumb. One rule keeps it from becoming the org chart: only one path is open at a time, so opening BODY closes SANO. A depth strip at the top counts how deep you are and a single floating button collapses everything.

OUT OF REACHONE-THUMB ZONE
04 · THE DESCENT — TAP SANO, THEN ITS THIS-WEEK RUNG · ONE PATH OPEN AT A TIME
The gesture
Tap to open, tap the same thing to close. That is the entire interaction model — there is nothing else to learn and nothing that can be got wrong.
The back path
Tap the open header again, or the floating ▲ CLOSE button in the bottom-right corner, which appears only when something is open. It collapses one level per tap.
What persists
Literally everything, because nothing was ever removed. The parent row is directly above the expansion and its siblings are directly below. Context is not preserved — it is never lost.
Thumb cost
Best-in-class for closing (corner button). Worst for reading: with SANO and its week open the page measures ~2,700px — what the second tap opened sits roughly 1,500px down, so he is scrolling to see what he just did. The auto-scroll is not a nicety here, it is load-bearing.
Where he lands
Tuesday → SANO and its week rung pre-opened and scrolled to, which is the only reason this model survives a default landing at all. Friday → the ritual opened inside the WIG's row, with the run still above it.

The ranking

What I would ship.

One of these is a zoom and three of them are navigation. That is the whole ranking.

1

01 · The One Spine — ship this

It is the only one of the four where zooming is not going somewhere. The spine, the dates and the burn never move; focus is a change of emphasis on furniture that stays put. That is what he described — "zoom in and zoom out of your different goals" — and it is the one model that cannot make him lose his place, because his place is still on screen as a row of ticks.

It also has the least chrome (a 40px pill against the Dial's 116px), and it is the only one that answers the cadence rule honestly: he lands at the week every day, and still sees the whole run behind it, so a tier he is only meant to touch monthly is never hidden and never nagged.

2

03 · The Dial

The best ergonomics by a distance — a fixed three-position control in the thumb arc that works at 6:40 AM without looking, and the only one where in and out are the same single gesture. Second because the subject filter is a real hazard: a week filtered to one goal is a week with work hidden in it, and the mitigation is a special case rather than a property. Its dial belongs in the winner: put F1's zoom pill where F3's dial is, and F1 gets F3's ergonomics for free.

3

02 · The Stack

Correct, familiar and slightly boring — and it is the version he pre-empted. Descending a sheet stack is not zooming; it is opening a document over another document. The one genuinely good idea in it is the crumb dock at the bottom, which fixes iOS's real ergonomic sin of putting Back in the top-left corner of a 6-inch phone. Steal the dock, drop the stack.

4

04 · The Descent

Theoretically the purest — nothing is ever hidden, so context cannot be lost — and practically the worst. Fully open it is the longest screen in the product, and a design whose governing law is "never render a wall" should be suspicious of a screen that renders everything. Its one transferable idea is auto-scroll-to-open, which every expanding UI should do and almost none do.

What I had to give up to make zoom work

1 · The Six Doors grid is gone. He liked the six tiles. Seven ranked goals do not fit a 2×3 grid, and Agent 1's model is right that LA Edible must be a goal rather than an integer. Tiles became ranked rows. That is a real loss of the thing he pointed at, traded for a level that can hold his actual portfolio and show rank — and rank is what decides which goal gets the 9 AM peak block.

2 · The Spine stopped being a calendar of everything. In Round 1 the Spine was all six domains across four month-end stations, and that cross-domain view is exactly what he called "very usable". Under a zoom model the Spine has to be able to mean one goal at the mid level. F1 is the only one of the four that keeps both meanings — the same spine, filtered — and that is the single strongest argument for it. In F2, F3 and F4 the cross-domain spine no longer exists at all.

3 · Something permanent now eats the bottom of the screen. Every zoom model needs a back path that a thumb can reach, and that costs 40–116px above the tab bar forever. On the Today tab that space is the schedule. The winner spends the least of it, and it is still not free.

4 · A third of the week view had to be deleted on Tuesdays. Agent 1's §4.3 forbids the capacity number being re-rendered mid-week as a shortfall. So the capacity line — the best thing in the Friday ritual — is absent from every Tuesday screen here. Correct, and it makes the Tuesday week view thinner than it looks like it should be.

Hand-off — Agent 2 · Round 2 · the fusion

For the GM. Nothing outside renditions/rend-goals-v2.html was written; rend-goals.html is untouched for comparison and index.html was read-only.

What I produced

  • Four navigation models over three altitudes, all live at 390×844, all rendering the same seven goals / four landmarks / six commitments from one shared data object — so the only variable between them is the zoom.
  • Each has a ▶ PLAY THE ZOOM button that runs its full RUN → GOAL → WEEK transition, and a global SLOW-MO toggle that stretches every transition 4× so the morph can actually be watched.
  • A THUMB REACH overlay draws the one-handed arc on all four phones at once. The gesture-cost claims in the notes are read off that overlay, not asserted.
  • A global TUESDAY / FRIDAY switch, because "where does he land" has two answers and the brief asked for both. Tuesday lands at the week, read-only. Friday lands at the same level in the four-card ritual.

The one I would ship, and the shape of it

  • F1 The One Spine, with F3's dial position for its pill. F1 has the right model — zoom as filter on fixed furniture — and F3 has the right ergonomics. The pill moves to where the dial sits and becomes a two-crumb control; nothing else about F1 changes.
  • Steal from F2: the bottom crumb dock concept (already absorbed above) and the FLIP morph into a header.
  • Steal from F4: auto-scroll so that whatever a tap opened is actually in view afterwards.
  • Do not ship F3's subject filter on the week. It is the one idea here that can hide real work.

Where I map onto Agent 1's spec — and where I diverge

  • Adopted verbatim: the five tiers; T0 as the unfailable layer; goals as ranked with a WIG; domain-as-tag so LA Edible is a real goal (7 goals, not 6); the four LANDMARKS (Aug 31 · Sep 28 · Oct 26 · Nov 23); the learning milestone before the outcome ones; 0 milestones for habit goals; parking as a first-class non-failing state; confidence as an unscored 1–5; and §4.3's rule that capacity is a Friday-only number — it appears on no Tuesday screen in any of the four.
  • My answer to his §8 open question — the one he was least sure about. I built the size bands, not the numeric block count: SMALL · NORMAL · BIG = 1 · 2 · 4 blocks. Three taps, no arithmetic, and the layout, capacity line and orphan check all read the same integer. Sizing five commitments numerically is five arithmetic decisions on a Friday afternoon, and the ritual's survival depends on it being short. His de-risked middle is the right default, not the fallback.
  • Divergence 1 — the mid tier is not only "one goal's milestones." Agent 1's T2 is per-goal. F1 renders T2 both ways: unfocused it is every goal's milestones on one shared timeline, focused it is one goal's ladder. The data model already supports this (milestones carry by dates, so grouping by landmark is a view, not a schema change) — but the spec never describes the cross-goal view, and that view is precisely what the owner called "very usable" in Round 1. Worth adding to the spec as a sanctioned second reading of T2.
  • Divergence 2 — a milestone whose date has passed unhit has no state in the spec. hitOn:null plus by in the past is silent. LA Edible's Aug 31 milestone is exactly this case and it is one of the most emotionally loaded rows in the product. I render it as PASSED · NOT HIT in gold with a one-tap push it action — never red, never a miss. The spec should name that state so it does not get invented three different ways.
  • Divergence 3 — seven goals, six domain colours. Adopting domain-as-tag means SANO and LA Edible are both WORK and therefore both gold. I distinguish them with the rank numeral and a dashed colour rail. It works, but it is the first visible cost of D1 and the GM should see it before it surprises someone.

Verified, not asserted

  • arsenal-lint clean; rendered headlessly at 390×844; every transition screenshotted mid-flight as well as at rest, because a morph that reads at its endpoints can still be wrong in the middle.
  • No console errors before or after driving every gesture in all four prototypes.
  • Scroll depths in the notes are measured from the rendered DOM. Tap counts are counted off the live prototypes.
  • GSAP Flip is not vendored. lib/gsap/gsap.min.js is the core only — the string "Flip" appears once, in a plugin-name list. The brief said Flip was available; it is not. The morphs here are real FLIP (measure, invert, play) driven by GSAP core, which is vendored. If the GM wants Flip.getState() in the app, the plugin file has to be added to the arsenal first.

For the GM — things I found that were not mine to fix

  • The tab rename knocks on, still. Round 1 flagged it; nothing has changed. Push currently carries the Anthony gate, the trading log and the LA Edible marker. Under Agent 1's migration the gate becomes m_sano_gate and LA Edible becomes g_kitchen — both land inside Goals cleanly. The trading session log does not. It is a daily action and it needs to move to Today or the evening close-out before Push becomes Goals.
  • Every zoom model here needs one new thing from the model: a "current subject". Which goal the user is looking at is UI state, not data, but F1/F3 both persist it across a tab switch and F4 restores it on landing. It wants to live next to the view state, not in S.goals.
  • The Friday ritual and the Goals tab are the same screen in all four fusions. That is a consequence of treating the week as an altitude rather than a mode, and I think it is right — but it means the ritual has no home of its own, and if the GM later wants a full-screen Friday takeover (like the verdict overlay), it will fight the zoom model. Worth deciding before build.
  • Nothing here shows a delivery load. Agent 1 §4.3 and PROFILE §8c both flag that the schedule assumes zero client-delivery blocks and becomes arithmetically impossible around October. The capacity line is where that will first become visible — these mocks show 9 of 20 committed, which will not survive Anthony signing.
