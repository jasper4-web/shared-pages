Burn the Boats · Agent 6 · The Bank, purely aesthetic

Seven materials for the same four things.

Every direction below carries all four elements — XP balance, privileges, boosts and rewards — with identical data. Nothing was dropped for prettiness. What changes is the material: what the tab is made of, and therefore how it feels to hold something you earned.

Demo state throughout: 1,530 XP banked · 2 privileges held · earn rate ~118/day · two rewards ready, three still locked. Locked is expressed by material in every direction — tarnish, frost, cold wax, a card back, an unfilled mould — never by dimming a row.

01 · THE ASSAY OFFICE
The idea
The furnace header already says the boats are burning. This says the fire made something. XP is stock, privileges are finished goods, rewards are work-in-progress.
Locked, expressed as material
An unfilled mould. The fill line is literally the progress bar, but it reads as a physical level of metal, not a UI element. No greyed-out rows anywhere.
Privileges as objects
Trapezoid ingots with stamped faces, plus a visibly empty third mould — the empty slot is the invitation, and it is the only "you don't have this" moment on screen.
Risk
Most ornamented of the seven. If the ember treatment creeps up, the tab starts shouting the same note as the Today header and the contrast between screens is lost.
Cheapest to integrate — it reuses the furnace gradient stack and the existing .card geometry. Roughly an afternoon.
02

The Ledger

No boxes · no icons · ink weight is the only state

The opposite pole, on purpose. Not a single card, chip or button fill — only rules, numerals and the weight of the ink. Locked rewards are not dimmed, they are set in a lighter ink, the way a private bank prints things you have not drawn against yet. This is the direction that reads "expensive" instead of "game".

02 · THE LEDGER
The idea
Restraint is the reward. Everything else in the app is austere; the surprise here is that the fun tab is the most composed screen he owns, which makes the numbers on it feel real.
Locked, expressed as material
Lighter ink and a smaller numeral. It reads as "not yet printed", not as "you failed" — which matters more here than in any other direction.
What it gives up
Privileges never become objects. They are roman-numbered entries. That is a real loss against his "rewards should be schedule privileges, not points" instinct.
Risk
Could read as cold on a bad day. This is the one direction where the tab does not obviously look like fun — and fun was the brief.
Almost free — it is the existing .stat row with the boxes removed. Half a day.

03 · THE TOKEN TRAY · tap a token
The idea
Two currencies, two materials. Brass plates are money; tokens are freedom. You can see at a glance that he is holding two of them without reading a word.
Locked, expressed as material
Tarnish. A locked reward is the same brass plate, gone dull. Nothing is hidden and nothing is crossed out — it just hasn't been polished yet.
The detail worth stealing
The two empty wells. An empty well is a want, not a failure — it is the only honest way this app is allowed to show absence.
Risk
Oxblood velvet is a hard left turn from the app's violet-black. It is the one direction that changes the palette rather than extending it. Would need a cooler velvet to sit inside the app.
Medium. Knurled edge and velvet are pure CSS, but the palette shift needs a decision.

04 · THE VITRINE
The idea
You do not buy a reward, you acquire it. Museum language raises the stakes of a golf day without inflating the price.
Locked, expressed as material
Real frosted glass (a live backdrop blur), with the wait etched into the frost. This is the most literal answer to "earned vs locked by material" in the set.
The detail worth stealing
The brass placard carrying his own condition under every case. His sentence becomes the museum caption — which is exactly the dignity that sentence deserves.
Risk
Cold blue is the rival's colour in this app. Glass leans cold, so the palette must be pushed warm or the Bank starts feeling like the ghost.
Medium. backdrop-filter is fine on iOS Safari 16+, but needs a fallback tint for the frosted state, which is not optional here.

05 · THE TEAR-OFF BOOK · tap a stub to tear
The idea
A ticket is the only object in normal life that means "this entitles you to something, and it cost you." Tearing it is the claim. Nothing has to be explained.
Locked, expressed as material
Uncut, unbleached stock — a duller khaki paper still in the block. It looks like it exists and is simply not yet issued.
The detail worth stealing
His condition printed as the fine print along the bottom of the ticket. It is the correct place for a self-imposed term, and it reads as binding.
Risk
Cream paper fills a lot of screen with light. On a dark app opened at 6:20 AM this is the direction most likely to be physically bright. Would need the paper knocked back to ~#D8CDB8.
Medium-low. Perforations are two pseudo-element circles; the tear is one transform.

06 · THE WAX CHARTER
The idea
He does not just pick a reward, he writes its terms. Every other direction treats that sentence as metadata. This one treats it as the point, and the reward as the consideration.
Locked, expressed as material
Cold grey wax. The decree still exists in full, terms readable — it simply has not been sealed yet. Nothing is withheld from him.
The detail worth stealing
"Break the seal" as the claim verb. Breaking is irreversible, which is emotionally correct for spending 2,000 XP, and it is what the strongest ceremony treatment is built on.
Risk
Deep red is the app's failure colour. Wax red must sit clearly warmer and darker than --blood or the reward tab starts signalling danger. The seals here are pulled toward oxblood for exactly that reason.
Medium. Seals are irregular border-radius blobs, no images. The cracking animation is the expensive part and is already prototyped in 08.

Hand-off · Agent 6 · Bank aesthetics

What I produced, what I'd ship, what I threw out

What this is and where

renditions/rend-bank.html — seven fully-rendered phone mock-ups of the complete Bank tab at 390px, plus three playable treatments of the claim ceremony. Every direction carries all four elements (XP balance · privileges · boosts · rewards) with identical data and identical conditions; nothing was dropped to make a screen look better. I did not touch index.html or any other file.

Ranked

1 · 06 The Wax Charter. It is the only direction that understood which element matters most. His conditions are the emotional core of this tab — "cooked + laundry every night this week" is a promise he made to himself — and every other direction files that sentence as caption text. This one makes it the contract. "Break the seal" is also the best claim verb in the set, and it gives the ceremony its shape.

2 · 01 The Assay Office. Best system fit and the cheapest to build. It extends the furnace rather than inventing a new world, and the filling mould kills the "~N days away" text problem outright: he reads distance as a level of metal, in half a second, with no arithmetic. If the answer has to be safe, this is the safe answer that is still beautiful.

3 · 03 The Token Tray. Wins on one specific thing nothing else gets right: privileges as objects with mass, and — more importantly — two visibly empty wells. That is the only honest way this app is allowed to show absence, because an empty well reads as a want rather than a failure. It is the single best answer to the wall-of-failure law inside the Bank.

The three details I would combine

  1. From 06 — the condition as the contract. His own sentence set large, under a "THE TERMS YOU WROTE" label, with the reward name above it. Non-negotiable in whatever ships.
  2. From 01 — the filling mould as the locked state. Replaces "~4 DAYS AWAY" as a text line with a physical fill level. Keep the number, but let the material carry it.
  3. From 03 — the empty wells. Two held tokens and two visibly empty slots, at the top of the tab where the balance currently sits. Privileges outrank XP visually, because he said they outrank it in fact.

The composite: warm parchment-dark charter cards for rewards, each carrying his terms and a wax seal whose colour is the ready/not-ready state; the seal's inner fill doubles as the mould fill for locked ones; the top of the tab is a token tray with two occupied and two empty wells, and the XP number is demoted to a plate above it. Ceremony: B, the breaking of the seal, with C's restraint on the closing line ("take it without guilt") grafted on.

Ceremony recommendation

B (The Breaking of the Seal), ahead of C by a nose. B earns its length by repeating his own condition back to him as evidence he met it — which is the one thing a toast can never do. C (The Withdrawal) is the better choice if the GM wants a single ceremony that works for every reward with zero art direction per item, and it is a third of the code. A (The Pour) is the most spectacular and the one I would cut: it is the longest, it is the most likely to feel long on the fifth viewing, and it borrows the furnace's visual language at the exact moment the app should feel like the opposite of urgency.

What I deliberately rejected

  • A shop / storefront grid. First idea, and wrong: it makes his XP feel like currency he is spending in someone else's store. Everything here he already owns.
  • A progress ring per reward. Six rings on one screen is a dashboard, and it re-introduces the "look how far you are from everything" wall in a friendlier font.
  • Confetti / burst-of-coins on claim. Cheap, and it reads as a mobile game paying out. He said expensive, not gamified. The pour and the seal both spend their motion budget on weight instead.
  • A "streak multiplier" on the balance. Not my turf, and it would quietly turn the fun tab into another place he can be behind.
  • Locked rewards hidden or blurred out entirely. Considered for the Vitrine; rejected because hiding the thing he is working toward removes the only reason to open the tab on a bad day.

For the GM — things I found that weren't mine to fix

  • The Bank has no spend history. Once claim() runs, r.done=1 and the reward is gone from the list forever. There is no record of "you took a day off on 14 Sep because you went 85% three weeks running" — which is the exact kind of evidence this app exists to give him. A claimed reward should become a permanent stamped entry, not vanish. Several directions here have an obvious home for it (spent ingots, torn stubs, broken seals).
  • Rewards are one-shot. claim() marks them done permanently, so "nice restaurant date" can be earned exactly once in 91 days. That is almost certainly not what he meant. Repeatable rewards need a decision from him — but the shipped code makes it for him, silently.
  • The privilege count is a bare integer. S.privileges is a number, and the two privilege types in renderBank() are hard-coded labels that both light up "AVAILABLE" off the same counter. There is no way to spend one, and no record of spending it. Every direction here draws privileges as distinct objects — that needs the data model to hold typed tokens, not a tally. Worth reconciling with whatever Agent 1 specs.
  • "~N days away" divides by lifetime average XP (dailyXp() = total XP ÷ days recorded), so early in the run it will swing wildly, and it will drift upward and make everything look closer as good days accumulate. A trailing 7-day rate would be honest. I used ~118/day flat in all mock-ups.
  • Boost XP and privilege both fire from one tap with no confirm, and logBoost awards a privilege every time. Four taps on "Read tonight" is four privileges. Not an aesthetic problem, but it will devalue the token the moment he notices.

Honesty

Seven directions, not ten — all seven are materially distinct (iron, ink, brass, glass, paper, wax, card stock) and I would rather hand over seven that survive a cold look than pad to ten. The weakest of the seven is 02 The Ledger: it is genuinely beautiful and genuinely wrong for the brief, because he asked for the one tab that is allowed to be fun and this one refuses to be. It is included because it is the only direction that could survive being wrong about that — and because its typography should probably be lifted into whichever direction wins.

Two weaknesses I did not design away, because they are real properties of those directions rather than bugs: 07 The Spread pins the privileges to the bottom of the viewport, which leaves a large band of empty table on a 844px phone — I filled it with a real line of information rather than pretend it isn't there, but on a shorter phone it will fight the tab bar. And 04 The Vitrine's frost has a narrow usable range: past about 2px of blur the reward name stops being legible, which breaks the "all four elements legible" rule. It is set at 1.4px here and should not be pushed.

Verification

arsenal-lint: clean ✓. Rendered headlessly in Chrome and screenshotted every phone at 390×844 (top and scrolled), plus the three ceremonies mid-animation and at rest. No console errors, no failed requests, no horizontal overflow on the page or inside any phone (the one horizontal scroller in 07 is the intended card carousel). All three families confirmed loaded and applied, not falling back: Clash Display 500/600/700, Satoshi 400/500/700, Geist Mono 400/500.

Five real defects were found by looking at the screenshots rather than at the CSS, and fixed: condition text rendering inline after the reward title in 02 and 03 (inline spans styled as blocks) which also caused a 140px horizontal overflow; a class-name collision where the price chip's .p inherited the 390px phone-frame width; 04's frost blurred at 3.5px, which made three reward names unreadable; 06's wax sitting on top of the app's failure red, so the reward tab read as an alert — pulled to oxblood with a gold-on-dark claim button; and 07's hand cards mispositioned because :nth-of-type was counting the label div, leaving one card unstyled and both clipped by the frame. In the ceremonies, A's spout poured into empty space (the mould was in flow, the spout absolutely positioned), B's crack line outlived the seal that made it, and C's balance sat off-centre because the faded debit still held its place in the flex row.
