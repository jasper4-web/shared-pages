DIRECTION E

The Notebook

Today is one page you write on. The blocks are the headings; your notes are the page.

This takes the only thing you liked and makes it the whole architecture. The notes aren't behind anything — there is no back to flip to, because the writing is the surface. Each block is a small heading with a time; underneath it is empty ruled space that becomes what you wrote. A day you worked hard is a full page. A day you didn't is a short one — not a red one.

Wins are ticks in the margin, the narrow strip down the right edge. Literally the "small little side section" you described. You never visit them; you flick them as you pass.

DIRECTION F

One Thing

The screen shows the block you are in. That's the entire page. Wins don't exist until the day closes.

The most extreme answer to "things feel thrown on": there is nothing to throw anything onto. No list, no rail, no tray, no strip. Open the app and it tells you what you are doing right now, in type you can read from across the room. The whole day is behind one door you have to choose to open.

Tap the block and it turns over — that's the flip you liked, applied to the whole screen instead of a card. Wins appear once, at 21:30, on the closing screen. During the day they are not on your phone at all.

DIRECTION G

The Log

The day writes to you and you write back. Only what already happened is on screen.

A thread, like a conversation. When a block ends it posts itself — "Block 1 finished. 90 minutes clean." Your notes are a reply underneath it, with your last reply to the same block quoted at the bottom. Nothing about the future is on screen except one line: what's next.

This is the honest version of "a by-product of a good day": wins post themselves into the thread at the hour they happen and then scroll away upward. They aren't a checklist you visit — they're things that appeared because they occurred. At the end of the day the thread is the record of the day.

DIRECTION H

The Ledger

Today is an account, not a schedule. One number at the top, and every line is a credit.

The economy is the part you said doesn't bend — so here it's the interface. The top of the page is what today has earned. Under it, every line that paid: a clean block is +65, cooking dinner is +20. They sit in the same column, and that is precisely what makes wins minor — you can see they're rounding. No argument needed, the arithmetic says it.

There are no debits. Nothing on this page is ever negative or red. A block you missed simply never posted a line. Notes are the memo on a line — tap a line, write on it.

DIRECTION I

The Two-Sided Day

Your one liked idea, promoted: the whole Today page has a front and a back. Front is doing. Back is writing.

Front: the live block and the seam either side of it. That's all — no wins, no tray, no extras. Everything that isn't "what am I doing right now" moved to the back. One flip, not one per card.

The back is today's sheet: every block's notes as one continuous page you can read top to bottom, which means at 21:30 you already have the day written up. Wins are tucked at the foot of the back — off the doing surface entirely, but not exiled to a different tab.

My read, since you'll ask. E · The Notebook is the one I'd build. It takes the only thing you liked and makes it the architecture instead of a feature, it puts wins exactly where your own instinct put them ("a small little side section"), and it's the only option where a hard day produces something worth keeping — 110 written pages by December 25 rather than 110 percentages. It also can't go red, which matters more than anything else on this page.
The one to be careful of is H · The Ledger. It's the most immediately impressive and it answers your complaint with arithmetic — but it turns your day into a scoreboard, and you have said the number must never become the point. I drew it because it's genuinely different and it solves the wins problem cleanly. I don't think you should ship it.
If you want the cheap win instead: I · Two-Sided is a week of work, not three, and it kills the wedged-in feeling immediately. It's the least exciting and the most likely to just be right.
