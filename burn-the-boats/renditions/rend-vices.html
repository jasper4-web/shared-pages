The vice screens

Tapping a vice today opens a flat sheet with two fields and a button. It records. It does not reward, and it dead-ends. Six directions for turning that tap into a destination: the streak shown grandly, the dial he tapped brought inside at full size, reasons that reconnect the vice to December, and an optional challenge system that makes each vice its own small game.

Every screen below is a real rendered mock-up, not a description. Sample state is held identical across all six so they can be compared fairly: smoke — 6 days clean, best 11, 2 slips this run, 24h cleared twice · the dishwasher — 4 weeks at or under, best 4, 0 of 1 used this week.

“Whenever I tap the dishwasher and smoke… I want the screen to pop up to show my streak a little bit more grand. You could even just bring that same image that you already have… And then maybe there can be more stuff, like reasons why I should do this. It just feels a little bit dead end right now.” — his brief

The challenge system

designed as a mechanic · every direction below draws the same machine

A challenge is a standing offer, not an assignment. The app offers; he accepts or ignores; it tracks itself; he claims. The single most important property is at the end: lapsing costs nothing and is never mentioned again. A challenge that can punish you is just another obligation, and obligations are what he stops opening the app to avoid.

1 · Offered
Hold until 2 PM → +35 XP
Surfaced only when it is still winnable from right now. Two taps available: accept, or not today — which dismisses it silently and records nothing.
2 · Live
Holding until 14:00 · 4h 18m
Tracks itself against the clock and the log. Shows on the vice screen, and as a second hairline on its dial in the gauge strip so it is visible without opening anything.
3 · Claimable
You held it. +35 XP
He taps to claim — the tap is the reward moment, same reasoning as the phone-pass tap. Anything unclaimed auto-credits at the evening close-out. Earned XP is never lost.
4 · Lapsed
Offer expired. Nothing lost.
No XP, no HP, no streak effect, no counter, no red. Shown once, then gone. It quietly returns to the offer pool on the next eligible day.
  • One live challenge per vice. Depth without overwhelm — you cannot stack four timers.
  • Only offered when winnable. Never “hold until 2 PM” at 3 PM. Never the week challenge on Thursday.
  • Accepting is the commitment. That deliberate tap is what makes it a game rather than a passive bonus.
  • Claiming is a tap, but forgetting is free. Unclaimed XP credits itself at close-out.
  • Lapses are never counted. No “failed 3 times” anywhere in the app. This is the nagging guard.
  • Escalation is a rank, not a moving goalpost. The ladder is visible from day one, so 24h → 32h reads as a promotion he unlocked.
  • A slip during a live challenge ends the challenge only. The slip is handled by the existing make-up mechanic; the challenge just lapses. It never doubles the penalty.

So the daily 2 PM hold at 35 is half a focus run — worth doing, not worth gaming. A 24h clean at 120 is “pays big” without being a shortcut past the work. A zero week at 250 is the largest recurring award in the app, and it should be: a zero week is literally the December behaviour. Ceiling check — even a perfect week only adds ~600 XP from challenges, against roughly 2,000 from blocks.

01

The Chain

the streak as a forged object · repairs stay visible

The streak stops being a number and becomes a thing he has made. One link per week (or per day). The point is the welded link: when he slips, the chain does not break — a link is repaired in front of him and the repair stays there forever. That is his own mercy-with-a-price mechanic rendered as an object instead of explained as a sentence. It is also the history view; there is no second list of failures, because the chain already tells the whole truth without a single red mark.

What dominates
The streak object. Everything else is arranged beneath it.
The dial
Brought inside at 176px — the identical arc, value and engraved label he tapped, roughly four times the size. It sits directly under the headline so the tap feels like a zoom, not a jump.
History
The chain is the history. No separate list, no wall of anything — a slip is one amber link with a seam, permanently visible and permanently attached.
Challenge shape
“Forge an extra link.” The reward is literal: a link appears that the calendar did not give him.
Risk
Links get small past ~14. Needs to collapse to “… + 9 earlier” with a scrollable full chain. Design that before shipping, not after.
Strongest single idea in the set: it renders the one mechanic that is entirely his own, and it renders it as mercy rather than as damage.
02

The Gate Clock

the whole day as one ring · noon at the crown

Both rules are fundamentally about time — one is a clock rule, one is a calendar rule — so time becomes the screen. The waking day is drawn as a 270° arc with noon at the top: the gate is literally the crown of the day, and every hour before it is a climb. His danger windows are shaded on the ring itself, and challenges are flags planted further around the arc — 2 PM, 5 PM, the 24-hour wrap. Accepting one plants the flag; the ember arc grows toward it in real time.

What dominates
Time. The ring answers “where am I in this rule right now?” before he has read a word.
The dial
Handled two ways here on purpose. On 2a the ring is the enlarged dial — same ember arc, same geometry, grown to fill the screen. On 2b the literal small dial is also placed inline at 104px so you can judge whether he needs the exact object or a faithful enlargement of it.
Danger windows
The three shaded bands come straight from his interview: the first 90 minutes, the midday break, and late night. Naming them on the ring is information, not moralising — and it is the only screen in the set that shows him when he is about to be tested.
History
Deliberately absent from this screen. Time-now is the job; the record lives one tap deeper. Flag as a real gap if the GM wants everything on one page.
Risk
The most engineering of the six — the ring has to be right at every hour of the day, including 06:00 and 22:00, or it will look broken. And it hard-codes his waking day at 05:00–23:00, which PROFILE §8c lists as unknown. Confirm before building.
Best answer to “what do I do in the next hour?” Weakest answer to “how has this gone?”
03

The Ledger

an account, contracts, and a balance that only grows

Cold, engraved, mono — a private account book. The streak is a balance. Challenges are contracts you sign, with a term and a payout printed like terms. And the reason this direction earns its place: the ledger is the only history format where a slip can be recorded with total honesty and still not read as failure — it posts as an entry marked PAID, because he did in fact pay it. Nothing is hidden, nothing is red, and the balance never goes down.

What dominates
The record. This is the only direction where history is the main event rather than a footer.
The dial
Set as an embossed plate beside the balance at 112px — smaller than 01 or 05 on purpose, because here it is a seal on a document rather than the hero.
The honest-history problem, solved
Week 13 went over. It is right there in the ledger, in sequence, with the streak visibly resetting to 0 in the last column — and it still does not read as a wall, because the entry says made up and the credit column says PAID. Honesty and warmth in the same row.
Challenge shape
Contracts with printed terms, and one rule made visible: the on-ramp and the full week cannot both run, which is how “one live challenge per vice” is expressed in this language.
Risk
Coldest of the six. A ledger is an accountant’s object and this is supposed to be the most emotional screen in the app. It suits the dishwasher’s weekly rhythm far better than smoking’s daily one.
Take the ledger component even if the direction loses. It is the best history view here.
04

The Quest Board

challenges first · the streak becomes the banner

He asked for the vices to be “their own little game.” This is the direction that takes him at his word: challenges dominate, the streak shrinks to a banner, and all four states of the mechanic are visible at once — one live, one claimable, one offered, one locked with its unlock progress showing. History is reframed entirely as a trophy shelf: things won, never things missed. It is the most fun screen and the one furthest from the app’s restraint.

What dominates
The game. Everything is a card that pays.
The dial
Only 118px and pushed to the banner — the weakest treatment of the dial in the set, and an honest strike against this direction given he explicitly asked for the image to come with him.
History
Trophies. Note the fourth medal: 2 made up. Counting his redemptions as a medal rather than hiding them is the most warmth this format can carry, and it stays truthful.
Risk — the real one
Four cards is four things asking for a decision. Two of these should be collapsed by default; a board that always has something to accept is a board that eventually feels like nagging. This is the direction most at risk of it.
If he says “I want it to feel like a game,” this wins outright. If he says “I want it to feel true,” it loses to 01.
05

The Testimony

almost nothing · one sentence at 60px

The opposite bet. Grandeur through subtraction: the streak is a full sentence in display type, there is a great deal of black, and the only interactive thing on the screen is a single quiet offer. Scripture is given real room instead of being a chip. This is the only direction where the screen would feel reverent — which matters, because the make-up he designed for himself is scriptural, and because a vice screen that shouts is a vice screen he will learn to avoid opening.

What dominates
White space and one sentence. It is the only screen here that would survive being looked at every single day for 91 days without wearing out.
The dial
204px — the largest in the set, and stripped of its engraved label because at that size the arc speaks for itself. Closest to a literal answer to “bring that same image.”
History
The scatter under the fold is the most honest chart in the whole set: each day is a dot placed at the time of first use against the noon line. Clean days sit on the top rail. A day he broke the gate sits low — but you can see it was 11:40, not 7 AM. That is progress a tick-box can never show, and it renders the bad days in ember, never red.
Risk
Genuinely thin. One offer visible means the 24-hour challenge has nowhere to live without a second screen. And restraint can read as an empty screen on the day he most needs something to grab.
Its history chart and its dial size should be lifted into whichever direction wins.
06

The Furnace Chamber

the locked header, continued downward · the streak is fuel

The one direction that treats the vice screen as a room inside the furnace he already owns. Weeks kept are coals in the bed; the fire is literally bigger because he held the line. Real embers (tsParticles, not a canvas loop) drift up behind the type. Challenges are “stoke it.” The argument for this direction is continuity: the header is locked and beloved, and this is the only screen that feels like it was cut from the same block rather than bolted on.

What dominates
Atmosphere. It is the only screen with a mood rather than a layout.
The dial
168px, sitting in the glow like the gauge on a furnace door. Reads as the same object, in its natural habitat.
History
The coal bed. Week 13 is ash with an ember still in it — over, made up, still part of the fire. Same truth as the ledger, told with heat instead of numbers.
Risk
Two real ones. Particles on a screen he opens several times a day is a battery cost on an installed PWA — cap it and pause on visibilitychange. And the coal bed is charming at 8 weeks and unreadable at 19; it needs to compress into a single wider bar.
Most likely of the six to be loved on first open and skimmed by week three.

Hand-off — agent 5

What I produced and where

This file — renditions/rend-vices.html — is the whole deliverable. Six full-screen vice treatments rendered as working phone mock-ups, plus a designed challenge mechanic with a live state demo, a proposed XP table, and two reusable components (the dial enlarger and the honest history chart). No other file was touched. index.html was not modified.

Ranked recommendation

  1. 01 · The Chain — build this. It is the only direction where the streak, the history and the slip mechanic are the same object. That collapses three of his five asks into one visual, which is how you get a grand screen that still reads in under a minute. Crucially it renders the mechanic he invented — never wiped, paid for instead — as a welded link he can see forever, which is warmer and more honest than any sentence explaining it. It also works identically for both vices with only the unit changing, so it is one component, not two screens.
  2. 02 · The Gate Clock — build the smoke half of it. Different question, also worth answering: “what do I do in the next hour?” Its danger-window shading is the only place in the app that would warn him before the hard moment. My recommendation is to ship 01 as the vice screen and fold the ring into it as the smoke-only top slot, rather than treating them as rivals.
  3. 03 · The Ledger — steal the ledger, drop the direction. Too cold to be the emotional centre, but its PAID row is the single best solution in this whole set to “show history honestly without a wall of red.” Put that row format inside 01’s expanded chain view.
  4. 04 · The Quest Board. The most fun and the most dangerous. Four simultaneous offers is the nagging failure mode wearing a costume. Worth showing him, because if he lights up at it we should know — but ship it with at most two live cards.
  5. 05 · The Testimony. Weak as a whole screen — genuinely thin, and the 24h challenge has nowhere to live on it. Strong in two parts that should be lifted regardless of the winner: the 204px dial (the most literal answer to what he asked for) and the time-of-first-use scatter, which is the best honest-history chart I built.
  6. 06 · The Furnace Chamber. Beautiful, on-brand, and the one I trust least over 91 days. Battery cost on a PWA he opens all day, and a coal bed that stops being readable around week 12.

Proposed final XP values

Smoke — hold until 14:00 +35 · hold until 17:00 +60 · 24 hours clean +120 · 32 hours clean +180 · 72 hours +400 (rare).
Dishwasher — hold to Thursday +90 · a week at zero +250.
Anchored to what already exists (focus run 65, small win 15–30, clean vice day 40, make-up cleared 60). A perfect week adds roughly 600 XP from challenges against roughly 2,000 from blocks, so challenges are meaningful without becoming the cheapest path to a number.

Where this risks feeling like nagging — read this before integrating

1 · Never show more than two offers at once. Direction 04 shows four to prove the states exist; that is a spec drawing, not a shipping default. Every additional visible offer is another unmade decision sitting on his screen.

2 · Never count lapses. Anywhere. No “offers missed”, no faded ghost of an expired challenge, no weekly summary line about it. The moment a lapse leaves a trace, an optional challenge has become an obligation with extra steps, and the whole mechanic inverts into the wall of failure.

3 · Do not push-notify a challenge offer. An offer he finds when he opens the app is a game. The same offer arriving unasked at 8 AM is his phone telling him not to smoke. He named notifications as something he wants for the schedule — do not extend that permission to the vices without asking him.

4 · The reason lines must rotate and stay factual. Every “reason why” I wrote is either his own words, a fact from his own interview (the 90-minute window is the trading window; 9–noon is his peak brain), or scripture presented without commentary. Nothing anywhere says a vice is bad. The instant this copy starts moralising, the screen becomes something he avoids opening.

5 · One live challenge per vice, enforced in the engine. Not a UI convention — a rule, or he will end up with three timers and a screen that feels like a job.

Things I found that were not mine to fix

  • The gauge arc overstates its own number. In dial() (index.html ~line 928) the dasharray is Math.PI*21 but the arc it is drawn on has radius 19, so its true length is Math.PI*19 ≈ 59.7 while the dash pattern is ≈ 66. At 50% the arc renders about 55% full. Same class of bug as the gauge the crew caught before. My enlarged dials use the corrected math, which is why a “4w / 8 weeks” dial here looks slightly emptier than the one in the shipped strip. Fix is one constant.
  • viceClean('smoke') can be tapped before noon. openVice offers “Clean day · +40 XP” at any hour, so a clean day can be banked at 9 AM and the gate broken at 11. The button should only appear after the gate has actually held — or the credit should settle at close-out.
  • The dishwasher dial and the smoke dial use different denominators (streak/8 weeks vs streak/7 days) while sitting side by side in the same strip at the same size. Two adjacent dials that look alike but measure differently is a small honesty problem worth a decision.
  • Slips are counted forever and shown as a subtitle. SLIPS THIS RUN · 2 is the one place in the current vice sheet that is a pure running tally of failure, sitting directly under the vice’s name. Every direction above deliberately re-frames it (“welded”, “made up”, “PAID”) — worth doing in the shipped sheet even if none of these directions wins.
  • The vendored tsParticles bundle paints nothing unless you register it first. Anyone else on this crew reaching for lib/tsparticles/tsparticles.slim.bundle.min.js will hit this: calling tsParticles.load(...) straight away succeeds, the container reports the full particle count, the canvas is created and sized — and the canvas stays completely blank, because the shape drawers are never registered. You must await loadSlim(tsParticles) first. I only caught it by sampling canvas pixels; a screenshot of a dark screen looks the same either way.
  • Second tsParticles trap: number.density scales against a 1920×1080 reference, so on a 372px-wide phone canvas a requested 46 particles silently became 5. Set density:{enable:false} and the value is literal.
  • Renditions live one level down. This file references the shared libraries as ../lib/… and ../assets/…. If the GM moves it, the fonts and grain go with it.

Honesty note

Six directions, and I would only defend five of them as real candidates — 05 · The Testimony is weak as a full screen and I have said so above rather than padding it into a contender. It is here because two of its components are the best in the set and should be harvested. Directions 01, 02, 03, 04 and 06 are genuinely different arguments about what a vice screen is for: an object you have made, a clock you are beating, a record you are keeping, a game you are playing, and a fire you are feeding.
