THE TODAY PAGE · FOUR DIRECTIONS · NOTHING BUILT

Four different shapes,
not four skins

These are genuinely different architectures for the same three problems — not variations on one idea. Each one puts the day, the small wins and the notes somewhere different, and each gives something up.

Every screen is a drawing. Pick a direction (or parts of two) and I'll draw that one properly.

01
Notes on a block. Tap the block you're about to do and write into it — the goal for the end of this block, and whatever else. Saved.
02
Merge the NOW card and the day. One component, not two sections with something wedged between them. A bar you can expand, jump from, and use to make a block "now".
03
"Always counts" and "Something extra" feel thrown on. Merge them, and make them feel like a by-product of a good day rather than a checklist nailed to the side.
DIRECTION A

The Rail

The day is a row of dots. One block is on screen at a time and you scrub through them.

Closest to what you described. There is no list — the rail IS the list, compressed to eleven dots you can read in half a second. Tap any dot and that block's card replaces the one you're looking at. The wins become a small tray directly under the rail, so they read as part of the same instrument rather than a separate section.

DIRECTION B

The Deck

The day is a stack of cards. The live one is face-up; the rest are slivers above and below.

Physical rather than abstract. You can always see what just happened and what's next without leaving the card you're on — the day has a felt shape. Notes live on the back of the card: tap to flip. Wins are a card in the deck too, sitting where they'd naturally fall, so they're literally part of the day rather than an appendix.

DIRECTION C

One Column

There is no NOW card. There is one list, and the current block is simply the row that's open.

The most radical and the simplest. The page contains exactly one object. Nothing is wedged between anything because there's nothing to wedge. The wins appear as rows at the time of day they'd naturally happen — "cooked" sits at dinner, "in bed on time" sits at the end. Notes expand inline under the open row.

DIRECTION D

The Cockpit

Two fixed zones. The live block on top, the whole day as a grid underneath, always visible.

The opposite bet from C: never navigate at all. Everything is on screen at once and nothing expands or collapses. The day is sixteen cells you read like a dashboard — you can see the shape of the whole day without scrolling. The wins are a permanent strip along the bottom. Nothing moves, ever, which is either restful or rigid depending on your taste.

My read, since you'll ask. C answers your complaint most directly — the two sections don't get merged, they stop existing, and the wins-at-the-hour-they-happen idea is the closest thing here to "a by-product of a good day". A is the closest to what you actually described and the fastest to read. D will fight the editable-day work we just agreed to. B is the most delightful and the most expensive.
The obvious hybrid is C's single column with A's rail pinned at the top — the rail for a half-second read of the whole day, the column underneath for everything else. Say the word and I'll draw that one properly.
