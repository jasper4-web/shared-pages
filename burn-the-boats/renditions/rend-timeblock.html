Agent 4 · the time-block region · 12 directions

Six things shouting at the same volume

Everything between the locked graph card and the schedule: the day rail · the deep-work-left readout · the push strip · the make-up banner · NEXT UP · always-counts. Six elements, all stacked, all the same weight. He called it "a lot of jumble" and said small wins feel "in the way."

Every direction below is the same content and the same clock — Wednesday 11:15, inside Block 2, 3h 15m of deep work left, Anthony pitch-ready and not sent. Only the structure changes. All twelve are live mock-ups at 390px, not pictures. Heights are measured by the page itself at load, not estimated.

Measured on the shipped build, not guessed. NEXT UP ended at y = 838 — 58px underneath the tab bar — and that was with a short life-block card. Rendered with a deep-work card, as in 00 below, it ends at y = 860. The single most important element on the screen is clipped today, before a single design change. The brief's "~206px already spent" is the furnace plus gauges only; the glance card adds another 270 and it is what actually breaks the budget.

So the real test is not total height — it is where NEXT UP ends. Every mock-up below draws a red dashed fold line at +299px from the top of the region. Anything crossing it is a design he has to scroll to reach on the one screen he opens twenty times a day.

And 299 is the generous number — it is headless Chrome, where the safe-area insets are zero. On his actual installed iPhone they aren't:

Headless / simulator — the dashed line299 px
Real iPhone, insets applied once (correct)218 px
Real iPhone as shipped today171 px

The gap between 218 and 171 is a bug, not physics: index.html applies var(--st) twice — once on .app (padding-top:calc(6px + var(--st))) and again on its first child .furnace. That is ~47px of dead space at the top of the only screen that matters. Fix it before choosing a layout — it changes which of these clear the fold. Flagged for the GM; the header is locked and not my turf.

My read, after measuring all twelve

Ranked on one question — does the focus-run button sit above the fold on a normal day, without selling out always-counts to get there? Height alone would crown 04; it doesn't win, because it buys its height by taking away the only thing he can do on a bad day.

1st

01 · The Command Line my pick with 12's earn board

The only direction that fixes the actual complaint — six things at equal volume — without hiding anything behind a tap, a swipe or a dock. It works because it is the only one that asks what each element is worth rather than where it goes: NEXT UP gets a card, the day gets a line, the push gets a line, the wins get a strip. Swap its horizontal pill rail for the earn board from 12 and it also keeps the quota pips, which the pill rail loses. Rendered combined above.

One caveat, stated plainly: it clears the simulator fold at +227 and it clears a fixed iPhone at 218 only if the double-inset bug is fixed first — 9px over otherwise. Fixing that bug hands back 47px and puts this comfortably clear. Fix the header padding, then build 01. If the GM won't touch the header, 04 is the only direction that clears today's real 171px budget, and the wins get demoted to dots.

2nd

12 · Now / Next Pair best home for always-counts

Its earn board is the best single component produced by this brief: six real labels with six real quota pip rows in ~140px, and it stops the wins reading as a leftover list by giving them one header and a border. The 2-up hero is the weaker half — 40% of the most valuable row goes to whatever comes next, which is Lunch more often than it is anything worth 40%. Take the board, leave the pair.

3rd

10 · The Dock the boldest idea here

Structurally the cleanest answer to "give it a better home", and the only one where he can log a win while looking at something else. It ranks third only because it isn't really a layout — it's an app-shell change that costs every screen 40px, so it's the GM's call and not mine. If the GM wants it, it composes with 01 perfectly: 01 minus its win rail, plus this dock.

4th

04 · The Dial wins on every number, loses on the law

Smallest region in the set and the only one where the schedule clears the fold. It is genuinely beautiful. It also reduces always-counts to six dots, and rule 4 of the brief is that he must be able to earn on a bad day. If the GM disagrees with me about how load-bearing the wins are, this is the layout to pick.

5th

07 · Wins on the Rail the best idea I couldn't make work

Conceptually the sharpest thing in this document: wins as part of the day rather than a list under it. It fails on physics — six 13px targets on a 364px track, no room for quota pips, and pinning "said no" to a time of day is a fiction. Worth keeping in the drawer for a bigger screen or four notches instead of six.

6–8

02 Fuel Gauge · 09 One Object · 08 Ledger

02 produces the most on-theme component in the set (the tank) but leaves the bottom half of the region untouched, so it doesn't actually solve the jumble — I kept it because the GM should steal that component regardless of which layout wins. 09 is the best-looking and the most unified, but welding three stripes onto one card makes a singular object that is internally complicated, and a penance adds a fourth. 08 is the calmest and the densest, and it is the right answer for a different app — it reads as an accounting screen, and he asked to be fired up.

9–12

The four I'd not build weakest

05 Three-Tab Panel and 06 Swipe Deck both fix the height problem by hiding two thirds of the region — on a screen he reads for fifteen seconds, a glanceable rail behind a tap is a deleted rail. They are in the set because "collapse it" is the obvious answer and he deserves to see it rendered before rejecting it; 06 also handles a penance worse than anything else here. 03 Vertical Spine costs 30px of width on a 390px phone to save vertical space the layout can get more cheaply, and nobody reads a day downward. 11 The Sentence is the one I most wanted to work — it is the only layout that sounds like the app's purpose — but a sentence read twenty times a day becomes noise faster than a number does, and it abandons the card language of the locked block directly above it.

What I deliberately rejected

  • Hiding always-counts. The brief forbids it and it's right to: five of the seven weekly quotas are fed by wins, and they are the only thing earnable on a blown day. 04, 05, 06, 08 and 09 all reduce wins to dots or a tap, and every one of them pays for its height with that trade. I rendered them so the trade is visible, not so it gets made by accident.
  • A "smart" region that changes shape by time of day. I sketched one that showed the rail only in the morning and wins only after 18:00. It measures beautifully and it is unacceptable: a layout that moves is a layout he has to re-learn every time he opens it, against a one-minute interaction budget.
  • Pretty near-duplicates. Three early passes were one idea with different padding. They're not in here. Twelve is what survived, and I've said plainly which four I would not build.

Things I found that aren't mine to fix

  • NEXT UP is below the fold in the shipped build, today. It ends at y=838 with the tab bar at y=780 — the focus-run button is under the tab bar before any redesign. This is the finding, not the layouts.
  • The safe-area inset is applied twice. .app has padding-top:calc(6px + var(--st)) and its first child .furnace has padding:calc(20px + var(--st))… — on a notched iPhone that is ~94px of top padding where ~53 was intended. Fixing it hands ~47px straight back to this region and would change which layouts clear the fold. Worth fixing before choosing a layout.
  • The glance card is 256px — bigger than the furnace and the gauges combined, and bigger than any layout in this document. It is locked, so I have not touched it, but the region below it cannot be fixed to everyone's satisfaction while the card above it costs a third of the screen. If Jasper ever unlocks one thing, that's the one with the most px in it.
  • The rail's own logic. renderRail() computes deep-work-left from b.kind==='deep' only, so Bible and gym never count — correct, but it means the number reads "Done" from 16:20 onward every day, which is a strange thing for a region titled around deep work to say for five hours each evening. Worth a look by whoever owns the header copy.
  • Trading still ends at 08:30 in WEEKDAY, which matches PROFILE.md — the brief's note that "the current build has trading ending at 7:15" is stale. Already fixed.

What verification caught in my own work
