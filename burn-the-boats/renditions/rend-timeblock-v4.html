The framework is untouched. The rail, deep-work-left, the push strip, the make-up banner, NEXT UP above the fold and ALWAYS COUNTS are exactly as approved in v3. The only thing that changes is what sits under the block you are in — and it is now different for every kind of block.

The rules the row obeys

He asked for this to be done "in the best clean way" and not sloppy. That is a craft instruction, so it is written as rules a machine can check — and at the bottom of this page every one of them is measured on the rendered output, not asserted.

Six rules

01
Three actions maximum. If a block needs four, the design hasn't decided. Two blocks here have one action. One block has none at all.
02
Every target ≥48px, primaries 56px — above Apple's 44px floor, because this is used one-handed at 6 AM and the thumb is not accurate then.
03
Nothing moves when state changes. Each block reserves a fixed row height; not-started, live, done, missed, waiting and off-script all render at exactly the same height, so the schedule below never jumps under his thumb.
04
One spacing scale — 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24. There are no one-off margins in this file; every gap is one of those five variables.
05
Nothing below 11px inside an action. This app has shipped 6.5px labels. Button labels are 14px, meta is 10px, and that floor is enforced by the audit.
06
Every action maps to something that exists. No button implies a feature the app can't perform today, with one deliberate exception — the sms: deep link for Kells, which is new and is the whole reason that block was named.

Hand-off to the GM

What I produced

renditions/rend-timeblock-v4.html — the approved Line framework with a contextual action row for all fourteen block types across weekday and Saturday, each of the six row states rendered at a frozen height, and a measured craft audit. v3 is untouched, and I have not substituted the X1 fusion: this is The Line on its own, as chosen.

The Bible block, since he named it

Two buttons. "Read · 20 min locked" starts the same fullscreen locked timer a work block gets — the app already has focus:20 on that block, so this is wiring, not new machinery. Then "Text Kells", an sms: deep link that opens Messages with the passage already in the body.

The reasoning: the reading is not what he misses — the texting is. Reading is solitary and takes twenty minutes; texting a real person about what you read requires composing something, and that is where a 3×/week habit dies. Pre-filling the message removes the only step that involves another human being. The button is deliberately live before the timer too — if he read earlier in the day, the friction to text should still be zero.

The two action sets I am least sure about, and what would settle them

1 · Market open — "Done for the day". I chose the anti-double-back guard over a session log, because his own definition of a won month is "stuck to the rules and did not double-back", and the double-back happens after a winning morning. A button that declares the session closed is the only thing on this screen that could actually interrupt that. But I have never seen him trade, and there is a real chance that tapping "done" at 07:40 and then legitimately re-entering on a valid setup would make the app feel like a liar — or worse, make him not tap it. What would settle it: one question — "when you double-back, is it a new idea or is it revenge on the same one?" If it is revenge, the guard is right. If it is a fresh setup, the guard is wrong and the block should only carry the close-of-session log at 13:00.

2 · Personal life — no actions at all. The brief says do not gamify it, so I gave it zero buttons and one line of text. That is either exactly right or it will read as the app having given up on the block that he named a top priority three times. The difference is invisible to me from here. What would settle it: show him the card with no buttons and ask a single question — "does this feel like respect or like neglect?" If it is neglect, the fix is one ghost button that is about presence rather than scoring ("Phone away until 21:00"), and nothing else.

Decisions inside these sets that the GM should know are deliberate

  • No smoke button on Wake. The obvious move is to put the vice log on the block that sits inside the hardest 90 minutes. I refused: naming the vice at the moment of peak craving is a prompt, not a defence. It belongs on the vices screen, which is Agent 5's turf.
  • No "mark complete" on deep work. The app's own rule is that only the timer completes a work block. Adding a manual complete would quietly destroy the one measure the score is built on.
  • No dollar figure anywhere near trading. Both trading blocks carry rule-following only, which is what setTrade() already stores.
  • No food logging on Lunch. Nutrition is explicitly out of the app; the block gets one button that means "I ate and I'm back", nothing more.
  • Missed is never terminal. The missed state carries two inline recoveries — move it to tomorrow, or it wasn't missed after all. The third path in markMiss(), "it was blocked", is not a button here because it is its own state (Waiting), one card along.

What verification caught in my own work

Six defects, found by measuring and looking rather than by reading my own code. The first one is the important one, because it is a consequence of this round's brief that I did not anticipate:

  • Richer actions cost fold budget, and broke the locked constraint. A three-action row is ~69px taller than v3's single button, which pushed the train card's bottom to +336 and the Bible card's to +374 against a 299 budget. My own audit failed the page and said so. Fixed structurally, not by shrinking type: the weekly quota moved into the kicker line (a whole row removed), and the collapse pill no longer duplicates the last past station. Now 291 and 295. The GM should know this trade exists — every action added to this row costs roughly 56px of fold.
  • The off-script note made that card 20px taller than the other five, quietly breaking rule 03 at the card level even though the action row itself was identical. Off-script is a day-level annotation and already has its own banner in v3, so it came out of the card. All six cards now measure 205px exactly.
  • "Text Kells" rendered as an underlined blue-ish link — it is the one action that is an <a> rather than a <button>, and it did not inherit the reset.
  • Bold text inside the rationale paragraphs was rendering as display headings, snapping sentences into fragments mid-flow. A selector meant for the card title was matching every <b> in the caption.
  • The quota strip had its own row: 27px spent on metadata that reads better as a chip beside the kicker.
  • I claimed the missed state offered three recovery actions; it renders two. The third path is a separate state, and the text now says so.

What I could not verify from here

The sms: body format differs across iOS versions — sms:&body= works on modern iOS but has been inconsistent historically, and it does nothing at all in a desktop browser. It needs one test on his actual phone from the installed PWA before this is called done. If it fails, the fallback is copy-to-clipboard plus opening Messages, which is worse but still removes the composing step.
