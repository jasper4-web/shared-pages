01

The screen

both vices · the shipping layout

The masthead in its ordinary, healthy state, for each vice. The layout is identical; only the rule changes. Everything fits on one screen without scrolling — the whole reason this composition won — and both frames measure under the 844px viewport.

What is on the screen
Masthead (streak + unit) · the ring · today’s fact in one line · the modules · the graph · one reason. Six things, in that order, every time.
The ring, at 128px
Round 2 drew it at 112. Rendered and looked at, the gate tick and the now-hand were under 2px and illegible. 128px is the smallest size where every mark on the ring survives; the masthead still clears 196px for type, which fits the longest streak the app can produce.
Today’s line
New in this round. Without it the screen never states what happened today — the single most important fact on it. Ember when the rule is live, green when it has been kept, plain grey when it was broken.
Both rules, one layout
The ring reads a day or a week. The graph plots a time against noon, or a count against the allowance. The streak counts days or weeks. He learns the objects once.
Measured on the render: 602px and 609px of an 844px viewport. Neither vice scrolls, and the tallest state in the whole set — a second-level make-up, which carries the strip and an open offer — still lands at 808px, inside the fold.
02

Entry, and the one motion moment

the gauge you tapped opens into the instrument

He arrives here by tapping a dial in the locked gauge strip. That dial is a 180° arc; the masthead ring is a 270° arc. So the transition is not a fade — the gauge unrolls. The arc he tapped stays almost exactly where it is on the glass, grows 2.8×, and opens the last 90° into the ring’s track; only then does the day’s ember arc sweep in behind it and the masthead type rise. It barely travels — 59px right, 4px down — and that is the point: the small instrument becomes the big one, in place.

The choreography
0–300ms the strip screen falls back and dims · 60–620ms the arc grows 2.8× and unrolls from 180° to 270°, sliding 59px right into the masthead’s ring slot · 380ms the ember day-arc fades in behind it · 500ms the flying arc hands over and disappears · 420ms the masthead figure rises 14px and fades up · 520ms+ the modules, graph and reason stagger in 60ms apart. Total ≈820ms, and it is interruptible.
Why this one and not five
It is the only transition that carries information: it tells him the thing he tapped and the thing he is looking at are the same instrument. Everything else on the screen appears with a plain stagger, which costs nothing and reads as calm.
The way out
The chevron reverses it — the ring rolls back down into the strip. iOS edge-swipe is free because the app is hash-routed, and it should play the same reverse. No modal, no dead-end.
Honesty note
The gauge arc and the ring arc encode different things — the strip dial is the streak, the ring is the day. So the flying arc becomes the ring’s track, not its value; the value draws in afterwards. Morphing one number into a different number would have looked better and lied.
Reduced motion: the whole timeline collapses to a 120ms cross-fade. One flag.
03

Every state

the seven a real user hits in 91 days

A composition only has to look good once. A screen has to hold on the first morning, on the best week, and on the worst day. These are rendered, not listed — and the rule they are all designed against is the same one: never render a wall of failure.

Day one — the state that decides whether there is a day two
The masthead never shows a zero. It reads DAY 1 — a fact, not a score — and the sub-line says exactly what earns the first one. The graph does not render an empty chart with nothing in it; it renders one line saying it fills in as he goes. There is one module, offered, worth 35 XP, and it is winnable this morning. Nothing on this screen is red, faded, or accusatory, and there is something to win within the hour.
Healthy
The whole screen goes green-ish only in the two places green is allowed: the kept-gate line and the claimable module. The streak is the largest thing on the screen and the graph shows an unbroken top rail. This is the reward state, and it is deliberately the plainest of the seven.
The broken gate is stated in grey
This is the most important typographic decision in the round. The today-line for a broken gate is plain grey — the same weight as any other fact on the screen. It does not turn red, it does not turn ember, it does not grow. Understatement is the mercy. The consequence lives in the make-up strip underneath, where it belongs, and the ring carries a single ember tick at 09:30 so the record is still honest.
The streak does not move
It is marked HELD and keeps its number. That is his own mechanic and it is the reason a bad morning does not become a lost month. The price is in the strip, and the strip escalates.
What the strip never does
It never counts. There is no “slip 2 of 3” tally anywhere — the escalation is legible from the make-up itself getting harder. A running failure count under his vice’s name is exactly the wall this whole product is built to avoid.
Three frames, no red pixel in any of them. The palette does the work the layout used to.
A missed challenge, in full
One low-contrast line above a hairline: “Offer expired. Nothing lost.” No card, no colour, no XP change, no HP change, no streak change, and no record of it anywhere else in the app. It is gone on the next open and the offer quietly returns to the pool tomorrow. A lapse that leaves a trace is an obligation wearing a costume.
Why the empty state matters more than it looks
Round 2 drew the modules two-up. Thought all the way through, most days will have one offer, and some will have none — so a fixed two-up grid spends its life with a hole in it. The modules now reflow: two-up when there are two, full-width when there is one, and a single line of text when there are none. The screen is never asking him to fill an empty box.
The ceiling, enforced
Never more than two modules. If a third qualifies it waits until a slot frees. This is the anti-nagging rule from round 1, now expressed as a hard layout constraint rather than an intention.

The make-up ladder

three levels · rendered, not described

His own escalation, in the strip that carries it. The wording moves from a receipt, to a heavier receipt, to a genuine warning — but the streak number above it never changes at any level, and no level is drawn in red.

Level 1
Bible 15 minutes · six passages written out · a photograph as proof.
Level 2
All of level 1, plus a 90-minute workout, with proof for both.
Level 3
The final warning. The only frame in the whole set that is allowed to feel heavy — and it is still amber, still keeps the streak, and still ends with a way through.
One thing the GM must not lose
The button says “Log the make-up”, not “Accept punishment”. He is paying a price he set for himself. The voice matters as much as the mechanic.
PROFILE §8c: the 90-minute workout at level 2 is un-gated by any injury or current bodyweight question. Worth confirming before it can be handed to him for real.

Interaction and touch targets

what is tappable · what happens · how he gets out

Every interactive element on the masthead, drawn with its real hit box. Dashed green is the touch target, not the visual. Nothing on this screen has a target under 44px, including the two controls whose visible size is smaller than that.

Two controls that are smaller than they look
The chevron draws at 19px and the ring at 128px. Their hit boxes are 44 and 140. This is why the top bar is 52px tall rather than the 44 it appears to need.
The one gesture that is not a tap
None. There are no swipes, no long-presses and no drags on this screen. Everything is a tap on a thing he can see. That is not minimalism, it is the sixty-second rule.

Hand-off — agent 5, round 3

What I produced

renditions/rend-vices-v3.html — thirteen rendered frames: both vices in full masthead form, all seven required states, the three-level make-up ladder, an annotated touch-target frame, and the entry transition running live with a replay control. Rounds 1 and 2 are untouched. index.html was not modified. XP values are unchanged from round 1: +35 / +60 / +120 / +180 / +400 · +90 / +250.

The tweaks I made, and why each one was forced by function

  1. The ring went from 112px to 128px. At 112 the gate tick, the now-hand and the danger bands rendered under 2px and were illegible on a screenshot at 3× — I looked, and they were noise. 128 is the smallest size at which every mark on the ring survives. It costs 16px of masthead width and the type still clears 196px, which fits the longest streak the app can produce (“32 weeks”).
  2. I added a today-line to the masthead. Round 2’s head said how long the streak was and nothing about today — which is the fact he opens the screen for. One mono line under the sub: gate live, gate held, gate broken, or “the gate starts today”. It is the only genuinely new element in this round.
  3. The modules now reflow. Two-up when there are two offers, full-width when there is one, and a single line of type when there are none. A fixed two-up grid spends most of its life with a hole in it, and an empty card reads as something owed.
  4. Day one shows “DAY 1”, never “0 days”. A zero in 64px display type on the first morning is precisely the wall of failure, before he has even had a chance. The figure is replaced by a fact.
  5. The display step gained a second optical size — 64px for figures, 44px for words. “DAY 1” at 64 overflows the 198px masthead column; at 44 it has the same optical weight as “6” at 64. This is a documented pair, not a break in the scale.
  6. The graph gained an empty state. One centred line instead of an axis with nothing on it.
  7. A broken gate is set in plain grey, not in ember. Round 2 would have coloured it as “live and at risk”. Thought through, colouring the worst line on the screen is the opposite of what this product needs — the consequence belongs in the make-up strip, and the statement belongs in the same grey as every other fact. Understatement is the mercy.
  8. The top bar is 52px, not 44. Required to give a 19px chevron a 44px hit box without crowding the label.
  9. The graph gained a fourth mark: a hollow dashed ring for today. Rendering the live state exposed it — at 09:42 nothing has been logged, and drawing today as a filled green “clean all day” dot claims a result the day has not produced yet. Today is now drawn hollow until it resolves.
  10. The smoke streak counts DAYS THE GATE HELD, not fully clean days. This is the largest change in the round and the today-line is what exposed it: a masthead reading “6 days clean” directly above a line reading “logged 14:40 today” is a contradiction, and round 2 only got away with it because it never stated what happened today. His rule is nothing before 12:00, so the streak that means anything is the run of days that rule held. Fully-clean days still show — they are the green dots, and they are what the 24h and 32h challenges are made of. Every chart in this file was rebuilt so the dots agree with the number above them.

What I was tempted to change and deliberately did not

  • The horizontal module rail from The Instrument. It is a better container for three offers and I wanted it. It is also a different composition’s idea, and the reflow above solves the real problem (usually one offer, sometimes none) without importing it. He chose the Masthead.
  • Making the masthead tappable. Every other big number in this app opens something. This one should not — it is the reward, and a tappable trophy invites fiddling on exactly the days he should be closing the app and going to work.
  • Polishing the graph. It could carry a rolling average, a best-streak marker, week dividers. He called it “rudimentary but a cool little style”, which is a compliment about restraint. It has been made consistent across both vices and otherwise left alone.
  • A second motion moment on the modules. A claim animation would be satisfying. One considered transition was the brief and the entry is the one that carries meaning.
  • Colouring the streak green when it is long. Green is reserved for banked, past-tense things. A running streak is not banked. Holding that line is most of why the palette still reads as one system.

What will be awkward when the GM integrates this into index.html

1 · The shipped slip logic contradicts the shipped copy — and this design sides with the copy. In logSlip() the toast says “Streak held. You owe a make-up.” while logSmoked() two functions above sets S.vices.smoke.cleanStreak=0 when the gate breaks. So today the streak is both held and wiped. Every frame here shows it held, marked HELD, with the price in the strip — because that is his stated mechanic and the crew’s law #3. This is a behaviour change in the engine, not a styling change, and it needs the owner’s nod before it ships. If he wants the wipe kept, the masthead needs a different answer for the broken-gate state and I would want to design it rather than have it fall out of an edit.

1b · The engine stores the wrong streak for the rule it enforces. S.vices.smoke.cleanStreak is written by viceClean(), which counts days he logged as completely clean — but the rule the app actually gates on is nothing before 12:00. So a day where he held the gate and smoked at 2 PM does not extend the streak the masthead is built to show. The screen needs a gate-held streak derived from day().smoked.at versus vices.smoke.gate, with the fully-clean count kept alongside it for the 24h/32h ladder. Both numbers already exist in the data; only the streak derivation is missing.

2 · The vice screen is currently a bottom sheet, not a view. openVice() calls sheet(...). The masthead is a full screen with its own top bar and back path, so it wants to be a fourth destination in route() alongside today/push/bank/record — otherwise the entry transition has nowhere to run and iOS edge-swipe does not work. That is a router change, and it is the single biggest integration cost in this round.

3 · The masthead needs a streak the engine does not currently store. The graph plots the time of first use per day; day().smoked stores exactly that ({at:'HH:MM'}), so the smoke graph is free. The dishwasher graph needs a per-week count history, and rollDishWeek() currently throws the count away when it rolls the week. One line to keep it, but it must be added before the graph has anything to draw.

4 · The gauge strip is locked, and the entry transition touches it. Nothing about the strip’s design changes — but the transition needs the tapped dial’s bounding rect at the moment of the tap, so openVice has to pass it through. That is additive and does not alter a single pixel of the locked strip, which I assume is inside the spirit of the lock. Flagging it rather than assuming.

5 · The gauge arc still overstates its own number. Carried from round 1 and still true: dial() uses Math.PI*21 as the dasharray for an arc of radius 19, so 50% draws as about 55%. If the entry transition flies that arc into the masthead ring, the two will visibly disagree at the hand-off frame unless it is fixed. This round gives that old bug a new way to be seen.

6 · viceClean('smoke') can still be tapped before noon, banking a clean day at 9 AM that the 11 AM gate break then contradicts. The masthead has no equivalent button — the clean day settles at close-out — so integrating this screen is a natural moment to retire it.

Still-open questions this design inherits

  • The waking day is hard-coded 05:00–23:00 in the ring. PROFILE §8c lists his actual sleep hours as unknown. The ring is wrong at both ends if that is off, and it is the most visible element on the screen.
  • Level 2’s 90-minute workout is un-gated by current bodyweight or any injury — also PROFILE §8c. It is rendered here because it is his design, but it should not be handed to him for real until that is answered.
  • The 32-hour rung has no defined clock. 24 hours clean is unambiguous; 32 hours crosses a sleep boundary and a noon gate, and the app will need to decide whether the count runs from the last log or from midnight. Not a design question — an engine one — but the module cannot display a truthful countdown until it is settled.
