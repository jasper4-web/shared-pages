Burn the Boats · Agent 6 · Round 2 · The Bank should be fun

Ten worlds. Ten layouts.
No two of them are the same page.

Round 1 gave you seven answers to one question, and you were right that none of them were fun. This is a restart, not a revision. The house aesthetic is off for this tab — colour, saturation, light backgrounds and outright silliness are all in play. The only two rules left are: it must stay legible, and it must not look like the other six tabs.

Same data in all ten: 1,530 XP · 2 privileges held · 4 boosts · 5 rewards, two of them affordable today, three still out of reach — and every one of them carrying the condition he wrote himself.

Step 1 — the three banned designs

  • A list of rewards with prices
  • A grid of cards
  • A balance at the top with items beneath

Round 1 was all three at once, seven times. Nothing below is any of them. I also rejected five of the ten starting provocations: gacha, the claw machine and the casino cage all make getting a reward a matter of chance, which destroys the one mechanic he actually cares about — he sets the price and writes the condition. The trophy shelf is about the past; this tab is about what's next. The restaurant menu is the banned priced list wearing a hat.

An arcade counter is the only place in ordinary life where you look at a number you earned and immediately see what it buys, hanging in front of you. It is also the least serious possible frame for a reward, which is the point.

World: a 1974 bank passbook
Primitive: typewritten prose — no rows, no boxes, no layout

This is NOT trying to be fun.

His words were "or it could be super not colorful on purpose." This is that, taken all the way: white paper, black typewriter, one red stamp, and not a single other colour on the screen. There is no list, no card, no bar and no button styling — the entire Bank is one letter written to him by an institution that does not care whether he enjoys reading it.

05 · STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT
Why it might work
Every other tab in his app is dark. Opening this one is a physical shock — a white page in a black app — and it takes zero colour to do it. It is also the only direction where his own conditions are set in full sentences rather than compressed to a caption.
Locked, expressed as
A sentence. "Approximately nine days." No state, no styling, no colour change at all — the paragraph simply does not end with YOU MAY DRAW THIS TODAY.
The joke that isn't one
The footer: "This document is not a reward. The rewards are listed above." A bank statement has no interest in delighting you, and the flatness is the gag — but the content underneath it is completely sincere.
Risk: a full-white screen at 6:20 AM is physically bright, and this is the direction most likely to be read as me ignoring the brief rather than answering the second half of it. If it ships it needs a paper-toned dark variant that keeps the typewriter and loses the glare.

Why it might work
It is the only direction where the balance and the distance are the same drawing. Moving the line up is the entire game, and the line moves every single day whatever else happens — which means this page is never static and never scolds.
Locked, expressed as
Rock above the snow line. Not dimmed, not disabled — a different altitude. The bands also narrow as they rise, so the summit is visibly the hardest and smallest thing.

Reward names are plotted in price order by height, so the cheapest thing is nearest his feet and the weekend away is a summit. He never has to be told which is the stretch goal.

The fix is that claiming plants a flag at that altitude and the line keeps its high-water mark — but that is a real mechanic decision, not a visual one, and it is not mine to make.

Ten holes per row is the most legible progress representation in this entire document. Hand-off · Agent 6 · Round 2

Ranked, and the two I expect you to hate

What this is

renditions/rend-bank-v2.html — ten renditions in ten worlds with ten different layout primitives, plus the round-1 ceremony carried forward and improved. Round 1 is untouched at rend-bank.html. I did not touch index.html or any other agent's file.

Ranked

1 · 04 Tilt. It is the loudest thing here and the only loud one where the noise carries meaning. A pinball machine already speaks in exactly the register this tab needs: "REPLAY AT 2,000" is a better motivator than any progress bar, because it is a threshold stated as a promise. And printing "TILT IS DISABLED" on the apron states the app's first law — this page cannot punish you — in the machine's own idiom, without a word of UX copy.

2 · 07 The Mountain. The best single mechanic in either round: the snow line is the balance. Price and distance stop being two numbers and become one drawing, and the line moves every day no matter how the day went. It is bright, it is nothing like the rest of the app, and it is the one I would bet on still being good in November. Its unresolved problem is what happens when he spends — see the risk note.

3 · 03 Departures. Ranked third on fun and first on copy. Reframing a locked reward as a scheduled departure is the strongest idea in the document: nothing on a departures board is ever refused, it is only not boarding yet. It also hands you a push notification for free — "your day off boards Thursday" — and no other direction generates a sentence that good without someone writing it.

The two I expect you to hate

08 The Fridge. It is a child's chore chart handed to a man with a February runway problem. There is a real argument for it — nothing on that door is judging him, which speaks directly to why v1 died — but the gap between "warm and disarming" and "condescending" is one bad morning wide. I built it at full commitment rather than hedging it toward tasteful, because a hedged version would have been another near-duplicate and scored zero.

05 Statement of Account. You asked for fun and this is a white page of typewriter prose that literally prints "This document is not a reward." You also said it could be "super not colorful on purpose", and I took that seriously enough to remove every colour on the screen except one red stamp. It is either the sharpest thing here or it reads as me ignoring the first half of your sentence. I don't think there's a middle outcome.

Runner-up risk: 10 The Bank Texts You. A voice that talks first is powerful right up until it congratulates you on a day you know you wasted.

If you want one page rather than one style

The composite I would actually build: Tilt's backglass at the top — enormous score, "REPLAY AT 2,000", "TILT IS DISABLED" — sitting above the Mountain's snow line as the body, with rewards plotted by altitude and Departures' status column ("BOARDING NOW" / "DEPARTS IN 4d") replacing every price countdown. Privileges as Card 0041's tear-off tabs pinned to the bottom edge, and boosts as The Bank Texts' quick-reply chips, which are simply the fastest way to log one. That is four primitives from four worlds, and they don't fight — because each is solving a different one of the four content types.

What I rejected and why

  • Gacha, the claw machine, the casino cage. All three make getting a reward partly a matter of chance. He sets the price and writes the condition himself — that is the mechanic he was emphatic about, and chance destroys it. A casino frame is also the wrong register for a man tracking two vices.
  • The trophy shelf. It displays what you already won. This tab's job is what's next.
  • The restaurant menu. It is the banned "list of items with prices" wearing a hat, and I would have been submitting round 1 again with better typography.
  • A tenth loud direction. I stopped at five loud, one plain, four in between. Ten loud pages would have converged just as hard as ten austere ones did — the divergence has to include register, not just volume.

Verification

arsenal-lint: clean ✓. Rendered headlessly and screenshotted every phone at 390×844, top and scrolled, and I looked at all of them. The round-1 overflow bug is fixed and tested: the gallery chrome is now checked at a 390px viewport as well as 1400px, because that was the width at which it broke last time — the phone frames go full-bleed and the wrapper padding collapses below 452px.

For the GM

  • Everything I flagged in round 1 still stands and none of it is visual: no spend history (claim() deletes the reward instead of recording it), rewards are one-shot for all 91 days, S.privileges is a bare integer with no way to spend one, dailyXp() uses a lifetime average so "~N days" drifts optimistic, and logBoost hands out a privilege on every tap with no confirm.
  • Two directions here need a data model that doesn't exist yet. The Mountain needs a high-water mark so spending doesn't lower the snow line. Departures needs an actual projected date per reward, not a day count, or the whole "boards Thursday" idea is unbuildable.
  • The handwriting is free. Marker Felt and Chalkboard SE are iOS system fonts — no download, no licence, renders on his phone. Worth knowing before anyone vendors a script webfont for 06 or 08.
  • Whichever wins, the ceremony must be re-skinned to match it. Its beats are right; its palette belongs to round 1.
