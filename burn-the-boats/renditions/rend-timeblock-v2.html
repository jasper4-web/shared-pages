Agent 4 · round 2 · ten directions

Ten apps, not ten paddings

"Every rendition is trying so hard to look like the previous one, just a little bit different. They're so boring. When I asked for 10 renditions I want 10 to be on the complete opposite sides of each other in every way, shape or form."

Fair. Round 1 was twelve arrangements of one vertical stack — twelve answers to the same question is one answer. This round is built under a hard constraint instead of a target count: ten layout primitives, each used exactly once, and ten different rules of the app broken, one each. Two of them are supposed to look like they came from a different product entirely. They do.

The one survivor from round 1 — the Now/Next pair — is direction 01, pushed a long way past where it was. Nothing else is built around it.

Step 1 · the three obvious layouts, written down so they can be banned

  1. A vertical stack of the six elements. Rail, then time-left, then push, then NEXT UP, then wins. This is the shipped build and it was ten of round 1's twelve.
  2. The same stack with something collapsed behind a tab, a swipe or a dock.
  3. The same stack reordered, merged into one card, or with two elements welded together.

All three are banned, along with anything a person would describe with the same sentence. Nothing below is any of them — which is why none of these look like the app you have.

card-deck

My read

Ranked on whether he would still want to open it in week nine — not on height. Height is at the bottom of each card and it changed nothing about this order.

1st

05 · The Hand my pick

It is the only one that solves the actual complaint and the always-counts problem with the same idea. Blocks become cards you play — which answers "I feel scrambled" with agency — and small wins become free plays you are holding, which is the first framing in either round where they read as an asset rather than a leftover. The deck's thickness tells him how much day is left without a number, and a red card jammed into the top of the deck is a better make-up mechanic than any banner. It fits the RPG soul of the product without any of round 1's stacking.

2nd

02 · The Board the safest radical option

The strongest answer to the law that governs everything — never render a wall of failure. Three struck-through tiles out of seventeen is not a wall, it is a board that is still mostly open, and that is true on his worst day as well as his best. Dissolving the line between "scheduled work" and "small win" is the single most useful structural idea in this document, because it makes earning on a bad day the same motion as doing the work on a good one. Least beautiful of my top three.

3rd

10 · The Core as a mode, not the region

The one he will react to. It is not an instrument and I have said so plainly — but "tap a coal, the fire burns brighter" is the most motivating thing either round produced, and it is the only design where always-counts is structurally load-bearing rather than politely accommodated. Ship it as what the screen becomes during a run, or as a 6am screen, with a swap into something numeric.

4th

01 · The Cockpit the survivor, pushed

The Now/Next idea taken as far as it goes: the live block owns two thirds of the screen and fills as it runs. It is the most buildable thing here and the most likely to survive contact with a real week. It ranks fourth because it is still recognisably a descendant of round 1, and this round was not about safe descendants.

5–7

03 The Line · 06 The Tape · 07 The Ghost

03 is the most emotionally intelligent idea in the set — today and December on one route — and the tallest thing I have ever built for a phone; take the terminus idea and put it somewhere cheaper. 06 is the only genuinely new interaction and I cannot convince myself he'd drag it more than twice. 07 is the highest-variance entry: built on his own favourite mechanic, and one bad week away from being the wall of failure that killed v1. It needs the ghost to vanish once beaten, and that is a rule change, not a layout.

8th

08 · The Receipt I expect him to hate this

Cream paper in a black premium app. He named the aesthetic himself — dark, premium, techy — and this breaks it on purpose to see whether the reaction is "wrong app" or "oh, that's different." Structurally it is sound: it is the calmest possible bad day and the best home for a running tally. Aesthetically I think he bounces off it in under two seconds, and that reaction is itself useful information about how much of the house style is load-bearing.

9th

09 · The Terminal I expect him to hate this

It is the fastest to read and it has no soul whatsoever. A non-technical founder asked for something that makes him feel like he burned the boats; this makes him feel like he opened a log file. It is in the set because the brief asked for ten opposite corners and this is a real corner, and because its speed is a genuine benchmark — if a prettier direction can't beat eleven lines of text on time-to-read, that's worth knowing.

10th

04 · The Face

Last not because it is bad but because it is the one whose idea I could not make earn its cost. A 344px dial is the entire fold budget, and everything a clock face tells you, the deck in 05 and the board in 02 tell you for a third of the space. Kept in the set because it is the only radial and the arcs-as-remaining-work idea deserves to be seen once.

Hand-off to the GM

What I produced

renditions/rend-timeblock-v2.html — ten directions built under the divergence protocol. Ten layout primitives, each used exactly once. Ten different rules of the app broken, one each. Every one carries its "This is NOT a ___" line and a table of where each of the six elements went. Round 1's file is untouched.

The two I expect him to hate

08 The Receipt and 09 The Terminal. The receipt puts a cream paper panel in a black app and the terminal strips out design entirely — one violates his stated aesthetic, the other violates the product's "must work for someone not good with computers" law. Both are deliberate. If he hates them for the reasons I've predicted, that tells us the house style and the emotional register are genuinely load-bearing, not just preference — which is worth knowing before we spend a week building. If he likes one of them, everything else in this document is over-designed.

What I'd actually build

05 The Hand's free-plays mechanic, on 02 The Board's structure. They agree with each other: both treat everything on the screen as something available to be earned rather than a schedule to be obeyed, and both make small wins first-class without hiding or shrinking them. The Core (10) becomes the run-mode screen. That is one coherent product out of three directions, and it does not resemble what ships today.

What I deliberately rejected

  • Six of the ten provocations in the brief, as instructed. "One sentence" and "no schedule at all" were round 1's #11 and #04 in disguise. "The rail IS the interface" survived as 06's tape only after it earned a real interaction. "Past collapses to nothing" is a property, not a layout — it's inside 02 and 08 rather than being its own entry.
  • Any layout where always-counts is behind a tap. Five of round 1's twelve did that. None of these ten do — in every one of them, all six wins are visible and tappable on the screen.
  • A second swipe-deck, tab panel or collapsible. Banned by step 1 and they were half of round 1.

Things the GM should know

  • The double safe-area inset bug from round 1 is still open — .app and .furnace both add var(--st), costing ~47px at the top of the only screen that matters. Still not my turf, still worth fixing before any of these is built.
  • Only 08 pushes NEXT UP past the fold (+305). The other nine keep the focus-run button above it — including 03 and 04, whose regions run long (617px and 398px) but whose hero sits high. So the honest cost of the tall ones is that the terminus, the push row and the account totals live below the fold, not the instruction. Per this brief I let the idea win and reported it.

  • A tsparticles bug that affects other agents' files, not just mine. The vendored engine is v3.9.1, and v3 registers nothing on its own: without calling loadSlim(tsParticles) before tsParticles.load(...), particles are created but no shape drawer or mover is installed — they never move and never paint. It fails silently: no console error, a canvas is present, and particles.count reports a healthy number. I only caught it by sampling the canvas pixels (108 live particles, 0 lit pixels). renditions/rend-bank.html and header-directions.html call tsParticles.load without loadSlim and will have invisible particles; rend-header, rend-vices and rend-vices-v2 do call it and are fine. Worth telling agents 3 and 6 before the GM integrates anything.

What verification caught in my own work

Recorded because "I looked at the screenshots" only counts if I say what looking found. Eight defects, all caught by rendering rather than by reading my own code: the dial's win labels sat on top of the day's arcs and its hand ran straight through the centre text; the card deck had no visible thickness — the back cards were empty divs that collapsed to nothing, which killed the one signal that direction exists for; the Ghost's rival card was completely hidden behind your own, so the comparison it is built on could not be read; the Board's past tiles were in the order 09:00, 06:30, 08:30 while the direction's whole claim is that position is time; the Cockpit's push strip sat outside the measured region; the Terminal's push line wrapped and broke the monospace column; the deck's edge line was printed underneath its own button; and the Core's embers never rendered at all.
