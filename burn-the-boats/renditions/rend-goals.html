Goals — six architectures
for a 390px screen.

The tab called Push becomes Goals. It has to hold six life domains, several horizons between now and December 1, the tactics committed this week, and the link from each of those down to the blocks on the day rail — without becoming an org chart nobody opens.

Each of the six below answers one question differently: what is primary? Time, category, focus, the week, mutability, or the ritual. They are not six skins of one idea. Two of them are rendered mid-edit, because restructuring is the requirement he cared most about.

Every mock is live. Tap the stations, open a door, drag a card, run the Friday steps. Each frame is a real 390×844 phone viewport that scrolls inside itself — so the scroll depth quoted in the notes is measured, not estimated.

Milestones are moved with a single ↓ push — the row physically drops into the next station, which is what "not stuck in one route" looks like as a gesture.

01 · THE SPINE — TAP A STATION TO COLLAPSE IT · TAP ↓ TO PUSH A MILESTONE BACK
What it makes easy
Time pressure, honestly. Four stations and a burned gold line answer "how much runway is left" before he reads a word. It is also the only option where load per month is visible — August holds four milestones, October holds four; if he pushes everything back, the stack at the bottom gets visibly, uncomfortably tall.
What it makes hard
Following one domain. THE WORK's chain is scattered across four stations — to read it he has to scan vertically and colour-match. Nothing here says "here is guitar, start to finish".
The weekly ritual
~90s. The week lives at the top so it is read first, but writing next week's tactics means scrolling to the August station and back up — the ritual is split across the scroll.
Restructuring
↓ PUSH on any row moves it one station later, with the row animating down into its new home. One tap, no drag, no sheet. Re-timing is the most common restructure he will do and this is the cheapest gesture for it.
Nothing scheduled
A dashed row inside August that reads "Nothing set for August — and that is allowed". Absence gets a seat rather than a red mark.

05 · THE BOARD — PRESS AND DRAG A CARD INTO ANOTHER COLUMN
What it makes easy
Re-timing, and seeing load. Four cards in August against three in October is a visible weight distribution, and moving a commitment is one gesture with no dialog. Nothing else here makes the tree feel as soft — which is precisely the feeling he asked for.
What it makes hard
Almost everything else. A column is 246px, so a goal gets ~30 characters a line. Chains are invisible — nothing on this board tells you that "first client live" and "3 SANO clients" are the same thread. And the horizontal axis fights the vertical scroll of every other screen in the app.
The weekly ritual
~2.5 min. Dragging is satisfying once and tiring by week four. A weekly ritual should be typing and tapping, not aiming.
Restructuring
The most fluid gesture of the six — and the most likely to be done by accident. There is no undo affordance on a phone drag, and a fat-thumbed re-time of the December lag is exactly the kind of edit that should be hard.
Nothing scheduled
Structural and immediate: MIND simply has no card in the first column, and the note below spells it out. Emptiness is a visible gap in the lane rather than a message.

The ranking

What I would build, and in what order.

Six architectures, one page. The honest finding is that no single one of these covers the brief — the brief contains two different jobs (run the week · restructure the tree) and they want different shapes. The ranking reflects that.

1

02 · Six Doors — with 06's Friday flow bolted on as the ritual

Six Doors is the only option that holds all six domains, several horizons, the weekly lead measure and the block binding in one unbroken column, and its ladder editor is the strongest answer to the requirement he cared most about. Split-in-two and insert-a-rung mean the tree bends instead of breaking, which is his "not stuck in one route" in concrete form.

Its one real weakness — a slow, six-door weekly ritual — is exactly what The Friday Table fixes. Ship the doors as the document and the Friday flow as the ceremony that walks them. They are not competing designs; one is the read view and one is the write view.

2

06 · The Friday Table

Ranked second only because it cannot stand alone. As the write path it is the best thing on this page: one question per screen, the month pre-filled, tweak-not-restate, and a step that asks about the neglected goals so parking becomes a decision. If only one of these six gets built this week, build this one — the Friday ritual is the load-bearing habit of the entire goal engine.

3

04 · The Week Is The Page

The best daily screen of the six and the cheapest solution to "goals affect the daily schedule" — the lineage line plus block chips puts the whole cascade in one row of a tactic card. Third because it is largely a better version of the Today tab; most of what it does well belongs there, not behind a Goals tab. Steal the lineage line regardless of which architecture wins.

4

01 · The Spine

The most emotionally correct and the most dangerous. It is the only one that makes the runway physical, and the ↓ push gesture is a genuinely good one-tap re-time. But four stations of unmet milestones stacked below the fold is the closest any of these comes to a wall of failure, and that is the one thing this app is not allowed to be. Worth revisiting as a read-only view inside the Record tab, where looking back is the point.

5

03 · The One

Wins the glance test outright and loses the brief. He asked for a system across six categories; this answers with one. Its idea is still worth keeping: put the single WIG's lead measure, at 42px, at the top of whichever architecture wins. As a whole page it hides FAITH and PEOPLE, and those are the two he most needs kept visible.

6

05 · The Board

Last, and I would not build it. It is the only one where the phone actively loses: 246px columns, no visible chains, a horizontal axis at war with the rest of the app, and an undoable destructive gesture on the goals that should be hardest to change. Built and shown because "drag between horizons" sounds right until you hold it — and now we know.

The strongest argument against my own pick

Six Doors starts closed. Six tiles, no instruction, nothing demanding attention — a page that waits. And the governing failure mode of this product is not a bad design, it is him not opening it. Options 04 and 06 both have a claim on the first screen that 02 does not: one shows him what he owes today, the other tells him what to do right now. A Goals page that opens as a polite grid of six doors is a page he will visit on Friday and forget on Tuesday — and a goal structure he does not look at is a goal structure that does not affect the daily schedule, which was the entire point of his ask.

If that argument wins, the answer is not to demote the doors — it is to make the Goals tab open on this week (04) with the six doors one swipe behind it, and let the Friday flow (06) take over the whole tab on Fridays.

What I produced and where

  • renditions/rend-goals.html — six live phone mock-ups at a true 390×844 viewport, all interactive. Two are rendered mid-edit: 02 Six Doors (ladder open, September rung in the editor, date stepper, split, insert) and 06 The Friday Table (step 2 of 4, month pre-filled, strike-and-insert diff).
  • Scroll depths in the notes are measured from the rendered DOM at 390px on load, not estimated. Weekly-ritual times are my estimates and are labelled as such.
  • The horizon count is deliberately different in each: 4 stations (01), 3 rungs (02), 2 horizons (03), 1 ribbon (04), 4 columns (05), month-as-unit (06). No answer is assumed correct.

Ranked recommendation

  • Build 02 + 06 as one feature. Six Doors is the document, the Friday Table is the ceremony. The doors hold the structure and the editor; the flow gets him through it in under a minute once a week. Neither is complete alone and together they cover the whole brief.
  • Steal 04's lineage line unconditionally — THE WORK ▸ AUG: first client live ▸ DEC: 3 clients under a tactic, with its block chips beside it. It is the cheapest thing on this page and it is the thing that makes goals visibly drive the schedule.
  • Steal 03's hero. Whatever wins, the WIG's lead measure belongs at the top at 42px. The scoreboard test is passed by one number, not by a tree.
  • Do not build 05.

What I deliberately rejected

  • A seventh option — horizon accordion / "four shelves." Killed before building: it is the same sentence as The Board ("horizon is the top level"), and the brief says replace, not pad. Five distinct primaries plus the ritual is the honest count; there was no sixth axis worth a mock.
  • A radar / hexagon of the six domains. Pretty, and it duplicates the locked glance card at the top of Today. A second radar would be decoration, and the glance card is locked.
  • Progress percentages on December lags. "62% to 3 clients" is a fiction — the goal engine is explicit that lags are not scored. Every option here shows lag goals as text with a count, never as a bar.
  • Red anywhere. No red appears on any of the six screens except the DELETE RUNG affordance. Parked, empty and behind are all rendered in violet, dashed grey or gold. That was a deliberate rule.
  • The Anthony gate. Left out entirely per the brief. No prospect is named anywhere in these designs — THE WORK's tactics are generic outreach counts, which is also what makes them re-usable for clients 2 and 3.

Things the GM should know that were not mine to fix

  • The tab rename has a knock-on. The Push tab currently carries the Anthony gate, the trading session log and the LA Edible marker (index.html renderPush()). If Goals replaces Push, those three need a home. The trading log is a daily action and belongs on Today or in the evening close-out, not behind a goals tab, regardless of which architecture wins.
  • The data model needs a "parked" state and a "no milestone set" state, and both must be distinguishable from "missed" in the execution score. Four of my six options depend on it. Agent 1 owns the model — flagging it because every one of these screens breaks the wall-of-failure law without it.
  • Block binding is currently one-way. tacticFor() reads a tactic for a block; nothing lets a tactic name its blocks. Every design here shows block chips on the tactic (B1 MON · B2 TUE), which needs the reverse index. It is a small change and it is what makes the connection legible in either direction.
  • PEOPLE has no August milestone anywhere in the source material and MIND's is unstated. I rendered both as honestly empty rather than inventing them. If the GM wants a fuller-looking mock, the missing content is real, not a rendering gap.
  • Weekly-ritual times are estimates. Scroll depths are measured; the ~40s/~60s/~2min figures are my judgement from tap-count and screen count, and should be treated as ranking signal, not data.
