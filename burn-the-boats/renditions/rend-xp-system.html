Every option below was walked through as a lived Tuesday, then run through a simulation of your actual scoring engine over the full 110-day run under four versions of you — the perfect run, the realistic run, a two-week collapse, and a grinder who does chores instead of work (the exploit test). Three facts from inside the engine

Read these first — every option lives or dies against them
FACT 1 · THE 99 IS DATE-LOCKED. "SOONER" IS CURRENTLY IMPOSSIBLE.

The engine divides all progress by the 110 days of the run, on purpose: a perfect run reaches 99 exactly on Dec 25 — never in October. Today, nothing you do can move the 99 earlier. What CAN move is the pace date the app already computes ("AT THIS PACE · 99 ON …"): fall behind and it drifts to January; catch up and it pulls back toward Dec 25. So "closer to the 99 sooner" is really a choice between two laws — that's the fork at the bottom of this page, and it's yours alone to call.

FACT 2 · THE LIFE→99 DOOR ALREADY EXISTS. IT'S JUST INVISIBLE — AND TRAPPED.

Your weekly standards (Bible 3×, cooked 4×, real time with someone 3×…) already feed the 99 — each one adds real weight to its life area's score, capped at its own number so it can't be farmed. Laundry 2× a week, made a standard, would move your 99 today. Two problems: the app never shows you this connection, and I found a live trap — adding or editing a standard silently re-scores every past week. Add "laundry 2×" in week 10 and the engine retroactively marks weeks 1–9 as failures at it. Your OVR would visibly DROP as a reward for committing to a new habit. That must be fixed no matter which option you pick.

FACT 3 · THE KNOCK-OUT ✓ PAYS NOTHING, AND A STRONG DAY IS ~445 XP.

Knocking out a small goal — your core loop — currently pays 0 XP. Meanwhile the Bank's ladder (900 → 22,000) is priced against a strong day of roughly 445: a clean deep run is 65, closing the day 50, a clean vice day 40, wins 15–30, boosts capped at 250/day. Whatever we choose, these prices are the ground truth the new system has to respect — or knowingly re-price.

The five systems

Each: how it works · a Tuesday lived in it · where it breaks · verdict
KEEP · AS PLUMBING

1 · The Standards Door

Anything you do repeatedly can become a standard — n× a week, your number — in one tap from the add sheet or the quick log. Standards are already the thing that feeds the 99 (Fact 2); this option just opens that door wide and makes the connection visible. XP stays money.

A TUESDAY, LIVED

7:40p — you throw in laundry, tap it in the quick log. +15 · LAUNDRY 1 OF 2 THIS WEEK · FEEDS MIND

That last part is the change: the tap has always paid XP — now it tells you it's moving the 99.

Sunday — the week closes: standards met 6 of 8, and the areas they feed climbed at full rate.

  • Grind-proof by construction — the 3rd laundry past your 2× pays XP but can't farm the 99.
  • The over-promise trap: set "laundry 5×" in an enthusiastic week 1 and every normal week quietly bleeds that area. Fix: standards BEND — lowering your number is one tap, never shamed, forward-only.
  • Requires the Fact-2 retro-fix first, or adding any standard drops your score for the past.
  • The honest limit: the sim barely moves (83 → 83). Ratio engines score promises kept, not volume — so this is the right door, but alone it won't produce the "getting closer sooner" feeling you asked for.
VERDICTThe correct foundation and a small build — but it's plumbing, not the headline. Every surviving option below assumes it exists.
KILLED · BY THE NUMBERS

2 · XP Is The Score

One currency. The 99 is computed from lifetime XP against a target curve (~400/day pace). Laundry pays 15, and those 15 visibly push the 99. The purest reading of your brief — every good act, one number.

A TUESDAY, LIVED

Every single tap moves the big number. It feels incredible — for about two weeks.

Week 6, behind pace: you notice boosts pay 250 and blocks pay 65. You are now optimizing prices, not living days.

  • The sim is fatal: all four profiles finish at 99 — the grinder, the collapse, everyone. The XP economy is too rich for a curve to hold, and any curve steep enough to stop the grinder also stops you.
  • The 99 becomes self-priced. You own the win and boost prices (that's locked, and right). The moment prices push the score, "did I become him?" becomes "did I set laundry to 100?" The number dies the way the old app died — you stop believing it.
  • Permanent inflation pressure on the Bank: every future price decision would be a score decision too.
VERDICTKilled, with the receipts shown. The one thing worth saving: the feeling of every tap visibly counting — option 3 delivers it without the disease.
THE STRONG CANDIDATE · BIG BUILD

3 · The 70/30 Day

Every day scores out of 100. The sacred blocks and the market are the 70. Everything else — standards, chores, wins, the stuff normal people put off — fills the 30, up to its cap. The 99 climbs on your accumulated day-scores ÷ 110, so it still never falls, and perfection still lands on Dec 25. (The split is illustrative — 70/30 vs 75/25 vs 60/40 is YOUR number to lock.)

A TUESDAY, LIVED

You clear 3 of 4 blocks — the 70 gives you 52.5. Laundry, cooked dinner, in bed on time — the 30 fills. Day: 82.

7:41p, the laundry tap: +15 XP · DAY 74 → 82 · PACE HOLDS DEC 25 — the tap moves the day, the day moves the 99. That's your brief, in one toast.

A rough Saturday where you only do chores and a walk still posts 30 real points — a bad day is never a zero, which is the first law holding.

  • The only option that passes all three tests: realistic you 83 → 89, collapse 80 → 85, and the grinder 81 → 75 — chores genuinely count and genuinely cannot replace the work. The 70 is a wall.
  • The "sooner" feeling is real and honest: mid-run the number climbs faster because the 30 is finally yours to fill — but the ceiling stays earned.
  • Biggest build of the five. A new scoring core alongside the domain radar (the radar survives — domains still say WHERE the life credit lands). Needs its own harness and a hostile QC pass before it ships.
  • Two numbers to lock with you before a line is written: the split (70/30?) and the 30's daily cap (how many life-units fill it — the sim used 3/day plus standards).
VERDICTThe strongest honest expression of what you asked for. Worth its build cost — but second, after the plumbing, and only with your two numbers locked.
PARKED · KINDNESS, NOT SPINE

4 · Repair Credit

Extra life acts can patch the week's holes: miss the walk Monday, deep-clean the house Thursday, and BODY's week is made whole — capped at one patch per area per week, and the sacred blocks and the market can never be patched. "Closer sooner" becomes literal: the pace date heals backward toward Dec 25.

A TUESDAY, LIVED

You missed Monday's walk. Thursday you deep-clean the garage. BODY PATCHED · PACE DEC 29 → DEC 27

It reads like healing, not scoring — the kindest possible mechanic after a bad stretch.

  • Kindest to the collapse profile (+3): the return path gets a real hand up, never a lecture.
  • The grinder pockets the most from it (+4, finishing one point under the realistic you — the thinnest margin of any option). The fences hold, but barely, and every future tweak risks them.
  • Invisible complexity: explaining why Thursday's cleaning "counted twice" needs a paragraph. Anything that needs a paragraph on a 390px screen loses.
VERDICTParked, not killed. If the 70/30 day ships and the collapse weeks still feel cruel in real use, this is the targeted medicine — added then, with its fences tested in its own harness.
KEEP · THE GRADUATION ONLY

5 · The Habit Ladder

The app notices repetition. Log laundry three weeks running and it offers, once: "This looks like a habit — make it a standard? 2× a week, your number." One tap, and it walks through the Standards Door into the 99. (The original idea also grew the XP as the habit matured — 10 → 15 → 20. That half is cut: it's complexity that mostly rewards remembering to tap.)

A TUESDAY, LIVED

Third Tuesday of laundry in a row. THIS KEEPS HAPPENING · MAKE IT A STANDARD?

That moment IS your sentence — "getting in the habit of doing laundry often" — noticed by the machine and turned into a commitment you size.

  • Zero exploit surface: graduation just opens option 1's capped door. Decline and it never nags — it offers exactly once per habit.
  • Tiny build: the quick-log history already exists; this is one detector and one sheet.
  • The escalating-XP half died in writing: values that grow must someday shrink, and shrinking XP is a fine — the first law forbids it.
VERDICTKeep the graduation moment, ship it with option 1. It's the smallest thing on this page and the closest to your actual words.
KILLED IN WRITING — SO THEY STAY DEAD

Multipliers ("a full life day ×1.1 on your blocks") — hidden math you can't verify from a toast; the score stops being explainable at a glance.

Paying OVR directly in XP per area — option 2's self-pricing disease, six times over.

Fines and negative XP (docking for unplanned blocks, un-earning on a miss) — the first law. Punishment killed the last version of this app; it doesn't get a second audition.

Streak bonuses that reset — the previous app's corpse. Your redemption mechanic (make it up with proof) already answers this better.

The fork only you can call

What does "getting to 99 sooner" actually mean?
DOOR A · THE 99 STAYS DATE-LOCKED — RECOMMENDED

Dec 25 is the meaning.

99 on the day the run ends IS the sentence "I became him by the time the boats finished burning." Nobody — not even the perfect run — hits 99 in October, because the 99 measures the run, not a sprint.

"Sooner" then means two real things: the number climbs faster mid-run because your life finally fills the 30, and the pace date pulls back toward Dec 25 whenever you're behind. Both are honest, both are visible, both are what the options above deliver.

DOOR B · THE 99 BECOMES REACHABLE EARLY

Overshoot allowed.

Great weeks bank surplus; hit 99 on Dec 1 if you've earned it. The genuine cost: Dec 25 stops meaning anything, and the last weeks of the run go dead the moment the number maxes — the exact endgame problem that kills every game that allows it.

If you want this feeling anyway, the honest version is a second number, not the OVR: "DAYS AHEAD OF PACE +4" on the Today header. The 99 keeps its date; your surplus gets its own scoreboard.
