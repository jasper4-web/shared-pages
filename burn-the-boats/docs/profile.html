Jasper — Operator Profile

Captured from a 28-question interview, 2026-07-25. This is the layer ABOVE the goal cascade: it is what generates December, and everything downstream inherits from it. Nothing in the app should be invented that could be read from here.

Run: Mon 2026-07-27 → Mon 2026-12-01.

Two things tie for first and he refused to separate them — correctly, because they're the same thing from two angles:

The app's stated purpose, in his words: "a real tool that helps me become the man I want to be in these last five months." If it stops feeling like that, he stops caring about it. It must actively motivate and remind — "you burned the boats, it's time to work now" — not passively record.

3 · Time and energy — the real constraints

  • Peak brain: 9 AM – noon. Protect it absolutely.
  • Trades the first 1.5–2 hours of the open: 6:30–8:30 PT. Barely looks after that, so 9–noon is genuinely available. The current build has trading ending at 7:15 — that is wrong and must move.
  • Sharp mid-morning, not early-early.
  • The dog is a real daily constraint: walks and play at loose times on weekdays, longer hikes Sat/Sun. Remote work (trail, laptop) becomes possible after a week or so and gives the dog more time.
  • Week is otherwise fluid — no fixed external obligations.

The two leaks: phone/scrolling, and other people's requests. Both must be actively defended.

4 · December goals — his words, not mine

SANO — 3 clients. Anthony is #1. Not blocked on assets; blocked on process clarity. He feels "scrambled." He does not need proof-it-works (Anthony is the proof). Pricing is roughly right but needs cleanup. The onboarding/delivery process is "crowded and muddy." What he wants is a gated readiness checklist with his own sign-off: "did we check the box of having the six things — yes. Did I look over the six things and give a yes to each — yes."

Trading — 3 live funded accounts by December. A won month = stuck to the rules every day and did not double-back trade (winning in the morning, then going back greedy for more). Rule-following is the lead measure, not P&L.

5 · The two vices — a real pillar, front and centre

He named these unprompted as "one of the biggest things" and asked for them to be one of the most rewarding parts of the app. Displayed front and centre with the OVR.

  • "Unloading the dishwasher" — his codename, deliberately chosen. Do not ask what it is; use his label. Goal: curb down to roughly once a week.
  • Smoking — roll back substantially. Hard rule: nothing before 12 PM. Optional bonus: 24 hours clean = a big reward. Escalating — after hitting 24h two or three times, the bar rises to ~32h.

Danger windows: the midday relaxation break · at night before bed · and for smoking, the first 90 minutes of the day are hardest — once he gets past that, waiting until noon is easy. Note: that window is identical to his trading window. Those hours carry double load.

The slip mechanic — his design, and it's the best idea in the interview

The streak is never wiped. You pay for it instead, and the price escalates.

SlipPenance
1stRead the Bible 15 min · write out 6 passages · upload a photo as proof
2nd1.5-hour workout · 30 min Bible · passages · proof for both
3rdFinal warning — last chance to keep the streak, and the make-up is significantly harder

"Give a little leeway so it's not one mistake and you're screwed — but you do have to make up for it."

This is the direct answer to why v1 died. When he falls behind, he stops opening it. A hard reset guarantees abandonment; escalating redemption keeps him in the system while keeping real stakes.

6 · Boosts and rewards — depth without overwhelm

His constraint: "simple but still in depth — personalised for me, but not overwhelming." The solution is his: the extras are not daily obligations, they're occasional events that pay out.

  • Golf — roughly monthly, scheduled
  • Reading — occasional nightly add-on for a boost
  • Networking events — a super-boost
  • Finances — wants it in, not yet specified

Rewards are schedule privileges, not points. This is the mechanic he cares about:

  • A strong week earns a longer break next week
  • Attending a networking event earns you the right to cut a work block in half, or replace one with whatever you want

"A reward for going to a networking event isn't really a reward [if it's just points]." Earned freedom in the schedule is the currency.

Boosts: app-suggested, never punished — mixed with real earned rewards.

6b · Small wins — the daily hook (added mid-interview, his example)

*"I just did laundry and I didn't want to. That's a good thing — get a little XP. If I do laundry and

make my food every single night, then this weekend I earn a really nice restaurant date for myself."*

Two mechanics live here and both matter:

1. A small-wins logger. Quick-add for the unglamorous things that aren't on the schedule: laundry, cooking instead of ordering out, dishes, errands, saying no to something. One tap, small XP.

Why this is structurally important, not a nice-to-have: it is the only way to earn on a bad day. When he misses three blocks the day is currently pure loss — which is precisely the wall-of-failure that killed v1 and makes him stop opening the app. Small wins guarantee there is always something he can put on the board. This may be the single most valuable retention mechanic in the whole design.

2. A real-world reward ledger. Rewards are things in the world, not points — a nice restaurant date, a golf day, a night off, a purchase. He defines both the reward AND the condition (e.g. "laundry + cooked dinner every night this week → restaurant date Saturday"), then cashes it in.

This pairs with the earned schedule privileges from §6: two currencies, one buying freedom inside the schedule, one buying real life outside it.

7 · The week shape

  • Mon–Fri — full load
  • Saturday — light. Late start, one work block, set Monday up, longer dog hike
  • Sunday — SABBATH. Closed, full-screen cross, no scoring
  • Friday is where next week gets set — and critically, by tweaking the MONTHLY goal, not from scratch: "this is the monthly goal, let me tweak it and add these changes."

Monthly example he gave: "end of the first month, text another 10 business owners I know."

8b · Round 8–9 answers (the audit round)

Anthony — a ONE-DAY gate, not an open one. "If you say it's ready it should be real easy to see the six things and log off on them and then boom, send to Anthony in one day." Build the checklist with the six items visible, one-tap sign-off each, and a days-since-pitch-ready counter in plain view. Context an audit surfaced: everything has been pitch-ready since 2026-07-24 and the recorded next action is "Jasper texts Anthony." The checklist must accelerate the send, never license the delay.

The WIG: 3 SANO clients AND at least one paying full freight by Dec 1. (Three at the $1 founding offer against ~$497/mo of GoHighLevel is a December where he is poorer.)

Runway: real income needed February–March. December is the pipeline checkpoint, not the money cliff. The countdown to Dec 1 is therefore not the real deadline — show both horizons.

Where it lives: an icon on the iPhone home screen. This is the highest-leverage answer in the whole interview. Installed PWA unlocks push notifications, Wake Lock (so the screen can sleep without killing a run), durable storage and offline. It also means it MUST be hosted and installable — a local file cannot do any of it.

THE RIVAL — REDESIGNED, his framing and it's better than the original: Not a permanent 99-vs-74 deficit to close. A daily verdict.

  • Perfect day → "You acted exactly like your millionaire self today." Give him his flowers.
  • Short day → "You fell short. Tomorrow, make sure you act like him." He is explicit that the cumulative stats lagging is fine — "you have to do something good for a very long time to get there." It is the daily comparison that motivates. Winnable every day.

Off-script days: one per month. Denominator shrinks, no misses, streak intact.

Completion: only the focus timer counts — with contingencies he named:

  • A 75-min block may be split into two rounds with a ~10-minute break between
  • 4 phone looks per BLOCK (not 2 per day), ~2 minutes each — because some phone checks are the work

Notifications: morning, evening, AND when behind. He wants to be fired up, not merely tracked. Lock-screen presence requested — deliverable as rich push notifications showing the current block and task. A true persistent lock-screen widget needs a native iOS app and is out of scope for now.

Timeline: wrap up today, at most half of tomorrow.

8c · Known-open questions (asked by auditors, not yet answered)

Not blockers for v1, but the build should not pretend it knows these:

  • Actual bedtime / sleep hours · caffeine timing
  • Alcohol — is there a third vice, and does it trigger the other two
  • The trading rules, in writing. There is no trading material anywhere on his machine — instrument, account size, risk per trade, daily loss limit, what "funded account" means (prop-firm evals?)
  • Delivery load: how many blocks/week a live client consumes once Anthony signs. The schedule currently assumes zero delivery load, which becomes arithmetically impossible around October
  • 50 calls/day (his own SANO doctrine) vs 12 conversations/week (what the app says) — a 20× gap
  • He is Pacific, every lead is Central: Block 4 at 14:50 PT = 16:50 CT and can never be an outreach block
  • Thanksgiving, Labor Day and market-closed days are currently counted as ordinary full-load weekdays
  • Current bodyweight and any injury — this gates the 2nd-slip penance (a 1.5-hour workout)

9 · Hard design rules

  1. Never render a wall of failure. He stops opening it. This is the single governing constraint.
  2. Daily interaction stays under a minute.
  3. Score execution, not results (12 Week Year). 85% = a won week.
  4. XP only rises. HP can fall. Daily loss floor −15.
  5. Streaks are never wiped — they are redeemed through escalating penance.
  6. The fullscreen locked timer is general-purpose, not just for deep work. Bible reading gets it too.
  7. Blocks are windows with candidates, not rigid slots — "you have this much time, here's what could fill it."
  8. The vices sit front and centre. The dishwasher keeps its codename.
  9. Nutrition, and anything he said to stay out of, stays out.
