THE GOALS PAGE — AUDIT AND PROPOSED STRUCTURE

Read this on the phone. It has three parts: what is actually broken, what the real systems do, and the structure I want to build. The first part is the one that matters, because the page does not have a design problem. It has a wiring problem.

PART 1 · THE DIAGNOSIS

The one-sentence version

The Goals page is a beautiful map of intention with almost nothing connected underneath it. It draws four goals across four dated landmarks and a week node, and every one of those elements is display-only. Nothing on this page can currently cause a single hour of your Tuesday to be spent differently.

I did not conclude that from looking at it. I read the code. Here is the evidence.

1 · A weekly commitment has no goal attached to it

This is the root cause of almost everything else. A commitment is stored as a bare string:


week.tactics.b1 = "Call 10 owners from the list"

There is no goalId. The app has no idea that block belongs to SANO. It cannot know. So it cannot count toward SANO, cannot warn you when SANO gets nothing for a week, and cannot tell you at the end of the run where your 91 days actually went.

2 · Because of that, the orphan check is broken

The page has a feature called THE ORPHAN CHECK, and it is the single best idea on the page — flag a ranked goal that got nothing this week, so neglect is a decision rather than an accident. Here is how it decides:


!Object.values(tac).some(t => t && t.toLowerCase()
   .includes(x.short.toLowerCase().split(' ')[0]))

It string-matches the goal's name against the text you typed. In the state I screenshotted, B1 is "Call 10 owners from the list" and B2 is "Anthony follow-up + send the deck" — both unambiguously SANO work. Neither contains the letters "sano", so the app reports SANO as orphaned. The one mechanism designed to catch drift produces a false alarm on the most common possible input, which means within two weeks you will learn to ignore it.

3 · There is no when, so these are not plans

The strongest finding in the goal literature is implementation intentions — an if-then plan with a specific trigger beats a plain intention by a medium-to-large margin (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 94 studies). The requirement is a when.

A commitment here is attached to a block slot (b1–b4) at the week level. Not to Tuesday. Not to 09:00. "Call 10 owners" is pinned to "the first deep block", of every day, indefinitely. That is an intention with a category, not an if-then plan. week.grid does not exist in the codebase. Neither does any day binding.

4 · Milestones are decoration

Four landmarks × four goals = sixteen milestone slots. In the live state most read "Nothing set." More importantly: nothing consumes a milestone. Marking one HIT changes no schedule, no score, no week, no prompt. msState() renders NEXT / PASSED / HIT beautifully and then the value is dropped on the floor.

A dated picture you can't act on is a mood board.

5 · There is no cadence, which is the actual engine of every system that works

4DX — the framework closest to this app — has four disciplines. The page implements two of them (a WIG, and lead/lag language). It implements neither of the two that produce the behaviour: a compelling scoreboard the player owns, and a weekly cadence of accountability.

Searched the codebase: friday → 0 matches. confidence → 0. There is no moment where you close last week's commitments and make next week's. The spec your goals agent wrote designed exactly this — a 90-second Friday ritual — and it was never built.

Without a cadence, a goal system is a wish with typography.

6 · Lead measures are strings, not counters

Each goal carries a lead measure. Here is SANO's, verbatim from the data:


lead: 'Outreach conversations'

It is a label. Nothing counts outreach conversations. The lag measure (3 clients) is tracked; the lead measure — the thing that is predictive, controllable, and the entire point of the 4DX distinction — is decorative text. You are shown the number you cannot move and told the name of the number you can.

7 · There is no capacity model, and the arithmetic already fails

capacity → 0 matches in the codebase. A "of 20 blocks" bar appears on Fridays only, and is derived from a count of four slots, so it can never exceed 4/20.

Meanwhile your own profile flags the real problem: the schedule assumes zero client delivery load, and once Anthony signs, that assumption becomes arithmetically impossible around October. Four goals, sixteen milestones, 2–3 working hours a day, and nothing anywhere in the system that says no.

8 · Nothing absorbs a bad week

The top of the page says BURN THE BOATS / become someone who executes. That is the right idea and it is static text. On a week you lost, it does nothing — and the week you lose is the only week the design has to survive, because your stated failure mode is that you stop opening the app.

The synthesis

Nobody serious uses one of these. What the good operators actually run is a stack, where each layer has a different job and a different review cadence, and — the part everyone gets wrong — the layers are not all scored. OKR without a cadence is a spreadsheet. 4DX without a scoreboard the player owns is a meeting. 12WY without a theme layer is a machine for quitting in week nine.

PART 3 · THE STRUCTURE I WANT TO BUILD

Five layers. Each has one job, one review cadence, and an explicit answer to "is this scored?"

L1 · THE VOW — never scored, never fails

Become someone who executes. Two horizons always visible: Dec 1 (pipeline) and Mar 1 (income). Reviewed never. Its only job is to be the thing still standing on a week you lost. This is the layer that keeps you opening the app.

L2 · DECEMBER — 4 outcomes, 1 WIG, reviewed monthly

Ranked, one WIG (3 clients with at least one at full freight). Each carries three fields that do not exist today:

  • Objective — the qualitative sentence (have it)
  • Evidence — what would prove it. Not "in talks with a kitchen" but "a reply from a named kitchen in my inbox." Derived, never typed twice.
  • Obstacle — the WOOP field. What will actually stop this.

Does not appear in the weekly ritual. GTD cadence rule. You touch it at month turns.

L3 · THE LANDMARK — a dated picture every 4 weeks

What you have (Aug 31 / Sep 28 / Oct 26 / Nov 23), with three changes:

  1. Every milestone must name its evidence, or it can't be saved.
  2. SANO's first landmark is a learning milestone — the delivery process is written down and I have signed off on each of the six things — and SANO shows no client-count target until that is done. This is Locke & Latham applied to the exact thing your profile says you're blocked on: process clarity, not assets.
  3. Dates default to month boundaries (fresh-start effect) but stay freely editable.

L4 · THE WEEK — the commitment layer. This is the missing engine.

A commitment stops being a string and becomes a real object:


{ id, goalId, text, day:'tue', block:'b1', done:0, confidence:null }

That one change unlocks everything:

  • The orphan check becomes true instead of a string guess
  • Lead measures become countable — because now the app knows which goal a block served
  • It becomes an if-then plan — a goal, a day, and a block is exactly Gollwitzer's form
  • The end-of-run answer becomes possible — where did 91 days actually go

Plus three mechanics on top:

  • Capacity is a hard cap. 20 deep blocks/week. The composer refuses the 21st. Kanban WIP limit + Burkeman fixed volume. This is the "no" the system currently lacks.
  • Pay yourself first. The WIG gets the 09:00–noon block by default and you have to actively take it away. Your peak brain is a scarce asset being spent on residue.
  • The Friday ritual, ≤90 seconds, never blank. Three taps: close out last week's commitments → one confidence tap per goal (1–5, never scored) → accept or edit next week, pre-filled from the current landmark. The week interprets the landmark; it does not re-derive it.

L5 · THE BLOCK — Tuesday, 09:00

The if-then made real. Already works. It just needs to receive a commitment that knows what it's for.

The three mechanics that make it stick

  1. Never miss twice. A missed commitment costs nothing. Two in a row on the same goal triggers one prompt with three buttons: shrink it · move it · park it. Never "you failed." This is the direct countermeasure to the effect that killed v1.
  2. Confidence, not percentage. On Friday, one tap per goal: will this land by Dec 1? The trend of that number across 18 weeks is the most useful signal in the whole app, and it is honest in a way a progress bar never is. It is never scored and never touches OVR.
  3. Parking is a first-class state. A goal can be parked with a date. Parked ≠ failed, and parking never zeroes a counter — because a zeroed counter is a manufactured lapse.

What the page looks like after this

Same spine, four changes:

  • The week node moves to the top and gets big. It is the only layer you act on daily. Right now it's a peek box above a wall of things you can't do anything about today.
  • Each goal shows its lead counter as the hero number, not its lag. Conversations this week: 4 of 12 — because that is the number you control. The lag (1 of 3 clients) goes small.
  • Landmarks collapse by default to one line each, and expand on tap. You are not meant to read sixteen milestones on a Tuesday.
  • The orphan check moves into the Friday ritual, where a "that's allowed but decide it" prompt belongs, instead of firing falsely on the daily surface.

Everything else above I can build without you.

BUILD ORDER

  1. Typed commitments (goalId + day) — nothing else works without it, and it fixes the orphan check for free
  2. The Friday ritual — the cadence; this is what turns the page from a map into an engine
  3. Lead counters — one per goal, derived from typed commitments
  4. Capacity cap + pay-yourself-first — the "no"
  5. Evidence + obstacle fields on December goals
  6. SANO learning milestone takes precedence over client counts
  7. Never-miss-twice + parking + confidence
  8. Page re-layout — week to the top, landmarks collapsed, lead as hero

Steps 1 and 2 are about 80% of the value. Everything after is refinement.
