THE GOAL SYSTEM — HOW WE BUILD IT

The brief changed and it changed correctly: I stop supplying goals and start supplying the machine you plug them into. So this document is not about your goals. It is about the shape of the thing that holds them, and it has to be right before a line of it gets merged, because the shape is the part you cannot cheaply change later.

Three parts: how other people build these, the eight decisions that matter, and the plan.

PART 1 · HOW THIS IS ACTUALLY BUILT ELSEWHERE

Last time I looked at goal methodologies — OKR, 4DX, 12 Week Year. That was the wrong axis for this question. What matters now is how the software is structured, which is a different literature. Six patterns, and each one settles something for us.

Linear — the three-layer model, and time as a separate axis

Linear runs Initiative → Project → Issue, and then does something important: Cycles are a separate container. An issue belongs to a project and a cycle at the same time. Time is not a level in the tree — it is a second axis crossing it. Their own docs describe the three-level hierarchy as deliberately avoiding "the complexity of Jira's epic-story-subtask pyramid."

Settles: how deep to go, and — the big one — that a horizon must not be a level in the hierarchy.

Things 3 / GTD / PARA — Areas and Projects are different kinds of thing

Every one of these systems draws the same line, and it is sharper than it first looks:

A project has a desired outcome that lets you mark it complete, and a timeframe.

An area of responsibility has a standard to be maintained, continuous over time.

Projects end. Areas continue indefinitely.

PARA adds the organising principle: sort by actionability, not by topic. Archive is not "old", it is "not actionable until it is again."

Settles: Areas and Goals are not the same object at different depths. An Area has no due date and can never be "done". A Goal must have both.

Notion — relations and rollups, and the ceiling nobody mentions

Notion is the most flexible goal-structure tool there is: any database can relate to any other, and rollups aggregate across the relation. But there is a hard limit — a rollup only reaches one hop. There are no rollups of rollups.

Settles: deep hierarchies do not aggregate cleanly even in the tool built for aggregating. If the most flexible system on the market caps at one hop, a phone app for one person should not attempt a computed tree. This independently confirms EOS's rule: dated pictures, never a computed tree.

Beeminder — the akrasia horizon, and this is the best idea I found

Beeminder lets you change any goal parameter you like. But the changes are asymmetric:

You can't make your goal easier any earlier than 7 days from today.

Making it harder takes effect immediately.

Changing the rate, adding breaks, quitting, lowering the stakes — all of it takes a week. Making it tougher is instant. It is explicitly designed against hyperbolic discounting: the version of you at 4pm on a bad Thursday should not be able to rewrite what the version of you on Sunday committed to.

Settles: the central tension in your ask. You want to add, change and edit freely. A system you can freely soften is a system that cannot hold you. The answer is not to restrict editing — it is to make easing visible and slow, and hardening instant.

Bullet Journal — migration as a deliberate filter

Every month you rewrite by hand anything still open. The friction is the entire point:

If a task isn't worth rewriting, it probably isn't worth doing.

If something keeps getting shuffled forward, that friction is the system filtering out

things burning your energy without going anywhere.

Settles: goals must not auto-roll to the next horizon. The month turn should make you re-decide.

The one-database-many-views pattern

Notion, Airtable, Coda and Tana all converge here: one table, several types, many views — rather than separate tables per concept. Fewer objects to learn, and a new view costs nothing.

Settles: outcome / learning / habit are a kind field on one Goal table, not three tables.

PART 2 · THE EIGHT DECISIONS

For each: the options, what I recommend for this project, and why.

D1 · How deep does the hierarchy go?

OptionVerdict
Area → Goal → CommitmentCHOSEN
Area → Goal → Sub-goal → CommitmentRejected — the Jira pyramid. Four levels means every entry is a filing decision.
Flat list + tagsRejected — you have genuinely separate businesses; they need a wall between them.

Three levels, plus an optional parent link on a goal ("this is a step toward that"). The ladder exists when you want it and costs nothing when you don't. Critically the parent link is navigational only — a parent's progress is never computed from its children. Linear caps at three; Notion can't roll up past one hop; EOS says dated pictures. All three point the same way.

D2 · Is a horizon a level, or an attribute? — the decision the whole thing turns on

A horizon is a named date. A goal points at one. That's it.

This is exactly why the app can't do what you asked today. Right now the four landmark dates are computed in code from weeks 6/10/14/18, and the goals are six hardcoded slots. Adding a two-month horizon is a code change. Adding a fifth business is a code change.

Make the date an attribute and all of it becomes data: monthly, two-month, December, a one-off date for a single goal, whatever you want, created from the phone in about eight seconds. This is Linear's cycles — time crossing the hierarchy rather than being part of it.

D3 · Are Areas and Goals the same kind of thing?

No, and the difference is load-bearing.

AreaGoal
Ends?NeverYes — has a date and a done state
ExampleSANO · Fruits · Personal"Three clients signed by Dec 1"
CarriesAn ongoing standard (a weekly quota)An outcome and its evidence
CountFew, stableMany, churning

An Area can carry a standing weekly quota — that is how "gym 2×, walks 3×" should live, attached to Personal rather than pretending to be a dated goal. That is Things/GTD/PARA's line, and it is the reason your Personal thread currently feels different from the other three: it is different.

D4 · One goal type or several?

One Goal table, a kind field: outcome · learning · habit.

  • outcome — the normal case. Date, evidence, done state.
  • learning — for a thing you have never done. It is complete when the method is written down and signed off, not when the result lands. (Locke & Latham: an outcome goal on a novel complex task causes tunnel vision and blocks learning the method.)
  • habit — no date, no done state, just a weekly quota. Lives under an Area.

Three behaviours, one object, one editor. The one-database pattern.

D5 · How do edits stay safe? — your explicit requirement

You must be able to add, change, edit, reorder, re-date and delete. Nothing gets locked. But:

The asymmetric rule (from Beeminder), in the lightest form that works:

ChangeEffect
Add a goal · make a target harder · pull a date earlierInstant, silent
Lower a target · push a date later · park itInstant, but recorded — the goal keeps a one-line trail: "target 12 → 8 · day 34"
DeleteInstant, and it takes its commitments with it
Anything at allNever zeroes a counter

No lockouts, no waiting, no money. Just memory. If you push a date three times, the goal says so, and that is information you want rather than a punishment.

Open question for you: Beeminder uses a hard 7-day delay on any easing. That is meaningfully stronger, and meaningfully more annoying. I have specced the visible-trail version because you said efficiency matters — but say the word and I'll build the delay instead.

D6 · What stops it becoming a junk drawer?

The migration ritual, at each horizon turn. Goals do not auto-roll.

When a date passes, that horizon's unfinished goals come up one screen at a time, four buttons each:

CARRY (pick the next date) · CHANGE IT · PARK · DROP

The friction is the feature. A goal you have carried three times gets a quiet marker — that is the system telling you it is burning energy without moving. This is bullet-journal migration, and it is the only mechanism in this document whose whole job is removal.

D7 · What's typed vs derived?

Derived — never typed, never stored: lead count this week · days to date · orphan status · capacity used · habit quota progress · carried-count.

Typed: everything else.

One hop only. A parent goal never computes from its children — it shows "3 steps · 1 done" as navigation, and its own state stays something you assert. Notion's ceiling, adopted on purpose.

D8 · What do you touch, and how often?

GTD's rule: different altitudes get different review cadences. December goals must not appear in the weekly ritual. The Goals tab is not a daily surface, and building it as one is what makes goal apps feel like homework.

PART 3 · THE MODEL


areas      id · name · colour · rank · quotas[] · archived
horizons   id · label · date
goals      id · areaId · horizonId? · parentId?
           title · kind(outcome|learning|habit)
           evidence? · obstacle? · lead{name,target}?
           wig · rank · state(active|parked|done|dropped)
           history[]  ← the edit trail
commits    id · goalId · text · day · block · done

Five fields do the work the current app cannot do at all:

  1. areaId — any number of businesses, created by you
  2. horizonId — any number of dates, created by you
  3. parentId — the ladder, optional, never computed
  4. commits.goalId — the missing link. This is what makes the orphan check true, lead measures countable, and a commitment an actual if-then plan
  5. history[] — how editing stays honest without being restricted

PART 4 · THE PLAN

Phase 1 · The spine (the prototype already proves this works)

Areas, horizons, goals, full CRUD, three views. Nothing hardcoded. → Already built and on your phone. Test it with real content.

Phase 2 · Migrate your live data — losslessly

Map the six fixed domains to Areas, their lag/lead to Goals, the sixteen milestone slots to dated Goals. Write it to a new key, keep the old one untouched, and provide a one-tap revert. Nothing is destroyed until you say the new shape is right.

Phase 3 · Typed commitments + the Friday ritual

The single highest-value change in the whole document. Commitments carry goalId and a day, the orphan check becomes true, and Friday gets a ≤90-second close-out → confidence tap → next week, pre-filled from the current horizon.

Phase 4 · Standards and quotas on Areas

Weekly quotas move to where they belong. Personal stops pretending to be a dated goal.

Phase 5 · The migration ritual

Month-turn screen. Carry / change / park / drop. Carried-count marker.

Phase 6 · The edit trail

history[] written on every ease. One line on the goal card.

Phase 7 · Merge into the app

Replace renderPush(). The Goals tab becomes this. Old routes keep working.

Phases 1–3 are ~80% of the value. Everything after is refinement.
