The Furnace · five strips

What sits under the plate

The Furnace header is locked. What changes is the strip beneath it — currently two half-empty chips. Every version below carries six live readouts instead of two: both vices, the three December results, and the week. All of them are built in the furnace language — stamped metal, heat, no visible flame.

01

The Gauge Cluster

Six instrument dials machined into the plate

Each reading becomes a small arc gauge — a needle sweep stamped into its own recess, heat pooling underneath the ones that are running hot. Reads like the instrument panel of something built to work, which is exactly the furnace's argument.

The move
A dial is read by its shape, not its number — you know a gauge is low before you've read the digits. Six of them is a cockpit sweep, not six things to parse.
Why it fits the Furnace
The header argues that this is machinery. Gauges are the only strip here that continue that argument rather than sitting beneath it.
The cost: an arc at 52px wide can only carry a rough proportion. "3 of 3 clients" and "0 of 3" look more alike than they should, so the digits are doing more work than the dials — which slightly undermines the whole idea.
02

The Stamped Plate

One continuous machined slab · cells cut into it

Not chips at all — one solid plate, divided by engraved seams and pinned with rivets. Dormant values are stamped into the metal in shadow; live ones are lit from within. The header and the strip become a single piece of hardware.

The move
It removes the chips entirely. Your complaint was half-empty boxes — this deletes the boxes and keeps only engraved seams, so there is no wasted padding anywhere.
The detail that sells it
Values you haven't earned are stamped in shadow, the same colour as the plate. They only light when they're real. Zero becomes a physical absence rather than a red number.
The cost: it's the most uniform, so nothing shouts. The vices you said should be front and centre get exactly the same visual weight as the week counter. Coherent, but flat in the hierarchy.
03

The Thermocouples

Molten slots · doing well literally runs hot

Each metric is a slot cut into the plate with molten metal filling it. Progress isn't a bar, it's temperature — the further you are, the brighter and hotter the channel glows. The furnace's own language turned into the data encoding.

The move
Heat is the metric. The header glows because there's a furnace behind it; here the glow means you are running hot. It's the only version where the aesthetic and the data are the same thing.

My read

All five carry six readouts instead of two and all five speak the furnace's language. I measured each one's height on a phone before ranking them — which changed my answer.

01

The Stamped Plate my pick — after measuring

I originally picked the Forge Tiles. Then I measured: this carries the same six readouts in 130px against 273px — less than half the height — and it's the most complete answer to your actual complaint, because it deletes the boxes entirely rather than making them prettier. The detail that wins it is that values you haven't earned are stamped into the metal in shadow instead of shown as red zeros. "0 of 3 accounts" becomes a physical absence rather than a failure. Its one weakness is hierarchy — so graft the Forge Tiles' idea onto it and give the two vices a full-width row across the top. That hybrid is what I'd build.

02

The Forge Tiles best looking, twice the height

Visually the strongest — the bevels and the heat pooling in the lit recesses genuinely look cast in the same foundry as the header, and the vices leading full-width is the hierarchy you asked for. It loses on cost: 273px for six numbers is a lot of your first screen, and the header above it is already doing the emotional work.

03

The Thermocouples smartest idea

Making heat the unit of measurement is the cleverest thing on this page — the aesthetic and the data become the same thing, and at 150px it's compact. I'd have picked it if a cold channel on a bad week didn't read quite so much like dead machinery, on the exact mornings that matter most.

04

The Gauge Cluster most on-theme

Dials are the most furnace-native object here. But at 52px an arc can't meaningfully separate 1-of-3 from 0-of-3, so the digits end up doing the work the dials were supposed to do — and it still costs 192px.

05

The Control Board most rows, least feeling

Eight readouts and the status lamps are excellent — one dot carries a whole verdict. Last because it turns the top of your app into admin, which is the opposite of what the furnace header exists to do.
