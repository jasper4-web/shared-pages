Round 3 · The Veil

The character sheet,
five ways to build a world around it

The character sheet is the core in all five — that's settled. What changes is what the rest of the page is, and how the veil tears. Every mockup below is running the flicker live right now on a 9-second cycle. Watch one for a few seconds before you read anything.

Both new mechanics — the veil and the phone pass — are specified once here and carry into whichever direction you pick.

◈ The Veil — piercing it

Your sheet is what's real. Every 9 seconds a scanline sweeps and he bleeds through — same layout, same positions, his numbers. It holds for about a second and a half, stutters, and collapses back to you.

The rule that makes it work: it is never a clean fade. It stutters, drops a frame, comes back wrong. A smooth crossfade reads as a UI transition. A broken one reads as something showing through that isn't supposed to be visible yet.

Two guardrails I'd insist on: a hold-to-pierce gesture so you can summon it deliberately instead of only waiting, and an off switch. A thing that flickers at you every nine seconds for four months is either haunting or maddening, and which one it is will depend on the day.

◈ The Phone Pass — Forest, fixed

Forest's rule is absolute: leave, tree dies. That's too rigid for real work — sometimes the phone is the job. So the run gets two passes a day.

  1. Tap STEP AWAY before you leave. The run pauses, a 2:00 countdown starts.
  2. Come back inside the window → resumes clean. No penalty, no lost bonus.
  3. Blow the window → the run breaks like normal. The block still counts, the bonus doesn't.
  4. Leave without tapping → instant break. No grace.

That last rule is the whole design. The pass costs you one deliberate tap, which is exactly the friction that separates I need to check something from I drifted onto my phone. Two per day keeps it honest.

The Veil

One screen · no scrolling · the character is the entire interface

Everything on one screen. Your rating lives at the centre of a ring, and the day's blocks are the ring itself — a clock face made of your schedule. Completed blocks light the arc. Nothing scrolls, nothing is behind a tab. You open it and the whole state of your life is one glance.

The signature move
The schedule became the ring. Your day isn't a list under the character — it's the circle the character sits inside. Each block is an arc that lights when you finish it, so a good day literally closes a halo around you.
Why this one is different
It's the only direction with no scrolling and no navigation. Everything is one composition. That's the highest possible bar for a daily driver — you open it at 6 AM half-awake and there is nothing to find.
How the veil reads here
He bleeds through the centre of the ring — the 74 becomes a 99 inside your own halo. Most concentrated version of the effect, because there's nowhere else on screen for your eye to go.
The cost: one screen means hard limits on detail. Trade log, weekly review, history — none of it fits without a second view, which undercuts the "one screen" purity that makes it good. You'd be choosing elegance over capacity, and you said you want room to do a lot in there.
Veil treatment · centred, unavoidable — the number you're staring at becomes his number.

Dense multi-panel · everything visible at once · maximum capacity

A real operating surface. Character panel, live schedule, duel record, focus control, fuel, and the running ledger — all on screen simultaneously. This is the direction that actually answers "I want to be able to do a lot in there." Nothing is one tap away because nothing is hidden.

Ledger

The signature move
Zero navigation. Six live panels, one surface. This is the only direction where you can log a block, start a run, check the duel and read the record without a single tap that changes the screen.
Why it may be the right answer
You said the page shouldn't be only the character sheet and you want room to work. This is the direct answer — the character is the largest panel, not the whole world, and everything else you need is beside it rather than behind it.
How the veil reads here
Only the character panel tears. The rest of the deck stays solid, which makes the flicker feel localised and eerie — like one instrument on the dash is picking up something the others can't.
The cost: density is the enemy of feeling. This is the most capable direction and the least moving — six panels of information is a tool, not an experience, and you asked for something that makes you feel like you're upgrading. It's also the hardest of the five to make work on a phone.
Veil treatment · localised to one panel — quieter, stranger, easier to live with long-term.

The Sanctum

Vertical hub · character is the entrance, rooms below

The character sheet is the hero at the top — big, ceremonial, the first thing you see. Beneath it the page opens into rooms: Today, The Duel, The Record, The Vault. You always enter through who you are, then descend into the work. Room to grow without ever cluttering the entrance.

The Mirror

Split seam · the veil isn't a fade, it's a wipe you can drag

The most literal reading of piercing the veil. One character sheet, split by a seam you can drag with your thumb. Pull it left and more of him is revealed; let go and it snaps back to you. The flicker still happens on its own — but here you can also tear it open yourself, and hold it there.

The signature move
You control the veil. Every other direction shows him to you on a timer. Here it's a physical gesture — drag to reveal, release to snap back. Piercing the veil becomes something you do, not something that happens to you.
Why the gesture matters
An automatic flicker gets tuned out — anything on a 9-second loop becomes wallpaper by week three. A gesture never becomes wallpaper, because it only fires when you choose it. This is the version most likely to still be affecting you in November.
How the veil reads here
Both: it sweeps on its own and responds to your thumb. The auto-sweep reminds you it's there; the drag is what you'll actually use.
The cost: a permanent split means everything is half-width. Six stats in half a screen is cramped, and the schedule gets pushed into a thin strip underneath. It's the most conceptually elegant direction and the most spatially constrained.
Veil treatment · a draggable seam — the only version you control with your hands.

The Chamber

Cinematic depth · he is standing behind you in the dark

Not a sheet — a room. Your figure stands lit in the centre. Behind you, barely visible in the dark, he is already there. When the veil tears he steps forward and briefly occupies your position, then recedes. Your stats float as glass panels around the figure. This is the one that isn't a UI. It's a place.

My read

All five keep the character sheet as the core, run the veil, hold the schedule, and take the phone pass. The real decision is capacity versus feeling — and they're genuinely opposed here.

01

The Sanctum my pick

It's the only direction that gets both. The character sheet stays ceremonial and full-size at the entrance — you meet yourself before you meet the work — and the rooms below give you unlimited space to grow without ever crowding that entrance. It takes the veil's most dramatic treatment and it's the one I'd bet on still being good in November. The scroll is a real cost, solved by making Today the first room so it's always half-visible.

02

The Mirror best single idea

The draggable seam is the strongest mechanic in this round, because it's the only one that can't become wallpaper. An automatic flicker is tuned out by week three; a gesture is not. I want the drag-to-pierce in whatever we build, even if you don't pick this. Loses only because a permanent half-screen split starves everything else.

03

The Chamber highest ceiling, highest risk

If it lands, it's the best thing on this page by a distance — "he was always standing behind you" is a genuinely better idea than any flicker. But atmosphere at 80% quality is just a dark screen, and it's the least room for a schedule. Pick this if you want to gamble on feeling over function.

04

The Command Deck most capable

Genuinely the best answer to "I want to do a lot in there" — six live panels, zero navigation. I've ranked it fourth only because you keep telling me the thing you want is to feel the progress, and density is the enemy of that. Worth reconsidering if what you actually want is a cockpit you sit in all day.

05

The Veil most elegant, least room

The schedule-as-halo is a beautiful idea and the single-screen discipline is the right instinct for 6 AM. It's last because you explicitly said you want room to do a lot, and this is the direction with the least. The closing-halo mechanic deserves to survive into the Sanctum's Today room.
