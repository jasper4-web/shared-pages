Agent 1 · Burn the Boats crew 2026-07-25. Companion to out-1-goals-research.md. Written against index.html (v3, 1825 lines), GOAL-ENGINE.md and PROFILE.md.

This spec is implementable as written. Every structure is given as code, every function that changes is named with its current line behaviour, and nothing is left for the GM to invent. 0 · What is actually broken today (the case for all of this)

Read the current build and the goal layer is three fields deep and stops:


S.december = { WORK:{lag,lead,at,target,unit}, CAPITAL:{...}, ... }   // 6 rows, flat
week().tactics = {b1:'…', b2:'…', b3:'…', b4:'…'}                     // 4 free strings, no owner
day().prio     = ['…','…','…','…']                                    // 4 free strings, no owner

Six concrete consequences:

  1. The cascade is a naming convention, not a structure. GOAL-ENGINE.md states "a level may only ever be justified by the level above it… a weekly tactic that serves no December goal is deleted, not done." Nothing in the code can evaluate that sentence, because a tactic is a string with no goalId.
  2. No milestones exist. The owner's central ask — a December goal, then a two-month goal, then a three-month goal — has no place to live at all.
  3. Nothing can be restructured, because there is nothing structured. Editing a December goal is one saveGoal() call that overwrites a string.
  4. g.at is a number he types about himself. saveGoal() (line 1510) writes parseFloat($('gAt').value) straight into the record. Three of six goals are at:30 / target:100 unit:'%' — that is a mood with a percent sign. The scoreboard test ("can I tell at a glance if I am winning?") cannot be passed by a self-reported number.
  5. week().theme is a hardcoded four-item dropdown (['SANO','TRADING','LA Edible','Admin + follow-ups'], line 1783). LA Edible therefore has no goal object anywhere — it lives as S.push.kitchen, an integer.
  6. A goal with nothing scheduled against it is invisible. There is no query in the app that could even ask the question.

The Friday ritual, today, is four dropdowns that set a business label. That is the whole of "set next week."

1 · THE MODEL

1.1 Design decisions taken before the code

D1 · A domain is a TAG, not a container. This is a deliberate deviation from the brief's "a goal per life category across the six domains" and the GM should know it is deliberate. His real portfolio is SANO · Trading · LA Edible · body · faith · people · mind — seven things, and WORK holds two of them. Forcing one goal per domain is what pushed LA Edible out of the goal model and into S.push.kitchen as a loose integer. So: many goals may share a domain; each domain has exactly one primary goal for the places that need a single row (the character sheet, the radar labels). Domains keep doing their real job — routing block completions into domainProgress() — untouched.

D2 · Goals are ranked, not equal. 4DX's ≤2-WIG rule, honestly applied. rank:1 is the WIG (SANO), rank:2 the runner-up (trading). Rank is the only input the block layout needs to implement Burkeman's "pay yourself first": the WIG gets the 09:00–12:00 peak window. Six co-equal goals cannot express the profile, which is explicit that SANO is priority 1.

D3 · Progress is a derived ledger, never a typed number. at becomes sum(entries[].delta). Editing a goal's text or target can then never move its progress — which is what makes restructuring safe (§3).

D4 · kind distinguishes outcome / learning / habit. From Locke & Latham (research §7d): a performance-outcome goal on a novel complex task produces tunnel vision, and "3 SANO clients" is a performance goal on a task he has completed zero times. The profile's own diagnosis — not blocked on assets, blocked on process clarity; "scrambled"; the process is "crowded and muddy" — is the textbook signature. So SANO carries a learning milestone before its outcome milestones, and habit goals (faith / people / mind) carry no fake percentage at all: their progress is the existing quota board.

The cliff is the other half of PROFILE §8b: "December is the pipeline checkpoint, not the money cliff… show both horizons." The Push tab already renders ${workdaysLeft()} TO DEC 1 · INCOME BY MAR (line 1614) as a hardcoded string — it becomes this field.

2.2 Why not more tiers, and why not fewer

  • Why not EOS's four-rung ladder (10yr/3yr/1yr/quarter): 18 weeks. A 3-year picture is unanswerable and a quarterly-only re-plan gives exactly two chances to notice the plan is wrong across the whole run. The owner's own words reject it: "a system instead of a linear path that we wrote down and can't really be changed."
  • Why not the 12 Week Year's flat two-tier (12-week goal → weekly tactics): it deletes the month, which is right, but it leaves an 18-week goal with no intermediate object — and an intermediate object is literally what he asked for.
  • Why not GTD's six altitudes: five of them are answered better in PROFILE.md than any in-app form. What GTD contributes is not a ladder but a cadence rule, applied below.
  • Why milestones are variable-count and not a fixed "2-month goal / 3-month goal" pair: he named those in a loose order mid-interview, and hard-coding two tiers would (a) force every goal to have exactly two, which is absurd for "gym 2× a week", and (b) rebuild the rigidity he explicitly complained about. A goal may have 0 to 5 milestones. Zero is legal and normal for habit goals.

2.3 The cadence rule — the single most load-bearing line in this section

Higher tiers are reviewed less often, and T1 never appears in the weekly ritual. (GTD's actual contribution; see research §5.)


const CADENCE = {

  T1: 'monthly + on demand',     // a prompt at a landmark. NEVER a field in the Friday flow.
  T2: 'at its landmark, or when hit',
  T3: 'every Friday',
  T4: 'every morning at 08:30',

Put December goals in the Friday form and two things happen: the 90-second ritual becomes a ten-minute one, and a goal that should be stable starts drifting every seven days. He can still edit a December goal at any moment — from the goal sheet, in two taps — it simply is not asked for weekly.

2.4 The landmark dates — proposed, never enforced

Milestone dates are free text-dates. But the app pre-fills from this list, because the fresh-start effect is real and measurable (Dai/Milkman/Riis — gym visits and goal commitments spike after temporal landmarks; research §7c), and a Monday that is also a month-turn is a double landmark:


/* Mondays, ≈4–5 weeks apart, each at or adjacent to a month boundary. Weeks 6 · 10 · 14 · 18. */
const LANDMARKS = ['2026-08-31','2026-09-28','2026-10-26','2026-11-23'];

Four checkpoints in 18 weeks — one every ~4.5 weeks. That is deliberately between the 12WY's "the week is the new month" and EOS's 90-day Rock: short enough that the "you have 11 months to procrastinate" arithmetic never applies, long enough that a checkpoint means something. It also gives four chances to re-plan where a quarterly system gives two.

Note for the GM: 2026-11-23 is Thanksgiving week (Thanksgiving is Nov 26, 2026). PROFILE §8c already flags that holidays are currently counted as ordinary full-load weekdays. The landmark date itself is fine; the load that week is a separate open question and is not mine to fix.

3 · RESTRUCTURING — the requirement he cared most about

*"just to give a little bit of flexibility so we're not stuck in one route, and the goals actually

becomes a system instead of a linear path that we wrote down and can't really be changed."*

The design must survive this test: any tree fixed at creation is a failed design. Here is why this one is not, and exactly what happens to earned progress in each case.

3.0 The finding that makes all of this safe

domainProgress() (line 701) recomputes from S.days records every single time it is called. It reads block completions, quotas and off-script flags. It never reads S.december. The comment on line 699 says so: "Recomputed from the stored day records every time — self-healing, cannot drift."

Therefore the OVR curve is already structurally immune to goal edits. Nothing he does to a goal can move a stat, because the stats are made of days, not of goals. This is the strongest property in the existing engine and this spec's first duty is not to break it.

INVARIANT 1 — domainProgress() must never read S.goals, S.milestones or S.commits.

If a future change makes goal completion feed the radar, restructuring stops being free and the tuned

Dec-1-at-99 curve stops being tuned. This invariant is the whole reason §3 can be short.

3.1 The five safety rules


R1  NEVER DELETE. Delete sets state:'archived' + archivedOn. Rows are never removed from S.goals /
    S.milestones / S.commits. History keeps resolving; nothing can ever point at a missing id.

R2  LEDGER, NOT COUNTER. Progress is sum(entries[].delta) on an append-only array. Editing title, lead,
    unit, target, rank, domain or kind touches NO entry. Changing `target` from 3 to 2 changes the
    denominator only — the numerator is history and history does not move.

R3  WRITE-ONCE FACTS. milestone.hitOn, check.on, goal.closedOn, commit.state:'met', and every S.days
    record are write-once. Once true, no later edit may make them false.

R4  RE-PARENT FORWARD ONLY. Changing a pointer (commit.goalId, milestone.goalId) affects the LIVE record.
    Frozen weeks keep the pointer they had. Re-parenting is not retroactive because rewriting what a past
    week was for is a lie about a week he actually lived.

R5  NEVER ZERO A COUNTER ON A CHANGE. A counter zeroed by an edit is a manufactured lapse, and the
    what-the-hell effect (research §7b) says a manufactured lapse buys a real abandonment. No
    restructuring operation may reduce S.xp, w.due, w.done, a streak, or any S.days value. Ever.

3.2 Reordering and re-dating


function reorderMs(goalId, fromIdx, toIdx){          // drag, or up/down
  const g=S.goals[goalId], ids=g.milestones.slice();
  ids.splice(toIdx,0,ids.splice(fromIdx,1)[0]);
  g.milestones=ids; ids.forEach((id,i)=>S.milestones[id].order=i);
  save();                                            // hitOn untouched. Dates untouched.
}

Order and date are independent. He may legitimately want "take the first guitar class" ordered after "feel ready" while both are dated the same month, or a later-ordered milestone with an earlier date because he reshuffled. The app must not "helpfully" re-sort by date — that is the rigidity he complained about. nextMs() walks order, not by.

Re-dating a hit milestone is allowed and changes nothing about the hit: hitOn is the fact, by was the plan.

3.3 Re-parenting


function reparentMs(msId, newGoalId){
  const m=S.milestones[msId], old=S.goals[m.goalId], g=S.goals[newGoalId];
  old.milestones=old.milestones.filter(x=>x!==msId);
  m.goalId=newGoalId; g.milestones.push(msId);
  g.milestones.forEach((id,i)=>S.milestones[id].order=i);
  save();                                            // hitOn, checks, note: all preserved verbatim
}

One pointer write, because the stores are flat. This is why the model is three keyed maps and not a nested tree — a nested tree makes re-parenting a move operation with a failure mode.

Commits re-parent identically (commit.goalId), subject to R4: only commits in a non-frozen week.

3.4 Deleting a milestone that was already hit


function archiveMs(msId){
  const m=S.milestones[msId];
  m.state='archived'; m.archivedOn=today();
  save();                                            // hitOn kept. XP kept (§5.4). Nothing recomputed.
}

It leaves the live ladder immediately, and it stays in the Record as "hit Sep 12 · removed Oct 3." Deliberate: silently erasing something he actually did is the exact felt experience of a wall of failure, delivered by his own edit. Milestone XP already paid stays paid (S.paid is a run-level ledger; §5.4).

3.5 Changing a December goal in October

Two distinct operations, and the distinction matters:


/* EDIT — same goal, better words. The overwhelmingly common case. */
function editGoal(id,{title,lead,unit,target,kind,domain,short}){
  const g=S.goals[id];
  Object.assign(g,{title,lead,unit,target,kind,domain,short});
  g.editedOn=today();
  save();                                    // entries untouched, milestones untouched, XP untouched
}

/* REPLACE — a genuinely different goal. Only when he says so. */
function replaceGoal(id, next){
  const old=S.goals[id], g=newGoal(next);
  old.state='archived'; old.archivedOn=today(); old.supersededBy=g.id;
  g.rank=old.rank; g.domain=old.domain; g.primary=old.primary;
  g.replaces=old.id;
  /* Milestones do NOT auto-move. He is asked once, per milestone: bring it, or leave it behind.
     Auto-moving them silently re-purposes work he did for a different reason. */
  save();
}

What he is told, once, and it is the anti-wall sentence of the whole spec:

*You changed the December goal on Oct 14. Everything you earned before that is still yours — the XP, the

streak, the rating. The old goal is in the Record.*

That sentence is literally true under this design, and it is true because of INVARIANT 1 and R5, not because we say so.

3.6 Parking — the answer that is not failure

The profile explicitly parks things: Spanish and guitar are "casual until SANO is further along", and four whole businesses are parked until December. A model with only live/dead states cannot represent his actual life.


function parkGoal(id, weeks, why){
  const g=S.goals[id], d=new Date(); d.setDate(d.getDate()+weeks*7);
  g.parked={until:iso(d), why:why||'', on:today()};
  g.state='parked';
  save();
}

Parked goals: excluded from orphans() (§4.4), excluded from the Friday ritual, still visible on the goal list, still capable of receiving progress entries if something good happens anyway. They wake up automatically on parked.until with a single question, on a Friday, never on a Monday morning.

function chainFor(b){

  const g=c.goalId?S.goals[c.goalId]:null;

4.2 The layout — the only writer of week.grid


const WEEKDAYS=['mon','tue','wed','thu','fri'];

/* Peak first (b1,b2 = 09:00–12:00), then b3, then b4 — so slot #1 offered to the
   highest-ranked commitment is always a peak block. "Pay yourself first", in code. */
function slotOrder(){
  const out=[];
  [['b1','b2'],['b3'],['b4']].forEach(t=>WEEKDAYS.forEach(d=>t.forEach(b=>out.push([d,b]))));
  return out;                                   // 10 peak · 5 mid · 5 late
}

/* THE LAYOUT.
   R1  PAY YOURSELF FIRST — rank order takes peak slots first (Burkeman; PROFILE §3 "peak brain
       9 AM–noon, protect it absolutely").
   R2  NEVER b4 FOR ANYTHING NEEDING CENTRAL TIME — b4 is 15:05 PT = 17:05 CT and every SANO lead
       is Central (PROFILE §8c). A commit with needsCT:true is never placed in b4.
   R3  SPREAD BEFORE STACKING — deal one slot per day per commitment before doubling up, so one lost
       day never costs an entire commitment.
   R4  THE PAST IS UNTOUCHABLE — slots on a date already gone keep exactly what they had.
   Idempotent: same commits + same date in ⇒ same grid out. Safe to re-run on every render. */
function layoutWeek(wk){
  wk=wk||mondayOf(new Date());
  const w=week(wk), tk=today();
  w.grid=w.grid||{}; WEEKDAYS.forEach(d=>w.grid[d]=w.grid[d]||{});

  const live=(w.commits||[]).map(id=>S.commits[id])
    .filter(c=>c && c.state!=='dropped')
    .sort((a,b)=>rankOf(a)-rankOf(b));
  const need={};
  live.forEach(c=>{ c.slots=[]; need[c.id]=c.blocks; });

  const slots=slotOrder();
  slots.forEach(([dk,bk])=>{                                   // R4
    if(dateOf(wk,dk) < tk){
      const keep=w.grid[dk][bk];
      if(keep && need[keep]!==undefined){ need[keep]--; S.commits[keep].slots.push(dk+':'+bk); }
    } else delete w.grid[dk][bk];
  });

  const perDay=(c,dk)=>c.slots.filter(s=>s.slice(0,3)===dk).length;
  for(let cap=1; cap<=4; cap++){                               // R3: one per day, then two, …
    let moved=true;
    while(moved){
      moved=false;
      for(const c of live){
        if(need[c.id]<=0) continue;
        const s=slots.find(([dk,bk])=>
          dateOf(wk,dk)>=tk && !w.grid[dk][bk] &&
          !(c.needsCT && bk==='b4') &&                         // R2
          perDay(c,dk)<cap);
        if(!s) continue;
        w.grid[s[0]][s[1]]=c.id; c.slots.push(s[0]+':'+s[1]);
        need[c.id]--; moved=true;
      }
    }
  }
  w.overflow=Object.values(need).reduce((a,n)=>a+Math.max(0,n),0);

  /* legacy mirrors, so week().theme and week().tactics keep resolving for anything not yet ported */
  ['b1','b2','b3','b4'].forEach(bk=>{
    const c=commitFor(bk);
    if(c){ w.tactics[bk]=c.text; if(c.goalId&&S.goals[c.goalId]) w.theme[bk]=S.goals[c.goalId].short; }
  });
  save();
}

4.3 Capacity — the fixed-volume truth


/* 4 deep blocks × 5 weekdays = 20. That number does not negotiate, and committing past it is the
   arithmetic origin of a wall of failure (Burkeman's fixed-volume; research §6c).
   Saturday's single block is deliberately EXCLUDED — Saturday is light by design (PROFILE §7) and
   counting it invites planning a six-day week. */
function capacity(wk){
  const w=week(wk||mondayOf(new Date()));
  const total=WEEKDAYS.length*4;
  const committed=(w.commits||[]).map(id=>S.commits[id])
    .filter(c=>c&&c.state!=='dropped').reduce((a,c)=>a+c.blocks,0);
  return {total, committed, free:Math.max(0,total-committed), over:Math.max(0,committed-total)};
}

Rule — and this is a hard one, from law #1. The capacity number is a forward-looking planning number only. It is rendered on Friday while he is deciding. It is never re-rendered mid-week as a shortfall, never as "you delivered 11 of 18." Delivery is what the execution score is for, and two competing shortfall numbers is two walls instead of one.

4.4 THE ORPHAN CHECK — the thing nothing in the app does today

function weeksSinceFed(goalId, from){
  let n=0, k=from||mondayOf(new Date());
  for(let i=0;i<30;i++){
    const w=S.week[k];
    if(w && (w.commits||[]).some(id=>{const c=S.commits[id];
      return c && c.goalId===goalId && c.state!=='dropped'})) return n;

}

How it is surfaced — and this is where law #1 governs the design, not the data.

  • It is a question with answers, never a red banner. Three buttons, always: Give it a block · Park it — on purpose · It's done, close it. Parking is a legitimate, non-penalised answer; an orphan that has been parked is silent.
  • It lives on the week / Record surface, never on Today. The Today tab is a ≤60-second surface and has no business asking a planning question.
  • Escalation is calm and slow: 1 week silent · 2–3 weeks one line on the goal list ("LA Edible — 3 weeks without a block") · 4+ weeks it appears once inside the Friday ritual as the fourth card. It never appears twice in a week and never goes red.
  • The precedent already exists in the app and should be matched: daysSincePitchReady() (line 1101) renders PITCH-READY 3 DAYS · NOT SENT. Same voice, same mechanic, generalised.

kind:'slack' commits (the migrated "Admin + follow-ups") carry goalId:null deliberately and are excluded from the orphan calculus in both directions — they feed nothing and they are not a goal. A week is allowed to contain slack. That is not a failure and the app must not treat it as one.

5.1 The three invariants


INVARIANT 1  domainProgress() must never read S.goals / S.milestones / S.commits.
             Goals do NOT feed the radar, the domain stats, or the OVR.
             (The curve is tuned so perfect execution hits 99 OVR on Dec 1 exactly — verified at

INVARIANT 2  recount() keeps BLOCKS as its denominator. Commitments introduce NO second denominator.

INVARIANT 3  No goal operation — create, edit, re-parent, reorder, park, archive, replace — may change

5.2 Commitment progress is a VIEW, not a score


/* Reads exactly the same rec.done[] that recount() reads. Double-counting is impossible by
   construction, not by discipline. */
function commitProgress(c){
  const hit=(c.slots||[]).filter(s=>{
    const [dk,bk]=s.split(':');
    const rec=S.days[dateOf(c.weekKey,dk)];
    return rec && rec.done && rec.done[bk];

}

8 · THE DECISION I AM LEAST SURE ABOUT

Should the week be planned in BLOCKS (a capacity budget, commit.blocks), or in SLOTS (a label per b1–b4, as today)?

This is the load-bearing choice in the entire spec. Blocks-as-currency is what produces the layout, the if-then plan, the capacity line, and — critically — the orphan detector. Take it out and §4 collapses back to a nicer version of what already exists.

The case FOR blocks (what I specified):

  • It is the only design where "a goal with nothing scheduled against it" is computable at all. Slots can tell you a block is unassigned; only blocks-as-currency can tell you a goal is unfed.
  • Implementation intentions need a when to get their d ≈ 0.65 (research §7a). "4 blocks, laid into Mon b1/b2, Tue b1, Wed b1" is an if-then plan. "SANO owns b1" is a category.
  • It makes over-commitment visible before the week rather than after. "You have committed 26 of 20 blocks" on Friday is a five-second fix; discovering it on Thursday is the wall of failure.
  • It is the only structure that will survive Anthony signing. PROFILE §8c flags that the schedule assumes zero delivery load and becomes "arithmetically impossible around October." A delivery commitment sized at 5 blocks/week makes that arrive as a number in September instead of as a crisis.

The case AGAINST (and it is not weak):

  • It adds arithmetic to a ritual whose survival depends on being short. Every deliberate design choice in this app pushes toward fewer decisions; sizing 5 commitments in blocks is 5 more decisions than picking 4 dropdown values, on a Friday afternoon when he has already worked.
  • It manufactures a second shortfall number. I have mitigated this by rule (§4.3: forward-looking only, never re-rendered as delivery), but a rule in a spec is weaker than a number that cannot exist. If Agent 2's interface shows "18 committed / 11 done" anywhere, law #1 is violated and the mitigation was paper.
  • It presumes he plans in units of blocks. Nothing in the interview says he does. He talks in outcomes — "text another 10 business owners I know" — not in block counts. The model may be imposing a planner's mental model on someone who thinks in results.
  • The auto-layout can be wrong in a way the slot model cannot. If layoutWeek() puts SANO in Tuesday b1 and he had a reason for Wednesday, he now has to fight the app. A label per slot has no opinion and therefore cannot be wrong.

Where I land, and how thin the margin is. I specified blocks, at roughly 65/35 confidence, for one reason that outweighs the rest: the orphan detector is the highest-value thing in this brief and it does not exist without them. But the failure mode is real and I want the GM to see the fallback clearly rather than discover it in week three.
