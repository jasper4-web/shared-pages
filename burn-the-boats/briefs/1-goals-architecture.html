AGENT 1 — GOALS: THE ARCHITECTURE

This is the most important lane in the crew. Goals are the structural spine of the whole app — everything else is downstream of them. Treat this as the hardest problem here and give it the effort that implies.

Mission: design the goal system — the research behind it, the data model, and above all how a December goal actually reaches a 75-minute block on a Tuesday.

Turf: agent-briefs/crew-2026-07-25/out-1-goals-research.md · out-1-goals-spec.md Depends on: nothing. Start immediately. Paired with: Agent 2 is designing the interface for this same problem, independently and at the same time. Do not coordinate with them and do not design screens. You own structure, data and logic; they own layout and feel. The GM reconciles. Divergence between you is useful, not a problem.

Why this lane carries the weight

Owner, verbatim:

"Goals really needs some extra emphasis because it's a real structure of this whole page. Me

really doing the things that I need to **revolves heavily around goals and how goals affect the daily

schedule, etc. So just want to make sure the goals really really gets that strenuous effort, data

build**, etc."

And on the shape:

"I want us to be able to make the structure of the goals for each of my different categories. For

example I can create my personal goal all the way till December of taking three guitar classes, then

my two-month goal getting so ready that I feel like I can make a guitar class, then my three-month

goal take my first guitar class… just to give a little bit of flexibility so we're not stuck in

one route, and the goals actually becomes a system instead of a **linear path that we wrote down

and can't really be changed**."

Note his horizons came out in a loose order. The number and spacing of horizons is a design question you must answer with reasoning — not a spec to copy literally.

Job

Part A — real research (required, not decoration)

You have web access. Go and find out how established systems actually structure a multi-horizon cascade, and be specific about mechanics rather than summarising philosophies. Cover at least:

  • OKR — objective/key-result split, why quarterly, how cascading works and the documented ways it fails for individuals rather than companies
  • The 12 Week Year — already partly implemented here (execution score, 85% threshold)
  • EOS / Traction — the 10yr → 3yr → 1yr → quarterly Rocks ladder, and the discipline of ≤7 Rocks
  • 4DX — WIG, lead vs lag, the ≤2 goals rule, the cadence of accountability
  • GTD horizons of focus — 50,000ft → runway, and how review cadence differs per altitude
  • At least two credible personal (not corporate) goal systems from the last few years

Write out-1-goals-research.md: what each actually does, what transfers to one person over 18 weeks, what to steal, and what to reject and why. Cite sources. Do not pad.

The app already implements 4DX lead/lag and the 12WY execution score — read GOAL-ENGINE.md first so you extend that rather than contradicting it.

Part B — the spec (out-1-goals-spec.md)

This is the deliverable that matters. It must answer, concretely:

1 · The model. A goal per life category across the app's six domains (THE WORK · CAPITAL · BODY · FAITH · MIND · PEOPLE). Give the actual JS data structure — fields, types, ids, how a goal relates to its milestones and to weekly commitments. Write it as code, not prose.

2 · The horizons. How many, spaced how, and why. Justify against the research and against a 91-weekday run ending Dec 1 (with a real income deadline in Feb–March that the app already surfaces).

3 · Restructuring — the requirement he cared most about. Reordering, re-parenting, changing or deleting a milestone mid-run, changing a December goal in October without losing history. Any design where the tree is fixed at creation is a failed design. Specify what happens to already-earned progress when a goal changes — this is the hard part and the app's OVR curve depends on it.

4 · THE CASCADE DOWN TO THE DAY — the centre of this brief. Read index.html and GOAL-ENGINE.md and specify exactly how a goal reaches a block. What exists today:

  • week().tactics{b1..b4} — the week's objective per block slot
  • week().theme{b1..b4} — which business owns each block slot
  • day().prio[0..3] — today's three, written at the 08:30 composer, which override the week
  • tacticFor(block) — resolves what a block is for
  • domainProgress() — what actually moves the six stats and therefore the OVR

Your spec must say: how does a weekly goal become week().tactics? Does it replace the composer or feed it? When he writes a weekly goal, does the app propose blocks? What happens to a goal with no block behind it — is that visible? A goal nothing is scheduled against is the single most useful thing this system could surface, and nothing in the app does it today.

5 · Scoring. How goal progress relates to the existing execution score and OVR curve without double-counting or breaking the tuned Dec-1-at-99 progression. Do not break that curve.

6 · The weekly ritual. He writes weekly goals each week. Specify the flow, what it pre-fills from, and how long it should take. There is a Friday "set next week" ritual on the Record tab already.

7 · Migration. He has live data. Specify how existing S.december goals move into the new model without loss.

  • out-1-goals-spec.md — a data model in code, all seven sections answered, unambiguous enough that the GM could implement it without inventing anything
  • You have named the one decision you are least sure about and argued both sides
