Read the correction precisely — this is the whole brief

Round 2 was told to be fun and to abandon the house aesthetic. It over-rotated into arcade, playful, goofy. Round 1 before it was too austere. Neither is right, and the target is not the midpoint.

It is a BANK. That word is doing real work. This is where he stores what he has earned, watches it accumulate, and deliberately spends it on something he wants. The feeling to design for is value held — weight, worth, permanence, a little ceremony when you take some out. Warmth and character are welcome. Whimsy for its own sake is not.

Why The Fridge worked, and use this as your tuning fork: a fridge door is a real object with genuine emotional weight — you put things on it that you are proud of, and everyone who walks past sees them. It is not a themed skin over a list. It earns its metaphor. That is the bar: a real thing from real life that already means something, not a costume.

What goofy looked like: primary colours, bouncy shapes, cartoon energy, a screen visibly trying to cheer him up. He is a grown man on day one of a hard 91-day run. It should feel like something he is pleased to walk into and spend from — not a children's game.

The process — this is a requirement, not advice

"Come up with 50 ideas. From those 50, narrow it down to the top 10."

Step 1 — generate 50. Actually write fifty candidate worlds/metaphors down in the page. Not five padded to fifty; fifty real attempts. Go far afield: places value is stored, displayed, counted, exchanged, saved, hoarded, withdrawn, earned, celebrated, kept.

Step 2 — cull in writing, and show the cull. Score every one of the fifty and kill forty. The page must contain a visible cull section listing all fifty with a one-line verdict on each. He wants to see the scrutiny, not only the survivors.

Step 3 — apply the viability filter he named. "Don't just choose silly ideas that won't actually work for this project." An idea survives only if it can genuinely carry:

  • a balance that grows all run and is legible at a glance
  • two different currencies — XP, and privileges that are earned rather than bought
  • a list of rewards with prices, conditions and locked/affordable states
  • a claim moment that feels like something
  • history — what has already been claimed
  • all of it on a 390px phone, and still good on day one at zero XP

If a metaphor collapses under any of those, it dies in the cull. Say so.

Step 4 — render the surviving 10. No two may share a primary layout primitive. Each gets a one-line "This is NOT a ___" — you have skipped this twice now; it is not optional.

What every rendition must carry

  1. XP balance — earned, never spent backwards
  2. Privileges — the second, earned-not-bought currency: "cut a block in half", "make a block yours". He was emphatic these should feel like objects you hold, not a number.
  3. Boosts — networking event · golf day · read tonight · finances hour. Pay XP + a privilege.
  4. Rewards — real things bought with XP, each carrying a condition he wrote himself. Show locked, affordable, and claimed.

Plus: day one at 0 XP must feel like promise, not poverty, and "nothing affordable yet" must not feel like a wall — that is the hardest state and it decides whether he opens this tab in week one.

Done when

  • 50 candidates written down and visibly culled, each with a one-line verdict
  • 10 rendered, no shared layout primitives, each with its "This is NOT a ___"
  • Every one carries all four content types and works at 0 XP
  • Nothing reads as goofy, cartoonish or as a children's game — and nothing retreats into the austere dark house style either
  • Lint clean, no horizontal overflow at 390px, screenshotted at 390×844, and you have looked
  • Hand-off: your top three, why, and which of the fifty you most regret killing
