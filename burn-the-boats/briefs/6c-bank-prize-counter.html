The calibration — read this before you draw anything

He has now seen the finished Prize Counter and refined his verdict:

"The only one that's kind of nice is the Prize Counter. Now that I'm looking at it, it's cool but

it's still not great. It's almost a little bit too colorful, playful, and just goofy. But I

This is a calibration, not a reversal. Round 2's brief withdrew the house-aesthetic constraint and that is what got you to a direction he likes. It stays withdrawn — do not retreat to the dark restrained language of the other tabs. But the target has moved:

Fun, not goofy. Characterful, not cartoonish.

Think a real prize counter — worn brass, aged ticket stock, a heavy mechanical dispenser, glass with fingerprints on it, hand-lettered signage that has been there for thirty years. Material, tactile, slightly nostalgic, built by adults. Not primary colours, bouncy shapes, emoji-adjacent icons or anything that reads as a children's game.

The test: it should feel like a place a grown man is pleased to walk into and spend something he earned — not a screen that is trying to cheer him up.

"A little extra functionality" — what that means

The arcade-counter idea is right; the screen underneath it is currently thin. Build it out until it can carry a real five-month run:

1 · The four content types, properly served.

  • XP — the ticket balance. Earned, never spent backwards.
  • Privileges — the second, earned-not-bought currency: "cut a block in half", "make a block yours". Earned by an 85%+ week or by logging a boost. He was emphatic these should feel like objects you hold, not a number. At a prize counter they are physical — use that.
  • Boosts — networking event · golf day · read tonight · finances hour. Pay XP + a privilege. Never scored against him.
  • Rewards — real things bought with XP, each carrying a condition he wrote himself.

2 · Things a counter must be able to do that a picture cannot.

  • Add a reward — he writes the reward, the condition in his own words, and the price. Show the flow.
  • Claim — carry forward the best of your round-1 ceremonies (The Pour / Breaking of the Seal / The Withdrawal) and make it belong to the Prize Counter's world.
  • Spend a privilege — where does that happen, and what does he see afterwards?
  • "~N days away" on a locked reward, from his real earn rate.
  • Repeatable rewards — weekly and monthly ones that re-arm rather than being consumed once.
  • History — what he has already claimed. A counter with an empty shelf behind it is a sad counter.

3 · Every state.

  • Day one — 0 XP, nothing earned, nothing claimable. Must feel like promise, not poverty.
  • Mid-run — a few claimed, one nearly affordable, privileges in hand.
  • Nothing affordable — the hardest state to make feel good. Solve it.
  • Something just became affordable — this should be a moment.
  • A privilege held and unspent.

4 · The arithmetic must work. A strong weekday pays roughly 400–490 XP. Round 1's ladder was spent out by mid-August. Propose real prices across small / mid / big / run-defining and show your working. He should be able to afford something meaningful in week one and still be reaching in November.

5 · One motion moment. Tickets, a dispense, a counter rolling — whatever the world argues for. One, done well. GSAP is at lib/gsap/.
