AGENT 6 — FINAL ROUND. THE DESIGN MUST BE IN THE PIXELS.

Turf: renditions/rend-bank-v4.html

This is the last set of renditions you will produce for this project. Three rounds have been rejected. Read the diagnosis below before you draw anything — the problem is specific and it is not effort, it is where the effort went.

The verdict

The owner's words: the Bank pages are lazy, they look like clip-art, and he wants real, curated pages. He is tired of this lane.

"Every single one that he shows me this time should be so awesome that I have a hard time choosing

between them. One out of the 10 should have very good congruency to the page we already have.

It should just actually look and feel good."

THE DIAGNOSIS — this is why three rounds failed

Your rounds have not been short of ideas, research or writing. They have been short of design.

Concretely, from your last output:

  • Each rendition is a small phone thumbnail beside a large column of prose explaining the concept. The essay is doing the work the screen should do.
  • "The Envelopes" is described as envelopes seen edge-on with thickness carrying the balance. The rendered pixels are a brown rectangle with a number in it. There are no envelopes.
  • "The Cellar," "The Counting House," "The Logbook" all render as the same vertical list of rows — icon, title, condition, price, button — with a different word at the top.
  • Your writing about the designs is genuinely good. That is exactly the problem: you are theming with words instead of with layout, form, texture and type.

The test you must pass this time:

**Cover every word of explanatory prose. Look only at the ten screens. Can you tell them apart, and

can you tell what each one IS?**

If the answer needs the caption, the rendition has failed. A metaphor that only exists in the paragraph next to the picture is not a design.

The rules for this round

1 · Mock-ups first, prose last — and small. Maximum two short lines per rendition. No "why it clears the bar," no risk analysis, no essay columns. If you want to argue for something, argue with the screen. Put the ranking and reasoning in one hand-off section at the very bottom.

2 · Render the phone at full size. Real device width, real fidelity, filling its column — not a thumbnail beside a wall of text.

3 · The metaphor must be visible without reading. If it is called The Envelopes, I must see envelopes — paper edges, thickness that varies with the balance, handwriting on the front. If it is a jar shelf, I must see jars with different fill levels. Build the object. SVG, CSS gradients, shadows, texture, transforms — the Arsenal has GSAP and tsParticles vendored and you have used neither.

4 · No two may be a vertical list of rows. At most one rendition may use that primitive. The other nine must be genuinely different objects.

5 · One must be congruent with the existing app. He asked for this explicitly: one of the ten should sit naturally beside the Furnace header, the three-dial gauge strip and the glance card already shipped in index.html. Read those and match their language — Clash Display / Satoshi / Geist Mono, the gold-on-near-black palette, engraved labels, the beam-and-plate feel. That one is the safe pick and it should be the most finished thing on the page.

6 · The other nine still must not be goofy. The calibration stands: real objects that already carry meaning, built by adults. Not cartoons, not primary colours, not children's-game energy — and equally not a retreat into flat austerity. Warm, tactile, made.

7 · Every one must be finished. Not a sketch of a direction — a screen you would ship. That is what "hard time choosing between them" requires.

The content, unchanged

Every rendition carries all four, legibly:

  1. XP balance — earned, never spent backwards
  2. Privileges — earned-not-bought tokens: "cut a block in half", "make a block yours". They should feel like objects you hold.
  3. Boosts — networking event · golf day · read tonight · finances hour. Pay XP + a privilege.
  4. Rewards — real things bought with XP, each with a condition he wrote himself. Show locked, affordable, and claimed.

Plus day one at 0 XP must feel like promise, not poverty, and one claim moment.

Done when

  • 10 finished screens, full size, high fidelity
  • The caption test passes — cover the words and they are still distinguishable and legible as objects
  • At most one vertical-list layout; nine genuinely different forms
  • One is congruent with the shipped app and is the most finished of all
  • ≤2 lines of prose per rendition; all reasoning in one hand-off at the bottom
  • Lint clean, no horizontal overflow at 390px, screenshotted at 390×844, and you have looked at the screenshots and asked yourself the caption test honestly
