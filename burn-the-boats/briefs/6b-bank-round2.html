The verdict

"None of these pages are really exciting. I really want this agent to **go back to the drawing

board and actually make this a fun page to be on**. It doesn't have to be so serious. It can

literally be super fun or colorful, or it could be super not colorful on purpose. None of them

are really fun at all… create **10 renditions that are completely opposite in every single manner of

style**. Really below par on how boring the section is."

"The only one that felt a little bit different was the Tear-Off Book — that one wasn't good, but it

felt different, so I appreciated it."

Read that last line carefully. He praised a design he thought was bad, purely because it diverged. Divergence is the currency of this round. A polished near-duplicate scores zero.

My brief caused this — the constraint is now reversed

Round 1's brief said: "Restraint still applies: dark, premium, house palette… It should look expensive, not gamified-cheap." That instruction produced seven variations of one austere idea. It is withdrawn.

The Bank is now explicitly exempt from the house aesthetic. Every other screen in this app is disciplined and dark on purpose — the Bank is the one place he goes to feel the work bought something. It is allowed to be loud, bright, warm, playful, tactile, silly, garish, retro, or aggressively plain. It does not have to look like the rest of the app. It has to be the page he wants to open.

Colour is permitted. Saturation is permitted. Light backgrounds are permitted. Only two things are not: illegibility, and looking like the other six tabs.

THE DIVERGENCE PROTOCOL — follow this literally

Round 1 converged because you designed ten answers to the same question. This time:

Step 1 — write down the three most obvious Bank designs. A list of rewards with prices. A grid of cards. A balance at the top with items beneath. Those three are banned. Do not submit them and do not submit anything a person would describe with the same sentence.

Step 2 — assign every rendition a different WORLD before you draw anything. Not a colour scheme — a reference domain from outside productivity software entirely. Ten different ones. Starting provocations, and you should reject at least half of these and find better:

  • an arcade prize counter — tickets spilling out, everything priced in tickets
  • a vending machine — you can see the thing behind the glass, you punch a code
  • a casino cage — chips, denominations, a cashier window
  • a trophy shelf — what you've already won, displayed, with gaps where the next one goes
  • a gumball / gacha machine — a capsule turn
  • a 1970s bank passbook — stamped, typewritten, brutally plain, zero ornament
  • a sticker album — spaces waiting to be filled in
  • a menu at a restaurant you can't quite afford yet
  • a claw machine
  • a loyalty punch card taken absurdly seriously

Step 3 — no two renditions may share a primary layout primitive. If one is a vertical list, none of the others may be. Not a grid, not a ring, not stacked cards, not a table, not a carousel — each is used once across the whole set.

Step 4 — for each, write one line: "This is NOT a ___." If you can't finish that sentence with something the other nine are, the rendition is dead. Replace it.

Step 5 — at least three must be visually loud. Bright, saturated, or high-contrast in a way this app has never been. At least one must be aggressively plain on purpose — his words: "super not colorful on purpose." Those are different failures of nerve if you skip them.

What every version must still carry

Non-negotiable content, however you dress it:

  1. XP balance — the earned currency
  2. Privileges — a second, earned-not-bought currency: "cut a block in half", "make a block yours". Earned by an 85%+ week or by logging a boost. He was emphatic that rewards should be schedule privileges, not points — these should feel like objects you hold.
  3. Boosts — networking event · golf day · read tonight · finances hour. Pay XP + a privilege. Never scored against him.
  4. Rewards — real things bought with XP, each carrying a condition he wrote himself ("cooked + laundry every night this week → restaurant Saturday"). Locked ones can show "~N days away".

Keep your round-1 claim ceremonies — The Pour, The Breaking of the Seal, The Withdrawal were not criticised. Carry the best one forward and improve it; do not rebuild that from scratch.

Fix from round 1: the page overflowed 390px by 24px across ~92 elements (414 vs 390 scrollWidth) — comparison-page chrome, not the mock-ups. Do not repeat it.
