Two instructions, and the second is the harder one.

"Every part of the page makes sense to the entirety"

The Vault currently exists as a strong idea rendered at concept fidelity. What it is not yet is a coherent screen where every element belongs to the same object.

This is the trap: a vault door at the top, then a list of rewards underneath that could have come from any of the other nine. That is a costume, not a design. The failure mode you already got caught on once — the metaphor living in one hero element while the rest is generic.

So: everything on this page must belong inside the vault. Ask of each element — what is this, in a vault?

  • The XP balance — what is it? Bullion on a shelf? A figure on the door's counter? Whatever it is, it is not a number in a box.
  • Privileges — earned-not-bought tokens, "cut a block in half" and "make a block yours". In a vault these are objects in a deposit box, not chips. He was more specific about this than anything else: they should feel like things you hold.
  • Rewards — what he is saving toward, each with a condition he wrote himself. Locked, affordable and claimed must be three visibly different physical states. Not three colours of the same row.
  • Boosts — networking · golf · read tonight · finances hour. These add. Where does adding happen?
  • History — what he has already taken out. A vault has a record.
  • The claim — opening something. This is the emotional peak of the app and it must feel like it.

If an element cannot be answered in the vault's own terms, either reconceive it or say plainly in the hand-off that you could not, rather than dropping a generic card in and hoping.

"Not sloppy"

Craft, concretely — this is the standard the Time-Block agent was held to and met:

  • Every touch target ≥44px, thumb-reachable. He uses this one-handed.
  • One type scale. One spacing scale. No one-off values.
  • Nothing shifts between states — a reward going from locked to affordable must not reflow the page.
  • No text below 11px. This app has shipped 6.5px labels; do not.
  • No horizontal overflow at 390px. Verify it, do not assume it.
  • Every state rendered and labelled — see below.

Every state, rendered

  1. Day one · 0 XP, nothing earned, nothing claimable. Must feel like promise, not poverty. This is the hardest state and the one that decides whether he opens the tab in week one.
  2. Mid-run — some claimed, one nearly affordable, privileges held.
  3. Nothing affordable right now — must not read as a wall.
  4. Something just became affordable — this should be a moment.
  5. A privilege held and unspent.
  6. The claim ceremony, in full.
  7. After the claim — what the vault looks like when something has just left it.

The numbers must work

A strong weekday pays roughly 400–490 XP. Propose a real ladder — small / mid / big / run-defining — such that he can afford something meaningful in week one and is still reaching in November. Show your arithmetic. An economy that empties by mid-August is what round 1 shipped.
