close to each other. When I asked for 10 renditions I want 10 to be on the complete opposite sides

"Get outside of the box and make another 10 — not the first 10, but the best."

You delivered twelve and he could not tell them apart. The count was never the problem. Twelve answers to the same question is one answer.

The single survivor: the Now/Next pair. He likes it and is lukewarm. Carry that idea into exactly one of your ten and push it much further; do not build the other nine around it.

THE DIVERGENCE PROTOCOL — follow this literally

Step 1 — write down the three most obvious layouts for this region. A vertical stack of the six elements. The same stack with something collapsed. The same stack reordered. All three are banned, along with anything a person would describe with the same sentence.

Step 2 — each rendition must break a different rule of the current app. Not shuffle it — break it. Ten different rules, one each. Provocations, and reject at least half of these and find better:

  • What if there is no schedule on this screen at all? Only what is happening now.
  • What if it is horizontal — the day runs left to right and you swipe through it?
  • What if it is a clock face rather than a list?
  • What if the small wins are primary and the schedule is the secondary thing?
  • What if there are no numbers anywhere — only shape, fill and position?
  • What if it is one sentence? "3h 15m left. Block 2 now: ship the AVRS page."
  • What if the rail IS the interface and everything else lives inside it?
  • What if it is a receipt printing the day as it happens?
  • What if the past collapses to nothing and only the future has height?
  • What if it is a single full-bleed object with no cards, no borders, no panels?

Step 3 — no two renditions may share a primary layout primitive. Vertical list, horizontal strip, radial, grid, single-object, typographic-only, layered/stacked, timeline-spine, split-screen, card-deck — each used once across the whole set.

Step 4 — for each, write one line: "This is NOT a ___." If you cannot finish that sentence with something the other nine are, it is a duplicate. Replace it.

Step 5 — at least two must look like they belong to a different app entirely. If a stranger saw all ten they should not immediately guess they were made by one person for one product. That is the bar you missed.

What the region has to do (unchanged)

Everything between the locked graph card and the schedule list:

  1. The day rail — 06:00→21:50, a segment per block, a live now-line
  2. "3h 15m OF DEEP WORK LEFT" + the weekday
  3. The push strip — SANO 1/3 · FUNDED 0/3 · KITCHEN 0/1, and a red line when Anthony's pitch is stale
  4. The make-up banner — only when a penance is unpaid
  5. NEXT UP — current/next block, its objective, the focus-run button. Never below the fold.
  6. ALWAYS COUNTS — small-wins chips with weekly quota pips and a tally

A rendition may merge, hide, demote or radically reinterpret any of these — that is the point — but every element's job must still be doable somewhere on the screen or one tap away. Say which you moved and where.

Do not solve "always counts feels in the way" by hiding it. It is the only thing earnable on a bad day and it feeds five of the app's seven weekly quotas. Give it a better home, or make it the hero.

The constraint that is NOT the point

Round 1 opened with "the measurement that decides this" and optimised for pixel height. Height is a tiebreaker, not the brief. Report it, but do not let it flatten your thinking again — a taller layout that he loves beats a compact one he ignores. Interestingness first, height second.

Done when

  • One is the Now/Next idea pushed much further
  • ≥2 look like they came from a different app
  • Each has a "This is NOT a ___" line and a note on any element you relocated
  • Lint clean, no horizontal overflow at 390px, screenshotted at 390×844, and you have looked at them
  • Hand-off: rank them, and name the two you expect him to hate — if you can't, you haven't gone far enough
