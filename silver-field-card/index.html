Your play tomorrow

The one thing to understand

You never pay spot — you pay spot + premium. The premium is the entire game. At your size, a 4% difference between two shops is $200–$800. That is worth three phone calls.

  • You said you'd flip it if it spiked. That makes premium doubly important — every point you overpay is a point silver has to climb before you're even. Check the break-even tool below before you buy.
  • You said barter matters too. Junk silver serves both: it's currently the cheapest silver on the board and the most divisible. Lead with it.
  • Target mix: ~50% junk silver, ~35% 10 oz bars, ~15% Eagles or generic rounds. That covers cheap ounces, easy resale, and small-piece divisibility in one trip.
  • Buy about half your budget tomorrow. You said it depends how it goes — so make tomorrow a learning run. Find the shop that treated you straight, then go back with the rest.

Payment — you said undecided

Debit card. Same price as cash at almost every shop, no counting risk, no Form 8300 paperwork. Credit adds 3–4%, which at your size burns $200–$800 for nothing. If you do pay cash over $10,000 the dealer files IRS Form 8300 — totally routine, but never split payments to dodge it; that is the actual crime.

Junk silver — your edge

Ask for this first, at every shop

Refineries hit capacity in 2026 and stopped buying 90% coin, so dealers are stuck holding it for weeks. It has been trading at or below spot — nearly unheard of, it normally carries a 3–25% premium. If a shop sells near melt, it's the cheapest silver in the building.

Pre-1965 coinSilverMelt now

Don't get these mixed in

1965–70 half dollars are only 40% silver and war nickels are 35%. They're worth far less per coin. Say the words "90% only" when you ask, and check dates.

What to say

"Hi — what's your out-the-door price today on [90% junk silver per dollar face]? What spot are you using? And what would you pay me for it back today?"
  • That last question is the whole thing. It exposes the full spread in one sentence and tells the dealer you're not a mark. A shop that dodges it is a shop you don't buy from.
  • At $5k–20k, say your number out loud. "I'm looking to spend about $10,000 today — what can you do on that?" Dealers move at that size. They will not volunteer it.
  • Never accept a flat dollar price. Always convert: "what is that as a percent over spot?"
8

At the counter

  • "Do you have a Sigma verifier? Can you test it in front of me?" Good shops have one and will happily do it. A dealer who gets defensive about testing their own product is the single biggest red flag there is.
  • Magnet slide. Real silver isn't magnetic, but a strong magnet sliding down a real bar visibly slows. A steel-core fake sticks; a lead or tungsten core slides off with no drag at all.
  • Ping it. Real silver rings like a tuning fork for a second or two. A fake goes thud.
  • An Eagle is exactly 40.6mm wide, 2.98mm thick, 31.103g. Fakes almost always miss on thickness — nothing else has silver's density.
  • Prefer known names: Asahi, Sunshine, Geiger, PAMP, Royal Canadian Mint. Sealed mint tubes for rounds. Skip no-name bars with no serial.
  • Texas charges zero sales tax on gold and silver bullion — no minimum. If tax appears on the metal, stop the transaction. (Tubes, capsules and safes are taxed. So is palladium.)
  • Get an itemized receipt showing date, item, quantity, the spot price they used, premium, and total. Photograph it before you leave the lot. That's your cost basis — and the IRS taxes silver as a collectible at up to 28%, so you will want it.
9

Walk out if…

  • They won't tell you what spot price they're using
  • They won't quote a buyback number
  • They steer you from bullion to graded, proof or "rare" coins. This is the classic bait-and-switch. Markups run 50–300% and you will never get it back. At your budget you are exactly the target.
  • Sales tax appears on bullion in Texas
  • "This price is only good today"
  • Talk of dollar collapse, confiscation, or "untraceable" metal
  • They refuse to test a piece in front of you

Where it lives — you hadn't decided

  • Know the size first. $5k–20k is roughly 75–300 oz — 5 to 20 lbs of metal. It is heavier and bulkier than people expect. 300 oz of junk silver is a genuinely large, loud pile.
  • Rule one: don't tell people you own it. Not a joke. This is the whole security model.
  • Home safe — must be bolted down; an unbolted safe is a box with a handle. Then call your insurer: standard homeowner's policies cap precious metals around $200–$1,000. You need a scheduled rider, and it's cheap.
  • Bank safe deposit box — safest from theft and fire, but not FDIC insured, and you can't get at it on a weekend. Fine for a hold, annoying if you want to flip fast.
  • Texas Bullion Depository (Austin) — state-run and fully insured. Overkill at your size but worth knowing if you keep stacking.
