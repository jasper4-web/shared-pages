What to say

"Hi — what's your out-the-door price today on 90% junk silver, per dollar of face value? What spot are you using? And what would you pay me for it back today?"
  • That last question is the whole thing. It exposes the full spread in one sentence and tells them you're not a mark. A shop that dodges it is a shop you don't buy from.
  • Say your number out loud. "I'm looking to spend about $10,000 today — what can you do?" At your size dealers move. They will not volunteer it.
  • Never accept a flat dollar price. Always convert: "what is that as a percent over spot?"

How to pay

Debit card — you said undecided

Same price as cash at almost every shop, no counting risk, no paperwork. Credit adds 3–4%, which at your size burns $200–$800 for nothing. Cash of $10,000 or more means the dealer files IRS Form 8300 — routine, but never split payments to dodge it; that's the actual crime.

  • Texas charges zero sales tax on gold and silver bullion — no minimum. If tax shows up on the metal, stop the transaction. (Tubes, capsules and safes are taxed. So is palladium.)
  • Get an itemized receipt — date, item, quantity, the spot price they used, premium, total. Photograph it before you leave the lot.
  • The IRS taxes silver as a collectible — long-term gains up to 28%, not the usual 15–20%. That receipt is your cost basis. Keep it forever.

Is it real?

  • "Do you have a Sigma verifier? Can you test it in front of me?" Good shops have one and will happily do it. A dealer who gets defensive about testing their own product is the biggest red flag there is.
  • Magnet slide. Silver isn't magnetic, but a strong magnet sliding down a real bar visibly slows. A steel-core fake sticks; a lead or tungsten core slides off with no drag at all.
  • Ping it. Real silver rings like a tuning fork for a second or two. A fake goes thud.
  • An Eagle is exactly 40.6mm wide, 2.98mm thick, 31.103g. Fakes almost always miss on thickness — nothing else has silver's density.
  • Prefer known names: Asahi, Sunshine, Geiger, PAMP, Royal Canadian Mint. Sealed tubes for rounds. Skip no-name bars with no serial.

    Where it lives

    • Know the size first. $5k–20k is roughly 75–300 oz — 5 to 20 lbs of metal. Heavier and bulkier than people expect.
    • Rule one: don't tell people you own it. Not a joke — that's the whole security model.
    • Home safe — bolted down, or it's a box with a handle. Then call your insurer: standard homeowner's policies cap coins and bullion at $200, and few go past $2,500. You need a scheduled rider, and it's cheap.
    • Bank box — safest from theft and fire, but not FDIC insured, and you can't reach it on a weekend. Fine for a hold, annoying if you want to flip fast.
    • Texas Bullion Depository (Leander, just north of Austin) — state-run, guarded by Texas State Police. Overkill now, worth knowing if you keep stacking.
