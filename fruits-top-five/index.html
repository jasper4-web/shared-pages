The front strip — top five

You kept the riso, killed the white box on 05·B, and said the words on there did not make sense. All three are dealt with. These are not five textures of the same layout — the texture was never the decision. Every one of the five carries a rebuilt information architecture, and the whole 177.8 mm is finally doing work.

What was actually wrong — five things, all measured

1The brand was not on the front. The full-colour logo is only 5.0% wordmark by ink area. Placed at 26.8 mm, "FRUITS" set about 15.8 mm wide and 2.1 mm tall — the same height as the legal small print. The only legible FRUITS on the label was on the flanks, facing away from the shopper. Fixed by splitting the artwork into two assets, so the hand-drawn wordmark can be set at 42–46 mm independently of the tree.
2The copy was about the printing. "PRINTED IN THREE INKS", "OFF-REGISTER ON PURPOSE", "SOFT PRESS · SMALL BATCH", "PRESSED IN ONE PASS", "OVERPRINT EDITION". That is the design describing itself — and to a shopper, "off-register on purpose" reads as an apology for a misprint. All of it is gone.
3Two-thirds of the label carried nothing. 121 mm of the 177.8 mm wrap held one mirrored wordmark and one repeated three-word line, while the entire government warning, ingredient list, nutrition, manufacturer and batch codes were assigned to a 50.8 mm base sticker. That sticker cannot hold them — the warning alone measures 14 lines at the 6 pt legal floor, and a 2" circle offers about 21 mm of usable stacked height. The flanks now carry it.
4The foot was off-centre and off-safety. The required stack was centred at x 85.0 while the illustration was centred at x 90.17 — a 5.17 mm mismatch the eye reads as wrong without knowing why. And the bottom copy sat 2.15–2.4 mm off the cut, under the 3 mm that wrap-application drift needs. Both fixed; the footer is now one band, and everything clears 3 mm.
5Nothing said what it was or how strong it was. No flavour, and the dose appeared on three of the seven in three different wordings. Both are now on the face, every time, worded identically.

Where everything lives now

◀Left flank · x 1.4–61.9. Cannabinoids per package and per piece, serving size, the four nutrition values California asks for on an edible, ingredients, allergens, how to take it, who made it, PKG / LOT / UID. 13 lines, widest 55.4 mm in a 55.5 mm column, last descender 5.0 mm clear of the cut.
■Front · x 61.9–118.5. The 56.6 mm that faces the shopper. FRUITS, the tree, then the four things CA requires on a primary panel — CANNABIS-INFUSED (bold, above, larger than the identity), the product identity, net weight in both units, and the black universal symbol at 12.75 mm — plus the dose line, which is the number people actually shop on.
▶Right flank · x 118.5–178.9. The government warning, verbatim, bold caps. 14 lines, widest 54.6 mm, last descender 4.8 mm clear of the cut.
✕The seam. Nothing crosses x = 0 / 180.34, where the label meets itself — so the wrap is three panels, not one long picture. Turn the jar either way and you land on the half you were looking for.
This kills the base sticker — and that is a saving, not a loss. One fewer label to buy, one fewer to apply, and one fewer surface to re-print every time a COA moves the potency. The one thing that could bring it back: a long real ingredient list. The panel is built with a placeholder; a typical gummy list runs three lines, which still fits, but if the co-packer's formula runs long the contingency is the peel-back / expanded-content label already on the Sana question list (Q10).
Still open, and it is not ours to close: this artwork is a cartoon with a human figure. California prohibits cartoons on cannabis packaging, scrutinises "attractive to children" harder on edibles, and the DCC pre-approves nothing — the licensee carries the risk. All five ride on that call. It needs a distributor or a cannabis attorney, and it should start now rather than after we make production art.
One thing I need from you. The pack still has no flavour. Every serious edible on a California shelf sells by one — Wyld sells Raspberry, Camino sells Watermelon Lemonade — and a pack that only says "Fruit Chews" reads as a white-label sample. I have set "Mixed Fruit Chews" as a placeholder because your own logo argues for it: the bonsai carries a different fruit on every branch, so an assortment is the product the mark already promises. Say the word and I will change it — but it should be a decision, not a default.

T1

Sunrise

The halation you singled out, with the brand finally the size of a brand.

05·C was the one you called the blurry cool effect, and it is still the best print idea in the set — heavy ink blooming outward and lighter into uncoated stock, so the softness reads expensive instead of broken. What it never had was a brand. The hand-drawn wordmark now sets at 45 mm across the top of the face with the glow behind it, and the tree hangs beneath it the way it does in the original logo. This is 05·C with the actual problem fixed, and it is the safest of the five.

T2

Orchard Night

The only one that is not cream — and the only one that will win a shelf.

Four of these five are dark ink on pale stock, which is where the brand has lived all along and which is genuinely lovely up close. None of them will beat a Wyld or a Camino from six feet away. This one floods the whole wrap in one deep riso green, knocks every word out in cream, and stands the tree on a soft cream sun-disc so the illustration's black linework still reads. Measured: cream on this green is 9.6:1, so the 6 pt legal copy is more legible here than on any of the pale ones — and §17410 permits the universal symbol in white, so it stays compliant.

Split

05·B's plate shift — with the white box you did not like taken out.

T4

Daybreak

The screen does the hierarchy, so nothing needs a box at all.

05·D's gradient screen — dense at the top, bare by the bottom, which is how a riso actually makes a gradient — turns out to be the answer to the panel problem. The wordmark sits up in the dense ink where it has the most contrast, the tree comes through as the screen opens, and the legal band lands on stock that is already clean. Its first build failed: the ramp ran the full 180 mm and printed straight across the 6 pt legal copy on both flanks. The screen is now held to the visible face and faded out at every edge, so it never touches a word it should not.

T5

One Pass

The roller drag, aimed — the only one of the five that is not centred.

05·G's smear was a good effect bolted to a symmetrical layout, which wasted it. Here the drag has somewhere to go: the wordmark and the tree sit left of the face centre and the ink trails right, into the space the CA symbol occupies anyway, so the pull of the composition and the pull of the press are the same direction. It is the most editorial of the five and the least safe — an asymmetric front on a round jar rewards the shopper who picks it up and punishes you when it faces the shelf slightly wrong.
