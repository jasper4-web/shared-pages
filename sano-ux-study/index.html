Eight categories, twenty-four entrepreneurs, one site

A first-visit study of the live doorway site. One researcher per trade, three different owners each — different ages, budgets, tech comfort and existing software — every one of them driving the real pages in a real browser. Plus my own technical audit of all 21 pages, run independently so the personas' claims could be checked rather than believed.

How to read this. The 101 findings below are leads — a persona's reaction is evidence of how the page lands, not proof of a defect. Everything in the “fixed” section I reproduced myself first, in a browser, against the live site; everything in the “needs you” section I verified is real but deliberately did not change, because it needs a fact or a decision that is yours. Two things the personas reported turned out not to be defects on inspection: the demo form's mailto hand-off is a disclosed design-test limitation with a working copy-fallback, and the app mock remembering which conversation you had open is ordinary app behaviour.

Fixed, deployed and re-testedEach one reproduced, fixed, and re-verified against the live site. Full regression after every change: 8 trade pages × 5 widths + the prototype, plus a 21-page audit and a WCAG contrast sweep.

1

The level you pick never reached the demo form

bugCritical to conversion3 categories (plumbing, retail, auto), 3/3 personas each

What was wrong. Every trade-page CTA linked to demo.html?ind=<trade> with no plan, while demo.html already reads ?plan= and the pricing page already passed it. The whole middle of the page gets an owner to choose a level, then threw that choice away — and the emailed lead said “Plan of interest: (not specified)”.

The fix. shared.js now carries the checked tier onto every demo link the same way it carries the trade, and re-writes them when the visitor changes their mind.

Re-tested. Verified live on 4 trades: default ?ind=hvac&plan=growth, switching to Scale rewrites to plan=scale, switching back to Starter rewrites again, and demo.html receives {plan:'total', ind:'hvac'} end-to-end.

2

“The honest all-in” was frozen on Growth's numbers

bugCritical — it showed the wrong price2 categories; the data file itself warned about it

What was wrong. The price line under the tier switch always read “$999/mo … a one-time $2,497 to build it”, whatever level you picked. A visitor on Starter saw $2,497 when theirs is $997; on Total, $2,497 instead of $14,997 — 6× low. SANO_TIERS carries the real numbers and its own comment warns “they are NOT uniform across levels, and a page that claims they are is quotable against us.”

The fix. The line is now one span per level, driven by the same :has() switch as everything else — the pattern the 2.0 prototype already used. Without :has() all four show rather than the price vanishing.

Re-tested. Verified live on 3 trades × 4 levels: exactly one line visible, carrying that level's real price, setup fee and minimum term (Starter $397/$997, Growth $999/$2,497, Scale $1,995/$4,997, Total $4,997/$14,997/six-month).

3

The flagship plumbing demo booked an active leak for tomorrow

copyHighPlumbing

What was wrong. The page promises “Emergency routing so urgent leaks reach you fast” and “Sorts the emergency from the ‘sometime next week’”. Its most-tapped proof — the missed-call replay — showed a customer whose water heater was “leaking all over the garage floor” being booked for tomorrow, with the owner never alerted. The single most persuasive object on the page demonstrated the opposite of the page's own promise.

The fix. The thread now flags it as an emergency, sends it to the on-call plumber instead of the queue, and books it today at 11:30 — which is what the calendar screen already showed.

Re-tested. Verified live: the booking line reads “Booked ✓ Today 11:30 AM”, the thread contains both “emergency” and “alerted”, and the calendar entry (“11:30 AM · emergency”) now agrees with it.

4

The booking promise contradicted the practice-software answer

copyHighDental raised it; the claim was sitewide

What was wrong. The Booking sheet (badged Starter, so shown on every plan and every trade) said it “writes straight into the calendar you already use”. The dental FAQ on the same page says that for Dentrix, Eaglesoft or Open Dental it “hands your team the booking to drop in rather than writing into your chart directly”. Only the flattering version was visible without opening a drawer. what-we-run.html carried the same over-claim (“Books straight onto your calendar”).

The fix. Both now say it books into the calendar we keep in sync with yours, and that where your own software won't accept an outside booking your team gets it to drop in — with “we confirm exactly how it connects before launch”, matching the FAQ.

Re-tested. Verified live: the over-claim is gone from the sheet and from what-we-run, and the sheet now contains the “drop in” language the FAQ uses.

5

Escape did not close a capability sheet

ux / accessibilityMediumFound by my own audit, not the personas

What was wrong. The 16 capability sheets are full-screen overlays with a scrim — functionally modal. The mobile menu closes on Escape, restores focus and marks the background inert; the sheets ignored Escape entirely, so a keyboard user had to find the close control. Two modal patterns, two behaviours.

The fix. shared.js now closes an open sheet on Escape and puts focus back on the tile that opened it. The sheet stays pure CSS — with JS off, the scrim and close button work exactly as before.

Re-tested. Verified live: sheet open → Escape → no sheet visible, co-none checked, focus back on co-textback; the scrim still closes it independently.

6

A keyboard stop that showed nothing

ux / accessibilityLowFound by my own audit

What was wrong. #co-none (the “nothing open” member of the sheet radio group) is 1×1 and transparent. Its focus ring is drawn on the capability grid — but with the drawer shut that grid is display:none, so tabbing landed on an element with no visible indication anywhere on screen.

The fix. It now lives inside the grid, so it is only reachable when there is something to show. No JavaScript involved.

Re-tested. Verified live: with the drawer shut the tab order runs skip → brand → nav CTA → burger → back-link → tier tabs → drawer → app, with no invisible stop; opening the drawer makes it reachable again.

7

“Dr. Ana Ruiz” was greeted as “Thanks, Dr.”

bugMediumDental — and it hits every titled name

What was wrong. The confirmation screen — the last thing a buyer sees — took the first whitespace-delimited token as the first name. Two of the eight categories are practices where nearly every buyer types a title. “ana ruiz” also came back uncapitalised as “Thanks, ana”.

The fix. Leading titles (Dr/Mr/Mrs/Ms/Miss/Mx/Prof/Rev/Sr/Sra/Srta, with or without the period) are dropped, and the first letter is capitalised.

Re-tested. Verified live across five inputs: “Dr. Ana Ruiz”, “Dr Ana Ruiz”, “ana ruiz”, “Ana” and “Mrs. Bo Hendricks” all now produce the correct greeting.

8

The phone mock promised a screen per card

uxMedium3 categories (retail, roofing, HVAC)

What was wrong. The banner said “Tap any card to jump to it”, but the mock has five screens and eight tap targets — “Quotes Open 6”, “Opportunities” and “Invoices” all land on the Contacts list, which shows no quotes, pipeline or invoices. For quote-driven trades that is the most persuasive screen on the page, and it doesn't exist.

The fix. The banner no longer promises a destination per card (“Tap a card or a tab to look around”), so the mock stops writing a cheque it can't cash.

Re-tested. Verified live on retail. Note: this is the honest minimum. The better answer — building an actual Quotes screen — is a content decision I've left for you; it was independently named as the most persuasive missing screen by the retail agent.

Verified, but yours to decideReal, reproduced, and left alone — these need a business fact, a legal answer, or a design trade-off I shouldn't make on your behalf. Ordered by how many of the eight categories raised them independently.

Nobody can hear the voice

5/8 categories

The flagship $999/mo feature is live voice answering and there are zero seconds of audio anywhere on the site. Five categories reached the bottom of the page still asking what it sounds like; for the 58-year-old HVAC owner it was the outright blocker: “I cannot hear the assistant anywhere on this site.”

What it needs from you. A ~15-second recording of the assistant answering a call. This is already on your owner-blocked list and it is now the single most-cited gap in the study.

No named integrations anywhere

8/8 categories

The word “integration” does not appear on any page. ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, QuickBooks, Shopify, Square, Clover — none appear. Every category's “I already have software” persona stalled here; the HVAC one put it exactly: “I can't tell if I'm buying a front end or a second system to keep in sync.”

What it needs from you. A business-truth answer from you: what you connect to today, in which direction data flows, and what stays manual. I can't invent this, and the booking fix above only removes the false half of it.

“We build yours” is invisible where the choice is made

8/8 categories

All eight doorway tiles look identical — nothing says three trades are pre-built and five are not. The badge appears for the first time in the trade-page hero with no explanation, and isn't resolved until the FAQ six screens down. It reads either as “built around you” (good) or “doesn't exist yet” (alarming).

What it needs from you. Chips on the doorway tiles would fix it — but the doorway has a hard, hard-won invariant: all eight tiles must fit one screen down to 320×568. Adding a line of text to each tile puts that at risk, so I've left it for your call rather than quietly trading away an invariant you fought for.

No build timeline for the five “we build yours” trades

7/8 categories

Pricing gives concrete targets (14 / 30 / 45 / 60–75 days), but those are the pre-built path. Five trades are told “mapped with you in week one, then built” and never given a number. The retail FAQ even concedes a longer build without saying how much longer.

What it needs from you. One sentence with a real number — e.g. “add roughly two weeks to the targets on the pricing page, and you get a date in writing at kickoff”. It needs your number, not mine.

The only sample deliverable is the HVAC blueprint

8/8 categories

Every trade — including “ready now” roofing — is sent to “read a fully-built one”, and lands on cooling emergencies, 85°F rules and spring tune-up campaigns. The blueprint is the single most convincing asset on the site (multiple personas said so) and seven of eight categories get someone else's.

Nothing tells an owner with staff how their people fit

5/8 categories

“Office manager”, “receptionist”, “seat”, “team member” and “user” appear zero times sitewide, while the app mock shows a “Team Inbox”. Every persona who already employs a front desk asked whether this replaces her, and the site's own framing (“A whole department, without a single hire”) reads as replacement.

The hiring anchor doesn't fit the small end

5/8 categories

Pricing opens with $12,000–$22,000/mo of staff. Credible for a multi-truck trades company; not for a boutique, a solo dentist or a one-van operator — several read it as evidence the price was arbitrary.

Trade-generic copy leaks into the specialist pages

6/8 categories

“Money sitting in your truck” on the Med Spa page; “What's going on with your system?” for dental; water-heater and invoice examples on the What-we-run page a shop owner clicks first. The clinical and retail categories read it as “this was built for tradesmen and relabelled”.

The “already run on this exact system” claim still reads as a contradiction

1/8 categories

The auto agent independently flagged it against the Why-SANO page — after this morning's reconciliation was already live. You've decided to keep this claim; I'm reporting only that a fresh reader still trips on it, so the about-page wording didn't fully neutralise it.

Dental: the insurance reply promises benefits verification

1/8 categories

The demo thread says “We will verify your plan before you come in and tell you your portion up front — no surprise bill after.” That is a substantive operational promise (someone must actually verify benefits) and it sits oddly beside the published boundary that the assistant won't quote prices. Same class as the booking claim I fixed — but I don't know your real answer, so I haven't touched it.

Med spa: no PHI carve-out in the liability cap, no subprocessors named

1/8 categories

Terms cap all liability at three months of fees with no carve-out for a PHI incident, and the privacy page names no subprocessors and no retention period. The compliance-scarred med-spa persona stopped there. This is a lawyer question, not a copy question.

The per-minute overage rate is published nowhere

1/8 categories

Every other number on the pricing page is concrete; overage is “the rate on your agreement”. For the storm-chasing roofer whose volume spikes 10× for two weeks, that is the number that decides it.

No Spanish site behind the Spanish link

1/8 categories

“Se habla Español” appears in the nav, the mobile menu and the footer, and every one lands on an English page about bilingual service. A Spanish-first owner reasonably expects a Spanish site.

The hero has no call to action

1/8 categories

On a trade page the first in-page “Request my free demo” sits at 88% scroll depth; the hero has none. (The sticky nav CTA is present throughout, which softens it.) Worth a decision, but the hero is something you signed off recently, so I haven't changed it unilaterally.

What the site got rightWorth recording, because it is most of the page.

Every one of the 24 personas understood the offer — that this is a done-for-you department, not software — usually within a minute, and most named the same line as the reason: “They give you a login. We give you your time back.” The honesty posture (“We're early, and we'd rather say so”, the research block naming its own sources' conflicts of interest, the money labelled “made-up numbers on a made-up account” before the numbers) was cited as a trust-builder in all eight categories. The sample blueprint was repeatedly called the most convincing thing on the site. Technically the build is clean: 21 pages, zero console errors, zero failed requests, no broken links, no horizontal overflow from 320px to 1440px, no WCAG AA contrast failures on any page tested, and first contentful paint between 144ms and 280ms.
